# The question this page answers

Sooner or later someone asks the reasonable question: *who certifies a Formation Companion?* A parent asks it one way — "who trained you?" A school asks it another way — "what's the credential?" A church in another town asks it a third way — "can we borrow one of yours?"

The answer is worth saying carefully, because we refuse it on purpose: **no one licenses a Formation Companion, and no one ever will from here.** There is no registry, no certificate, no title that travels, no fee for standing. This page says what we do instead, and why.

# What we are

Three things, and only three.

**A published curriculum.** The books, the handbooks, the lesson plans, the cards — open to anyone, free to read and free to run. We received this without paying; we hand it on the same way (Matt. 10:8).

**A formation pattern.** Companions are not trained; they are *formed* — by receiving every practice before leading it, by second-runnings in front of the room, by the Labs, by a covering that watches. The pattern is written down so that any local body can run it on its own people. That is not a limitation of the pattern; it is the pattern (2 Tim. 2:2 — entrusted to the faithful, who entrust it again).

**A steward of the texts.** Over time, a small council keeps the written pattern honest — versions, corrections, integrity of the canon. It stewards *pages*. It will never steward *persons*.

# What we are not

We are not a licensing body, a credentialing organization, or a certifying institute — not now, and not when the work grows. This is a refusal, not an oversight, and it stands on three legs:

- **Trust that is real lives close to the ground.** A credential is a promise made to strangers by an office far away. But formation is witnessed up close or not at all. Any structure distant enough to certify people it cannot see is distant enough to be wrong about them — and too distant to weep with them. Authority in this work stays where the weeping can happen.
- **A certificate bends the formation.** The moment a certificate exists, people begin forming *toward the certificate* — performing for the gatekeeper instead of becoming the person. The desire question, the vital signs, the honest dry season: all of it corrupts when standing depends on displaying it well.
- **Hands are laid on locally in Scripture.** Timothy was told not to be hasty in laying on hands (1 Tim. 5:22) — and the hands are always *someone's*, someone near enough to know the life. The character tests for leaders (1 Tim. 3, Titus 1) are written to be applied by people who can actually observe: household, reputation, years. There is no New Testament office that certifies workers it has never eaten with.

# The unit of trust is the covering

A Formation Companion's standing is exactly this: **a named covering, close enough to see their life, says so — and keeps saying so only as long as they can see it.** The commissioning at the end of a series (the Week 22 rite, and its like) is that covering's public word about a person they watched form. It is a *sending*, not a certificate. It means everything inside the household that witnessed it. It claims nothing anywhere else.

The same nearness that grants standing can withdraw it. The covering that laid hands on a Companion can take them off — quietly, locally, restoratively where possible. No distant board is needed for this, and none could do it as well.

# If your body has no Companion

Then build your own, from your own people — that is what the written pattern is *for*. Take your steadiest people; have them receive every practice before they lead one; run the second-runnings and the Labs; put them under your own covering; commission them yourselves when your own eyes are satisfied. A body that forms its own Companions from its own people, under its own covering, has something no imported expert can give it: leaders whose lives the room already knows.

Do not wait for someone certified to arrive. No such person exists, and the waiting builds the wrong thing.

# Receiving a Companion from outside

Sometimes a formed Companion arrives from elsewhere — moving to your town, offering to help you start. (I am that person to every body except my own.) Receive them the way the early church received Apollos: he was already able, and still Priscilla and Aquila drew him close and filled in what was lacking — and when he moved on, *the brothers wrote a letter* so the next community could welcome him (Acts 18:24–28). A letter from their covering is the right way for standing to begin traveling. It is never a substitute for what must happen next:

**Know them before you trust them.** Not their materials — their life. Paul's credential to the Thessalonians was not a document; it was *you know what kind of men we proved to be among you* (1 Thess. 1:5, 2:8). So take the time. Ask, and check:

- *Who is your covering, and when did you last sit with them?* (A Companion without a live covering is not ready, whatever their history.)
- *Where have you led with someone watching — and what did you first receive as a participant, before you led it?* (In this pattern no one leads a practice they have not first received in the circle themselves, and first leads happen with a seasoned Companion in the room. Only-ever-led — or only-ever-led-alone — is its own answer.)
- *May we speak with your covering ourselves?*
- Then: table, time, and the ordinary tests of a life seen up close — before any room is entrusted to them.

If the answers are thin, the answer is not-yet, said with warmth. And an outside Companion worth trusting will be the first to insist on all of this — the ones who resist being known are answering the question for you.

# About the levels

The handbooks speak of Companion I, II, and III. These are **safety floors, not ranks** — they exist so a junior circle is never held by someone unready, not so anyone can climb. No one issues these levels. The local team assigns them to itself, honestly, using the level definitions in Volume 5 as a mirror, with its covering in the conversation. If the mirror is used to flatter, the floors fail; used honestly, they are enough.

# The sentence

When a parent asks a Companion, "who trained you?", the answer this whole page exists to make true is one sentence:

> *"I was formed in this pattern here at [name of household], under [name of covering] — and you're welcome to go talk with them."*

No certificate improves on that sentence. Most certificates exist because that sentence has become impossible.
