# For Readers of the Article

*The living record behind "If a Teenager Can Use It…: A Design Framework for a Curriculum in Hearing and Obeying God"*

The article sets out the design of Fellowship of the Heart: its principles, its method, and the decisions that shape it, each with its reasons. The curriculum itself keeps changing, because it is built by listening to the room. So the article describes the design as it stood on one date, and this page keeps the record from there.

Everything here is at the level of the room. No page names a participant, and nothing describes any one person. What is watched is the health of the work, never the worthiness of the people ([the Measurement Covenant](../shared/measurement-covenant.md)).

## How the article was checked

This is ministry practice, not university research, so no review board stands over it, and that makes the obligations weigh more on the leaders, not less. The parents signed consent before the evening form began, are in the room the whole time, and have consented to this work being written about; each teenager has given their own assent. Oversight of the evenings rests with an elder of the covering church, who is also a parent in the room, and he can pause the work. A draft of the article goes to the parents in the room for their correction before it is submitted.

The article also passed through many drafts of its own. Two kinds of material came out of it along the way and now live here: the dated record (the history, the counts, the decisions, what has been seen), which belongs on a page that can change; and the working procedures (every question asked, how the answers are handled, the safety protocols), which belong in the handbooks the leaders actually use.

## Using the materials

The materials are published under [Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Adapted versions are shared on the same terms, so that what others learn flows back into the common pool. The license covers the materials only; nothing a participant says, writes or journals is part of them.

The article's advice for anyone who tries: keep what is clearly taught, adapt what is inferred to your setting, test what is speculative, and share back what you changed and why. And walk it yourself before you lead others into it.
