# How Fellowship of the Heart Got Here, 2024–2026

*From one full day to twenty-one evenings. A history of adaptation: the core held while the room, the rhythm and the length changed.*

The longer history, from 2002, is in *Intentional Journey of the Heart*: [The Four Connects, a Dated History](https://jgtittle-ministries.github.io/Intentional-Journey-of-the-Heart/reader.html#docs%2Fvolume-2-knowing-to-doing%2Fexploration-05-appendix-four-connects-dated-history.md). This page tells the part that belongs to *Fellowship of the Heart*.

---

## One full day

*Fellowship of the Heart* began in 2024 with a single full day. All the adults walked [the Four Connects](four-connects.md) end to end in one sitting. The intent was simple: put the approach of *Intentional Journey of the Heart*, Volume 2, into practice, adults first, and see what happened.

That day turned out to be three things at once, and only the first was planned.

- **The foundation.** Every process the group used afterward was built on the ground laid that day.
- **The compass.** For two years, whenever the group asked what to look at next, the answer came the same way: connect the candidate process to some part of hearing and obeying God, using the Four Connects as the map. Side trails were tested, and some were abandoned. The compass told us which was which.
- **The energy.** What the adults tasted that day carried the group through its choices and its dead ends. We did not have a theory for that at the time. We have one now: people persist in what they have already tasted the value of.

## The trail we abandoned

Some of the deeper heart work the adults explored dealt with intense emotional knots. It was good work, even needed work. But as the work turned toward including the teenagers, we saw two things. That depth was too much for teenagers at this point in their lives. And it was probably not right for a teenager to watch their own parent go through it.

Parents going first is the engine of this design. But the engine has a depth limit. So we drew a line and held it: we stay away from therapy-type activity. Several of our members had a significant background in that discipline, which is why the line was credible. The restraint was informed, not naive. The phrase has been in our vocabulary ever since: *this may be therapeutic, but it is not therapy.*

## The prototype

The prototype was four families with nine teenagers among them, and it ran about two years. The design was adults first. The parents did the process work together, men and women in one group, and got it clear and working. Around the six-month mark the teenagers were invited in, so they could see in their own parents how this work changes a life.

Along the way the group wore smooth the working vocabulary the curriculum still runs on: the container, the clearing, being present, being intentional, and the blockages to hearing and obeying God, with what to do about them.

The group also shrank. Four families became three, then two, and for the last six months one. We report that as data. Some of it was the ordinary pressure of busy family life. Some of it was the work itself, which asks things not every season of life can give. As the room got smaller, the focus turned steadily toward the teenagers: how to train them in a way that fit their point in life, and that they could carry forward when they left the table. The prototype began as an adults-first experiment and ended as an apprenticeship.

Two more lessons became design convictions. Real families miss weeks, so each session was built to be whole for whoever came, while the practices build for those who stay. And the order of the Four Connects held: self and others first, then God, then mission.

## Three designs

Out of the prototype came a documented design in three series: *Getting Started*, *Going Deeper* and *Going Out*, thirty-four weeks written for adults. That sequence remains as [Adult FotH](adult/index.md).

Then a K–12 Christian school agreed to host the work as an after-school club, and its year had twenty-two club weeks. Fitting the work to twenty-two weeks forced one question: what would it take to train up teenagers? The answer became a design principle: *if a teenager can grab a practice and use it, adults can use it too.* Adult material does not simplify down. Teen-usable material scales up. That school-year design is [the CCA Pilot](cca/index.md).

## Zero, treated as data

Offered as a fall after-school club in 2026, the twenty-two-week series drew no enrollments. We treated the zero as data and went and asked. Interviews with the two interested families in early September found that the barriers were child care for younger siblings and the design's rule that a parent must attend. They were not the curriculum, and not the clock. Two interviews are a small window, and what they establish is modest: where the barriers were, not whether the curriculum forms anyone.

## The evening form

The re-scope that followed took the work into a home. [FotH Evening](evening/index.md) meets every two weeks, dinner first. The twenty-two sessions were re-clocked to ninety minutes, and where two sessions worked better as one, they were combined, so the series ran to seventeen evenings. In November four evenings were added: Heart Bible Study, the group form of the scripture practice; Doing the Word, an evening for the obey step; Serving Together, where the room serves someone side by side; and Carrying the Word, Scripture learned by heart as families. The series now runs to twenty-one. Two teenagers lead each evening, one holding the room's container and one carrying the content, prepared in a planning lab between meetings. The first evening was 15 September 2026.

The two-week rhythm turned out to do something the school design had planned only for its breaks. Each gap is two weeks in which nobody gathers the room, and what a family brings back is the reading. The evening form runs that test at every return.

A second form, a semester elective at the same school, has been proposed. It would remove both barriers the interviews found.

## Where it stands

The evenings are running, and every change to them is written down with its date and its reason in the [Evening change log](evening/CHANGELOG.md). What the evenings teach flows back into the CCA Pilot and Adult FotH. The account of what this first evening cycle is teaching is being written as an action research report.

The apprenticeship the prototype ended in has become the way the work is carried: by teenagers who lead.
