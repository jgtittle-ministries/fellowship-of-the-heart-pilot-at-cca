## WEEK 20 — THE LONG WALK (75 minutes)

**Aim.** Consolidate the year at the threshold of the sending. Three movements: the Companions-in-Formation teach the Signs Card and the Path Home Card to the whole room — their final rep before the Week 22 commissioning; the room walks back through its own year, journal in hand; and one-sentence testimonies are gathered, with real consent, for the two commissioning evenings. The Post-Series Survey goes home at the close.

**Scripture anchor.** Isaiah 50:10 — "Let him who walks in darkness and has no light trust in the name of the LORD and rely on his God." Supporting: Psalm 23:4 (the valley walked *through*, not around) and Luke 15:20 (the father who runs).

**Connect focus.** God, primary — the God who is still there when the feelings are not. Self in the journal review; Others in the clusters and the cards; Mission ahead, in what the testimonies will do for the rooms that hear them.

**Mode.** WHOLE ROOM for the two teaching segments — the Companions-in-Formation teach standing, with a named adult catch seated where each can find their eyes. Then quiet individual work (the journal arc review), then FAMILY CLUSTERS — 2–3 whole families with a Cluster Companion, own family always together — for the one-sentence shares and the testimony gathering. Whole room for the survey, the Leader Feedback Round, and closing. If total attendance is about ten or fewer, skip the clustering — the whole room stays as one circle throughout.

**Center.** The teaching itself. The Signs Card (the four kinds of dry, and the one question that sorts them) and the Path Home Card (the written way back — and how a room receives someone coming back). Taught by the cohort's own senior teens, so the room hears the year's weightiest pastoral equipment in the voice of its next generation of leaders.

**Who leads.** The Companions-in-Formation carry both teaching segments from their scripted spines — rehearsed last week in clusters (Week 19), tonight delivered to the full room. Two different seniors if numbers allow, one per card; an adult catch is named for each segment. The Lead Companion frames the night, holds the journal review, and closes. The Cluster Companions hold the testimony gathering.

**Between-session practice.** Complete the Post-Series Survey (H20.1) — back in the bin at the Week 21 door. And one small errand that is really a teaching: find both mercy cards at home — the Signs Card and the Path Home Card — and show one other family member where they live.

**IJH source.** *A Church Prepared for Revival*, Movement III ("When the Ground Goes Dry" — the full teaching behind the Signs Card); the shared Signs Card and Path Home Card (docs/shared/); Vol 2 (the container; the long-walk frame); Handbook Sections 8 (the survey) and 11 (the Companion-in-Formation track).

## WATCH FOR (Week 20 specific risks)

- The person for whom the dry-season teaching is not theoretical — tonight. Expect at least one; a face will say so. This is the session working, not failing: the card exists so that this exact person hears "not a verdict" while the room is still warm. The teaching normalizes without exposing — nobody is looked at, nobody is asked. The adult Companion who saw the face follows up offline within 48 hours.
- A Companion-in-Formation freezing in front of the full room. Week 19's rehearsal was in clusters; tonight is the whole room, families and all, and the jump is real. The freeze-recovery grace protocol is decided in advance (see the teaching blocks): the adult catch steps in with warmth, picks up the spine at the next numbered point, and hands it back the moment the senior reaches for it. A stumble is not a fail — the gate is fluency across the year, not perfection tonight — and the feedback round still honors the rep.
- Testimony-gathering pressure. The consent is real or the gathering is worthless. A Cluster Companion who wants a full set of cards more than a true set of cards will lean, without meaning to. Say it in the team room beforehand: an empty card is a complete answer, and a teen who declines leaves zero footprint — no list of decliners, no follow-up ask, nothing.
- The room treating the four kinds of dry as a quiz — or as a sorting tool for other people. Rule 1 of the card, taught plainly: it is for your own heart, and for one trusted person helping you read it. Nobody gets filed under a column — not a spouse, not a teen, not a friend.
- Parent recognition landing mid-teaching — a parent realizing a gray stretch of years finally has a name and was never a verdict. Receive it; do not process it in the room. The 1:1 follow-up offer stands for parents exactly as it does for teens.
- The journal that is not in the room. Someone will have forgotten theirs, or lost it in March. Memory serves: the three prompts on the board work from recollection too. No fuss, no spotlight — hand them paper and let them walk the year from memory.
- The night going sentimental instead of solid. Tonight has real emotional weight — a year reviewed, testimonies gathered, a sending ahead. Let the weight land, but keep the teaching the center. The room should leave equipped, not just moved.

## CRISIS CONTINGENCIES (Week 20)

*Week 20 is moderate-risk in a specific, predictable direction: the dry-season teaching describes somebody's current life, and tonight they find that out in a room full of people. The protocols below are rehearsed at team pre-work. Handbook Section 6 remains the floor for anything heavy.*

**If a participant says, in the room or after, "I think my dryness is the bad kind."** Do not diagnose. Ask the card's own question, gently: "When you're honest — do you still miss Him? Does the distance ache?" Receive whatever comes without a verdict. Then schedule the follow-up: "Let's sit down this week and read the card together slowly. That's exactly what it's for." The sorting happens in an unhurried conversation with one trusted adult — never in the room, never on the spot.

**If the teaching visibly lands on someone mid-block.** The handoff is scripted and rehearsed: the teaching senior finishes their point — cleanly, no pause, no glance — and an adult Companion moves quietly to sit beside the person. The teaching continues. The adult stays through the close and opens the follow-up conversation before the family leaves. The senior never holds the person; rule 2 is the bright line, tonight as always. A disclosure passes to the adult in the room immediately, and handing it over well is honored as leadership.

**If anything surfaces that touches self-harm, abuse, or anyone's safety.** Full Section 6 protocol. Two-adult rule. The Signs Card's own rule 4 applies out loud if it is needed: if the gray has swallowed everything — sleep, appetite, humor — that is a treatable illness, not a spiritual verdict, and getting help is obedience. Licensed care and faithful companionship cooperate; they do not compete.

**If a practice or the review overwhelms someone.** Run the Settle Protocol (Handbook §6): stop with plain directive words, bring them back to the room, breathe and anchor, and nobody leaves activated — a Companion stays until their body has settled, and settling that doesn't come is a same-evening call to the backup.

# **Session at a Glance**

## **Why this session, this week**

Every walk that lasts includes seasons when the practices go dry and God feels absent. That is not failure, and it is usually not sin — it is a documented season of the long walk, walked by the psalmists, by Spurgeon, by Teresa of Calcutta, by a great cloud of the dry-but-faithful. The most dangerous thing about such a season is not the dryness; it is the wrong diagnosis — the voice that says dryness means fake faith, or a God who looked at you and left. Whole journeys have ended over a misread dry season. Tonight exists so that never happens to anyone in this fellowship.

Why tonight, and not back in the fall? Because the question is live *now*. The year is about to release this room into a summer without the weekly container — no Wednesday, no circle, no faces. Some rhythm will slip; some week will go gray; that is not a prediction of failure, it is a description of every long walk ever taken. Teaching the dry season at the threshold of the gap — while the room is still warm, and before anyone is in the dark trying to improvise — is the design. The Signs Card must be learned before the dry season, because two of its four cases command opposite motions. The Path Home Card must be filed while everyone is in the room to hear what it is for, because the person who eventually needs it will, by definition, not be sitting in a circle like this one when they do.

And there is a second design decision in the room tonight: *who* teaches. The Companions-in-Formation carry both cards — their final rep before the Week 22 commissioning, rehearsed last week in clusters, delivered tonight to the whole room. This is deliberate twice over. For the seniors, it is the last and largest rep of their formation year: carrying the most important pastoral equipment an FC1 will ever hold, in front of everyone who loves them, with the adults in reach. And for the room, it is the teaching made flesh — the cohort hears "a dry season is not a verdict" in the voice of a seventeen-year-old they have watched grow all year, which lands in a register no adult voice can reach.

The rest of the night consolidates. The journal arc review walks each person back through their own year — not the curriculum's year, theirs. And the testimony gathering harvests, with real consent, the one-sentence fruit that the two commissioning evenings will read aloud. Week 21 sends the families; Week 22 commissions the Companions; tonight gathers what those two nights will hold up to the light.

## **The teaching's heart**

For the team, plainly, so every Companion holds the same center: the founder's deepest pastoral concern in this whole body of work is the dry or desolate believer — the one who is still showing up, still wanting God, and privately concluding that the grayness means they have been disqualified. Tonight the room learns to recognize a dry season without panic, to sort the four kinds of dry by the one question that matters (*which way does the desire point?*), and to know when dryness is actually something else — the Section 6 floor holds for anything heavy, and rule 4 of the card says when to call the doctor.

And the Path Home Card teaches two things at once, and the second is just as important as the first: the way back, and how to *receive* someone coming back. No interrogation. No accounting. The father ran while the son was still a long way off (Luke 15:20). A room that has been taught the father's posture before anyone needs it is a room a wanderer can come home to.

The cards are taught so they can gather dust in a known drawer. The hope is that they are rarely needed. The point is that everyone knows where they are.

## **Block 3 — The Signs Card, Taught by a Companion-in-Formation (4:28–4:44, 16 min)**

The first teaching segment. The senior stands, Signs Card in hand as a net; the adult catch is seated in their sight line. Whiteboard ready. The room takes the Signs Card out of their folders at the senior's word.

### **The scripted spine (verbatim — the senior works from these six points)**

*The spine is short on purpose. The senior says these lines as written — they are load-bearing — and travels between them in their own words. Rehearsed twice this week; delivered without reading.*

**1. Why the card exists.** *"Take out the Signs Card. Here is why this card exists. Sooner or later, everyone who works the Rhythm Card hits a season where the practices go gray. Prayer feels like talking to the ceiling. Scripture reads like cardboard. And when that season comes, the most dangerous thing in the room is not the dryness — it is the wrong diagnosis. Whole journeys have ended over a misread dry season. This card exists so that never happens to anyone in this fellowship."*

**2. The tide.** *"The card says dryness is usually the tide, and only sometimes the thorn. The tide goes out and comes back — that is how seas work, and how souls work. This card is for telling the tide from the things that are not the tide."*

**3. The sorting question.** *(Write it large on the board:)* **Which way does the desire point?** *"One question sorts everything. Not 'how do you feel' — feelings are exactly what have gone quiet. When you are honest: do you still want God? Do you miss Him — does the distance ache? Or has the wanting itself faded, and the world tastes fine without Him?"*

**4. The four columns.** *(Walk the card's four columns, about a minute each — in your own words, hitting each column's tell and first step:)* One: **neglect** — the desire has faded and the distance doesn't ache; the practices lapsed and you don't grieve them. First step: wake and turn; rebuild the rhythm. Two: **worn out** — the desire is buried under duty; you want escape, not God; the whisper says "I'd be better off elsewhere." First step: rest; a lighter yoke; stay put; smaller faithful steps. Three: **the enemy's dry wind** — you feel accused and pressured, and there is a voice counseling change: quit, undo, leave. First step: resist — change nothing, pray a little more, tell someone. Four: **God's own stripping** — the desire is intact and starving; the ache is the sign; you keep showing up and it feels like nothing, and you are worried about failing God. Lukewarm hearts never worry about that. First step: receive — wait, stay faithful, stay accompanied.

**5. Three and four.** *"Now look at three and four, because this is why we learn this card tonight and not in the middle of a dry season: they command opposite motions. One says fight. The other says yield. Getting them backwards is the costliest mistake in the dry country. And when you cannot tell which one you are in — the card's own rule — you do not decide alone. That is what Companions are for."*

**6. The four rules, and the sentence to carry home.** *"Four rules on the card. This card is for your own heart, and one trusted person helping you read it — never for diagnosing somebody else; nobody gets filed under a column. In every case, tell someone — darkness spoken aloud loses half its weight. Make no big decisions in the dark. And know when to call the doctor — if the gray has swallowed everything, sleep and appetite and humor, that is a treatable illness, not a spiritual verdict, and getting help is obedience."*

*"And here is the sentence I most want you to carry out of this room. The card says it plainly: the fact that His absence hurts you is the evidence that He has not gone. Drifting hearts do not ache. Abandoned fields do not thirst. A dry season is not a verdict."*

### **Freedom notes (for the senior)**

- The six numbered lines are yours verbatim; everything between them is yours in your own voice. The column walk (point 4) especially should sound like you, not like a card being read.
- Slow down at point 5. It is the hinge of the whole teaching. Two beats of silence after "opposite motions" is not a freeze; it is the point landing.
- Do not look for the person the teaching is describing. There is one in the room; the adults are watching for them. Your job is to teach the whole room so that person can receive without being seen receiving.
- If a question comes from the floor, one sentence of answer or *"hold that — the Companions and I will get you a real answer after"* are both right. You are not the sorter, tonight or ever at FC1.

### **The freeze-recovery grace protocol (decided in advance, held by the whole team)**

If the senior loses the thread in front of the full room: the adult catch stands, steps beside them with visible warmth — a hand to the shoulder is right — and picks up the spine at the next numbered point, conversationally, as if this were always the plan. The senior re-enters whenever they reach for it — a nod, a hand out, taking the next point — and the catch yields immediately and sits back down. Nobody apologizes; nobody explains; the room takes its cue from the team's ease. The gate is fluency across the year, not perfection tonight — a senior who taught this cleanly in clusters last week has already shown the competence; tonight is the bigger room, and the recovery itself, done with grace, is leadership on display. The feedback round still honors the rep, whatever happened.

## **Block 4 — The Path Home Card, Taught by a Companion-in-Formation (4:44–4:54, 10 min)**

The second teaching segment — a different senior if numbers allow, with their own named catch. The room takes out the Path Home Card.

### **The scripted spine (verbatim — the senior works from these five points)**

**1. Why the card exists.** *"One more card. Take out the Path Home Card. Here is what it is. Every formation journey worth taking has people who fall away from it. Some drift. Some burn out. Some blow it — really blow it — and conclude that a room like this one would not want them back. And most churches, without meaning to, leave the way back unwritten: shelves of material for starting the journey, and not one page for returning to it. So the returner improvises the hardest walk of their life alone, guessing at the reception. Not here. This card is the written door."*

**2. The five steps.** *(Walk them briefly, in your own words, hitting each step's center:)* Come as you are — and learn to tell conviction from shame: conviction is specific and hopeful and draws you toward home; shame is vague and hopeless and drives you away, and the voice telling you the door is barred is not the doorkeeper's voice. Tell it straight — to God in plain words, and to one trusted person; you choose the person and how much. Grieve what needs grieving — lament is faith with tears in its voice, refusing to leave the conversation. Return to the table — small; the Rhythm Card's own rule was written for this moment: do not start over, begin again on the next available day; five dry minutes tomorrow morning is a full return. And let it be celebrated — because in the Lord's own story of return, the ending is not a probation meeting. It is a feast.

**3. The father.** *"Hear the reception, because the card stands on one verse. Luke fifteen, twenty: 'But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him.' Still a long way off. The father was watching the road. He ran first, embraced first, and asked for no accounting before the robe. That is the reception."*

**4. Both sides of the door.** *"And that means this card teaches every one of us two things, not one. If you are ever the one coming home: this is the road, and it ends at a feast. And if you are ever the one at home when somebody returns — and some of you will be — this card is your instructions too. No interrogation. No accounting. No 'where were you.' The father ran while the son was still far off. We receive the way the father received. A fellowship that has learned that before anyone needs it is a fellowship a wanderer can come home to."*

**5. The witnessed filing.** *"Now — why are we handing you this tonight, when nobody in this room has gone anywhere? Because the person who eventually needs this card will not be sitting in a circle like this one when they need it. They will be far off, sure the door is locked. So we file it now, while everyone is here to hear me say it: the door is not locked. The light is on. Everyone take the Path Home Card and put it in your folder — right now, while we watch each other do it. That filing is a promise this room is making to every person in it."*

*(Wait for the filing. Watch it happen. Then:)* *"The door is not locked. The light is on. That's the teaching."*

### **Freedom notes (for the senior)**

- Point 3 is the emotional center of your segment. Read Luke 15:20 from the card or the Bible — exactly, not paraphrased — and then say the three observations (watching the road; ran first; no accounting) in your own words.
- The filing at point 5 is a real moment, not a logistic. Give it ten full seconds of silence. Let the room hear the folders close.
- If someone calls the card morbid — "why are we planning for failure?" — the answer is on your spine's back pocket: *"A fire extinguisher is not a plan to burn the house down. We put it on the wall because we intend to live here a long time."* One sentence, warmly, and move on.
- Expect the quiet question afterward, probably from a teen: "Can I give this card to someone?" — an older sibling, a friend who left. The answer is yes. The card is a door, and doors are for sharing.

## **Block 5 — The Journal Arc Review (4:54–5:08, 14 min)**

The room stays seated where it is. The Lead Companion uncovers the three prompts on the board. Then twelve genuinely quiet minutes — no music, no walking commentary — while each person goes back through their own journal from Week 1.

## Script (Lead Companion)

*"Now the year itself. Take out your journal — the one you have been writing in since September. If yours isn't with you tonight, take paper and walk the year from memory; it is in you even if it isn't in your hands."*

*"Here is the invitation. Go back to the beginning — and start at the very first page, the question you wrote on night one, the one you would have asked Him if you knew He would answer. Read it before anything else, and notice what the year has done to it: answered, changed into a different question, or still standing — every one of those is a real answer, and each one tells you something about the road you just walked. Then your first entries, Week 1, Week 2, reading forward. Not every word; you're walking the road again, not re-taking the class. As you go, hold three questions."* (Uncover the board:)

**Where did I begin?**

**Where did He meet me?**

**What do I not want to lose?**

*"Where did I begin — what was actually true of you in September; be honest with the person who wrote those first pages. Where did He meet me — the entries where something happened: a garden visit, a doubt named, a scripture that landed, a conversation in this room. Mark those pages; fold a corner. And what do I not want to lose — because in two weeks the Wednesday container is gone, and something in these pages is worth carrying out with both hands."*

*"Twelve minutes, in quiet. Parents and teens both — this is everyone's year. If something you wrote in October brings up tears in the reading, that is the year doing its work. Begin."*

## Notes

- Hold the silence. This is one of the few blocks all year where the leaders' whole job is to do nothing audibly. Companions read their own journals during this block — visibly. The room should see every adult walking the same road.
- Watch the faces, gently, without staring: the review will land hard on somebody — a page from a season they had half-forgotten, or an entry from a week that has since gone gray. Note who; do not approach mid-block unless distress is real (then the Settle Protocol, quietly).
- Two-minute warning, softly: *"Two more minutes. Find your one sentence — the thing you most don't want to lose. You'll share exactly one sentence in your cluster, nothing more."*

## **Block 6 — One Sentence, and the Testimony Gathering (5:08–5:19, 11 min)**

Split to family clusters — 2–3 whole families with their Cluster Companion, own family always together. (Whole room stays as one circle if attendance is about ten or fewer.) The Cluster Companion carries the blank testimony cards and the pen.

### **Inside the cluster — Companion script**

## The one-sentence round (5 min)

*"One sentence each, from your review. Where He met you this year, or what you don't want to lose — whichever sentence is yours. Just the sentence; no story around it, no commentary after it. [Parent's name], would you start us?"*

*(A parent opens — arranged before the session, never a cold call on a teen. Around the circle, parents first, own family together. Receive each sentence with eye contact and nothing else. Pass-anytime holds; a pass gets the same nod a sentence gets.)*

## The gathering (6 min)

*"Thank you. Now — one more invitation, and it is genuinely optional. In two weeks, at the sendings — the family evening at Week 21 and the commissioning at Week 22 — we want the rooms to hear what actually happened this year. Not a report from the leaders. Your sentences. The ones you just said."*

*"So: if you are willing for your sentence to be read aloud at one of those evenings, I will write it on a card right now, exactly as you say it — or you can write it yourself. You choose signed or anonymous — a name makes it a testimony; no name makes it a gift to the room either way. And hear me clearly: this is consent, the real kind. If you would rather your sentence stay in this circle, it stays in this circle — nothing is written, nothing is noted, and nobody will ask again. An empty card is a complete answer."*

*(Pen ready. Take each willing sentence down verbatim — read it back to its owner and get the nod. Mark each card "signed" with the name, or "anon." Collect nothing from decliners — no card, no list, zero footprint. If every person in a cluster declines, that is a fine outcome; say so with your face.)*

### **Cluster Companion notes**

- The read-back matters. The card must say what they said, not a smoothed version. Their words, their spelling of their life.
- Watch for the teen glancing at a parent before answering. If you see it, add lightly: *"And this is each person's own call — parents can be on the card and teens off it, or the other way around. Families don't have to match."*
- Watch for the sentence that is really a disclosure — a sentence with something heavy inside it. Receive it, bless it, do not write it on a testimony card, and hand it to the adult team after the cluster closes. The bright line runs through this block like every other.
- A participant may offer a sentence about another family member ("watching my dad do the doubts night"). Lovely — but the named person gets a veto: check with them, tonight, before the card goes in the stack.
- Cards go into the Companion's pocket, not a pile on the table. From this moment they are pastoral material: the consent list is a confidential document.

## **Block 7 — The Post-Series Survey (5:19–5:22, 3 min)**

## Script (Co-Companion, Parent)

*"One piece of paper to take home, and I'm not going to apologize for it, because it matters. This is the Post-Series Survey — H20.1. Every family took the Pre-Series Survey back at orientation; this is its bookend."*

*"Here is why it is worth twenty minutes of your week. This is a pilot — the first year this has ever run, anywhere. What you write on this page informs the whole thing: what next year's cohort experiences, what gets kept, what gets fixed. Your honest answers — including the critical ones, especially the critical ones — are a gift to families you will never meet. The same covenant that has covered every instrument all year covers this one: no scores, no grades, mirrors not levers, and nothing you write travels with your name beyond the team that signed the covenant."*

*"It goes home tonight, one per person — parents and teens each do their own. Bring it back to the bin at the door next Wednesday, Week 21. Signed or anonymous, your choice, same as always."*

## **Block 8 — The Leader Feedback Round (5:22–5:25, 3 min)**

*The evening's leaders close the working part of the evening the same way every week — and tonight, by design, the Companions-in-Formation go first, together.*

## Structure

1. The Lead Companion frames it: *"All year, the journal has ended every week with the same question — if you were to teach this to someone, what would you tell them? Tonight our seniors stopped answering it on paper and taught. So they go first."*
2. The seniors, together, as a team: *"Before we close — three things from us, same as every week. What we think went well tonight: ______. What we'd do differently next time: ______. What we'd tell whoever teaches this next: ______."* (Each senior speaks; they may divide the questions between them or each answer all three. Tonight's rep — the teaching — is what they assess, honestly, including anything that wobbled.)
3. Then the room — same two questions about tonight. What went well? What would you do differently? Two or three voices on each is plenty. The seniors receive without defending — "thank you" is the whole response.
4. The Lead Companion closes the round with one specific sentence about the teaching the room just received from its own seniors — what it was, and what it means that they carried it. Specific, witnessed, brief. If a freeze happened and the catch stepped in, this is where it is honored in one sentence, not managed away: the recovery was the leadership.

*(The leader may drop this round if the room's energy needs something else. Dropping it twice running goes to the team debrief.)*

## **Block 9 — Closing Container (5:25–5:30, 5 min)**

## Script (Lead Companion)

*"Between-session practice, two pieces, both small. One: the survey. Twenty honest minutes, back in the bin next Wednesday. Two — and this one is really the teaching finishing itself: this week, at home, find both mercy cards. The Signs Card and the Path Home Card, the ones that went into your folder tonight. Decide where they live — a drawer, the front of your Bible, the folder itself on a known shelf — and show one other person in your family where that is. That's the whole practice. The hope is that those cards gather dust for years. The point is that everyone in your house knows the drawer."*

*"Hear tonight in one sentence before we close. Every long walk has dark stretches — Isaiah said so, David said so, and now you have it in writing: the dark stretch is not a verdict, the ache is the evidence He has not gone, and if anyone ever wanders, the door is not locked and the light is on. You are now a room that knows that. Rooms that know that are rare, and they are where people come home."*

*"Next Wednesday is Week 21 — the sending of the families. The Wednesday after is Week 22 — the commissioning of these Companions. Bring your whole family to both. What you gathered tonight will meet you there."*

*(Run the standard closing protocol — the one-word landing, the one thing, the one practice, blessings. Then:)*

*"The blessing. Hands up if you want."*

*"The Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make His face shine on you and be gracious to you; the Lord turn His face toward you and give you peace."*

*"Go gently. See you Wednesday."*

# **Team Debrief**

Companion team debriefs Week 20 in the look-back of the Thursday Call (Handbook Section 3). Tonight's debrief carries two items with consequences beyond the week; take them in order and in writing.

### **1. The CiF readiness verdict (carried to Week 22 prep)**

Tonight was the final and largest rep before the gate. For each Companion-in-Formation, the team records a verdict against the Handbook Section 11 criteria: did the senior carry the teaching with fluency across the arc (not perfection tonight — fluency across the year); did they stay inside the bright line (any care that surfaced handed to an adult immediately); did they receive the feedback round undefended? If a freeze happened: the recovery, and the senior's return to the spine, count as evidence *for* readiness when done with the grace the protocol trains — say so explicitly in the record. Any hesitation the team holds is voiced now and taken to the parent + Lead Companion conversation this week — never discovered at Week 22. The verdict, with its evidence, is carried into Week 22 preparation.

### **2. The testimony cards secured**

All cards to the Lead Companion tonight, counted. The consent list — who is on a card, signed or anonymous, and which evening each card is suited to — is a confidential document, kept with the Companion observation notes in CCA's designated secure location. Two working decisions while the night is fresh: which cards read at Week 21 (family sending) and which at Week 22 (the commissioning) — sort by whom the sentence blesses; and who reads the anonymous ones aloud. No card is ever edited; a card that cannot be read as written goes back to its owner for their own revision or quiet withdrawal.

### **3. The three vital signs (as every week)**

- **Costly Telling:** the one-sentence round and the gathering — did anyone's sentence cost something? Did the seniors' feedback-round self-assessment cost something?
- **Response to Load:** if the teaching landed on someone, what did the room do in the first ten seconds? If a freeze happened, what did the room do?
- **The Sought Stray:** who went quiet during the journal review or the teaching — and who moved toward them without being asked?

A gray sign sends the team *up* (what have we not tended?) before it looks *around*, per the Three Vital Signs card.

## **If the session worked**

- The room can say the sorting question from memory — you will hear it in the hallway: *which way does the desire point?* The sentence to carry home ("the ache is the evidence") traveled out the door in participants' own words.
- The Path Home filing landed as a warm promise, not a strange ritual. You could hear the folders close in the silence; you could see which faces understood what was being promised.
- The seniors taught rather than performed — the room looked at the cards, not at the teachers. If a wobble happened, the recovery was graceful and the room barely rippled.
- At least one person the dry-season teaching is currently describing was identified by a watching adult — by a face, a stillness, a restroom trip — and is on the 48-hour follow-up list without ever having been exposed in the room.
- The journal review produced real quiet — the working silence of people reading their own year, some of them moved. The one-sentence round afterward was specific, varied, and unforced.
- The testimony gathering produced true cards, freely given — and at least a few declines, which is evidence the consent was real. A gathering with a 100% yield and no declines should make the team ask whether the invitation leaned.
- The survey went home framed as a contribution, not homework.

## **If the session did not work**

- The teaching landed as a quiz — conversation afterward was about sorting other people into columns. Re-teach rule 1 warmly at the Week 21 open: the card is for your own heart, and for one trusted person helping you read it.
- A senior froze and the recovery was clumsy — the catch hesitated, or the room's attention broke to the freeze instead of the teaching. The repair is twofold: a specific word of honor to the senior this week (the freeze is not the story; the year is), and the team's own protocol review before Week 22, where the final rep is public by design.
- The journal review was restless instead of quiet — the room was not held, or the prompts were explained too long instead of trusted. Note it; the Week 21 reflection circle needs the same held silence and gets one more chance to practice it.
- The testimony gathering leaned — a Companion coaxed, a teen glanced at a parent and wrote what the glance asked. Any card the team suspects was not freely given goes back to its owner privately this week with a no-cost exit: *"Only if you still want it read."*
- Anyone left the room with the teaching visibly on them and no adult made contact before the door. That is a same-evening call, not a this-week one.
- The survey was apologized for. Small thing, real thing: an apologized-for instrument returns half-empty. The Week 21 door reminder is warm and unapologetic.

### **People to follow up with this week**

- The person the dry-season teaching is describing — within 48 hours, with the card in hand and no verdict in the pocket. The conversation is the card's own: the desire question, read slowly together, one trusted adult.
- Any parent for whom the teaching named a gray stretch of years — the 1:1 offer stands for parents doubly.
