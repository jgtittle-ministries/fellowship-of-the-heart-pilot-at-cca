## WEEK 14 — SECOND RUNNING: BLOCK A (75 minutes)

**Aim.** Give one more willing teen a complete leading rep — front to back, in front of the cohort — on a practice the room already owns. Nothing new is taught tonight; the every-teen rotation is the curriculum.

**Scripture anchor.** 1 Timothy 4:12 — *“Let no one despise you for your youth, but set the believers an example in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity.”* The rotation’s charter verse.

**Mode.** Teen-led end to end, adults in the room. One of two running options, chosen at the prep meeting per the cohort’s need — Option 1: PROAPT in pairs on a fresh passage; Option 2: the full opening container from memory plus a Family Conversation Cards round. Family clusters as always — two or three whole families with a Cluster Companion, own family always together, parents share first; whole room as one circle when about ten or fewer. The Leader Feedback Round runs at the close, tonight’s leading teen first.

**The option decision.** Made at the prep meeting, by the Companion team with the leading teen — never improvised on the night. The rule of thumb: if the daily PROAPT has thinned since the break and the pairs need another shared rep, run Option 1. If the room needs warmth and the family-conversation muscle more than another scripture rep, run Option 2. Both are full reps; neither is the lesser evening.

**Between-session practice.** Continue the daily rhythm — the morning question, the evening journal note, PROAPT one short passage. And each family pair finishes at home anything the evening opened: the passage’s Tell said again at the table (Option 1), or one more card pulled during the week (Option 2).

**Watch for (Week 14 specific risks).**

- The teen who wants to lead but whose family is in a hard week. Leading the room while your own house is heavy is a weight no teen should carry unnamed. The discernment pair — that teen’s own parent and the Lead Companion — makes the call together, and “not this one; the next one is yours” is an honorable answer, said privately and warmly.
- Comparison between teens. By tonight the cohort has watched several of its own lead, and teenagers keep score. Name it from the front: the rotation is not a competition, there is no ranking, and the room’s job for every leader is the same — receive them. A rep is a rep; nobody’s rep grades anybody else’s.
- Teaching *about* instead of leading *through*. The first-lead temptation, every time. Coach it in the dry run: fewer words, more silence, keep the room working.
- The room deferring to the adults. If eyes drift to the Lead Companion, the block is not teen-led. Adults: chairs outside the circle, eyes on the teen, faces warm and unhelpful.
- Treating tonight as filler between the Mission week and the Rhythm week. It is not filler; it is the mechanism. Say so at the open — this evening exists so that more of the room’s own can lead.

**Crisis contingencies (Week 14).** Tonight is among the lowest-risk evenings in the year by design — scripted practice, low disclosure. Standard Section 6 protocols apply, and the bright line of Handbook 11.2 governs the night: the teen leads the practice; the adults hold anything heavy. If a share turns weighty while the teen is leading, the teen’s only job is the handoff — catch the named adult’s eye, hand it over, keep holding the room. The handoff is honored publicly as leadership, not failure.

# **Companion Preparation**

## **Why this evening exists**

The mechanism is Handbook 11.3: see one, do one, in the same series, in front of the cohort. The fifteen-session map had four teen-led slots — enough to form one Companion-in-Formation. This overlay evening exists so the rotation can breathe: every willing teen, not only the seniors on the FC1 track, gets a full rep at leading a practice the room has already received. The practices themselves need the second pass too — PROAPT deepens on repetition, and the container gets stronger every time a different voice holds it.

1 Timothy 4:12 is the charter. Paul does not tell Timothy to wait until he is older; he tells him to lead in such a way that his youth becomes an argument nobody can use. Tonight the room watches that verse happen.

The bright line (Handbook 11.2) is unchanged and worth restating at the prep meeting: a teen leads *practices*, with an adult present; a teen never carries another person’s disclosure or care. Anything heavy passes immediately to the adult in the room — and that handoff is honored publicly as leadership, not failure. The three rules that never bend (Handbook 11.6) are all satisfied by design tonight: two adults are in the room, the handoff is rehearsed, and the teen leads only what they have first received as a participant.

## **Choosing the teen**

- A willing teen who has *not* already led a marquee, if possible — spread the reps. The Week 4, 8, 10, and 15 slots belong to the seniors being formed toward FC1; tonight belongs to the wider rotation.
- The discernment pair is the same as always: the teen’s own parent and the Lead Companion, both agreeing the teen is ready — and both checking the week, not just the teen. A family in a hard week does not put its teen at the front of the room, however willing.
- Received-first rule: the teen must have participated in the practice they will lead. For Option 1 that is Weeks 7–8; for Option 2, the container they have watched every week since September and the cards their family has held in their folder since orientation.

## **Choosing the option**

Decide at the prep meeting, with the teen in the room for the decision. The inputs:

1. **The pulse of the daily practice.** If the January re-entry showed the daily PROAPT thinned badly, Option 1 gives the whole room a working rep and the pairs a fresh start.
2. **The temperature of the families.** If the room needs warmth — a season of exams, a heavy month, clusters that have gone quiet — Option 2 is the better medicine.
3. **The teen’s own strength.** A teen at home in scripture takes Option 1 naturally; a teen with the host’s gift — the one who makes a circle feel safe — will shine in Option 2. Play to the rep they can win.

For Option 2, the teen chooses the card set with the team at prep — one Connect’s cards, from the shared deck. Coming off the Mission week, the MISSION cards (31–40) land naturally; the GOD cards (21–30) are the evergreen alternative. The teen’s call, with the team’s counsel.

## **Coaching the teen**

Three commitments, copied in spirit from Week 8 and non-negotiable:

1. **The teen lives in the material first.** Option 1: the teen PROAPTs the chosen passage daily for at least three days before leading it — the passage will speak differently on the third hearing, and that experience is what they will invite the room into. Option 2: the teen runs the opening container protocol from memory, out loud, at home, at least three times, and pulls their chosen cards with their own family once during the week.
2. **One full dry run, mid-week, with the Lead Companion.** The whole arc, out loud, timed — not a conversation about it. The Lead Companion plays the room. Coach the two classic first-lead errors: explaining instead of leading, and rushing the silences.
3. **Rehearse the handoff and name the catch.** One adult — usually the Lead Companion — is named as the catch, and the teen knows exactly where that adult will sit. Drill the handoff question until the answer is boring: *“A share turns heavy while you’re leading — what do you do?”* Catch the adult’s eye, hand it over, keep holding the room. No heroics.

**The freeze-recovery grace protocol.** First leads sometimes freeze — mid-sentence, mid-silence, the card suddenly blank in the hand. The protocol, agreed at prep and rehearsed in the dry run: the catch counts silently to ten (most freezes resolve on their own; the recovery is the formation), then steps in seamlessly at the next one-liner — warm, unhurried, no flag on the play — and offers the lead back at the next step with a nod. A freeze is met with warmth, never rescue-with-shame. If the catch had to step in, the feedback round names what the teen did well *first*, and the stepping-in is described as the team working, not the teen failing. Nobody apologizes for a freeze; everybody honors a recovery.

## **Team pre-work**

1. Confirm the option, the teen, the passage or card set, and the catch. Make sure the teen knows where the catch will be sitting.
2. Agree the discipline of the night: adults outside the circle during the teen’s blocks, eyes on the teen, no rescuing short of the protocol. A ten-second stumble the teen recovers from is worth more to their formation than a smooth block an adult saved.
3. Walk the merged Leader Feedback Round order (Handbook 11.7 folded into the weekly round): the teen answers the two questions first — what went well, what I’d do differently — the Lead Companion answers the same two second, with one specific affirmation and at most one growth item, and the room takes the same two questions only by the teen’s consent, popcorn-style, “for you” language.
4. Arrange the parents-first openings: which parent opens the mission round, and which parent opens each cluster’s sharing round. Never a cold call on a teen.
5. Update the rotation ledger: who has led, who is willing and has not yet, who tonight’s rep leaves for Block B week and beyond. The ledger is how “every willing teen” stays a fact instead of a hope.

## **Logistics pre-work**

1. Option 1: choose and print the passage sheet — Luke 10:25–37 or Matthew 14:22–33 suggested (see Block 4A) — one per participant, passage printed with space to write each step. The teen leads from the shared PROAPT card itself; print one cardstock copy for their hand plus a spare.
2. Option 2: pull the chosen card set from the shared Family Conversation Cards deck — one printed set per cluster, plus the teen’s own copy. Confirm each family still has their home deck; bring spare decks for any that have gone missing.
3. Confirm the family-cluster groupings — or plan for one circle if the room will be about ten or fewer.
4. Journals and Bibles as every week; pens in every cluster space.

# **Block-by-Block: Scripts and Notes**

*The italicized scripts in the teen’s blocks are theirs — written as a model for the dry run, not a cage. The teen should say it in their own words; what is fixed is the shape, the timings, and the handoff rule.*

## **Block 1 — Welcome and Opening Container (4:15–4:22, 7 min)**

## Leading teen’s script

*“Welcome back. Phones silenced and away, please — just for these ninety minutes.”*

*“Let me speak the blessing first. The Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make His face shine on you and be gracious to you; the Lord turn His face toward you and give you peace.”*

**Option 1:** the short weekly restatement is enough — *“Container reminder: Safe, Present, Clear, Intentional. Same four conditions, same room, same us.”* — then straight into the check-in word around the circle.

**Option 2:** this block is the first half of the rep. The teen runs the full Section 5 opening protocol from memory: phones away, standing circle, one-word check-in, put out / bring in, the spoken commitment — *“I am here. I am paying attention. I am willing to be moved.”* — and the opening prayer naming the Holy Spirit. The adults’ measure of this block is not polish but completeness: all the pieces, in order, unhurried.

### **Note for the adult team**

From the teen’s first word, sit back — outside the circle, eyes on the teen, faces warm and unhelpful. The room learns who is leading from where the adults look.

## **Block 2 — The Mission Round (4:22–4:26, 4 min)**

*Week 13 sent everyone home with one small act aligned with their downhill mission. One round, one sentence each, entirely optional.*

## Leading teen’s script

*“Two weeks ago the Mission night sent us home with one assignment: one small act, lined up with your downhill mission — the thing the room gets when you’re most yourself. One sentence each, whoever wants: what was your one act, or what got in the way? ‘I didn’t get to it’ is an honest sentence. [Parent’s name], would you start us?”*

*(The arranged parent opens. Take contributions around the circle — one sentence, pass anytime, receive them all the same way. Do not let it become a testimony hour; the round is an acknowledgment, not a debrief.)*

*“Good. The works were prepared beforehand; we keep walking into them. That assignment doesn’t expire.”*

## **Block 3 — Why Tonight: the Rotation’s Charter (4:26–4:28, 2 min)**

## Leading teen’s script

*“Tonight nothing new gets taught. That’s on purpose. Everything we do tonight, this room has already done — we’re running it again, and this time I’m leading it. That’s the design of this whole year: see one, do one, in front of each other. It’s not just the seniors headed for commissioning — it’s every one of us who’s willing. My turn is tonight. Somebody else’s turn is coming.”*

*“Paul wrote to a young leader named Timothy: ‘Let no one despise you for your youth, but set the believers an example in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity.’ That’s First Timothy 4:12, and it’s the charter for tonight. And one thing to say plainly, because we’re teenagers and we keep score: this is not a competition. There’s no ranking of who led best. The room’s job for every leader is the same one — receive them.”*

*“The team is here. But the practice is ours now. Here’s tonight.”*

## **Block 4A — PROAPT Walked Together: the Fresh Passage (4:28–4:42, 14 min)** *(Option 1)*

The marquee block. The teen leads all six steps, timed, from the shared PROAPT card in their hand — the same card the whole room carries, which is itself part of the point. The room works the passage sheet. The adult team does not speak unless the handoff comes.

**The passage.** Fresh and narrative. Two suggestions, either of which carries a first-time leader well:

- **Luke 10:25–37** — the Good Samaritan. Rich Observe material: a lawyer testing, a question answered with a question, two religious men passing by, an enemy who stops, oil and wine and two denarii. The Apply presses gently on everyone: *who has been left on your road — and which of the three are you being?*
- **Matthew 14:22–33** — Peter on the water. The storm, the fear, the call, the sinking, the immediate catch. The Apply lands on courage and on what happens when it fails: *where is He calling you out of the boat — and what happened last time you sank?*

The teen chooses with the Lead Companion at prep. Some of the room may have met the passage in daily practice; name it, don’t apologize for it — *“a passage speaks differently on a second hearing.”*

## How the teen walks it (the six steps, timed)

**PRAY (90 sec).** *“Thirty seconds, eyes closed. Tell the Holy Spirit you want to hear what He has for you tonight — not what you already know about this passage.”* Silence. Then one sentence aloud: *“Holy Spirit, You are welcome here. Speak. We are listening.”*

**READ (2 min).** The teen reads the passage aloud once, slowly. Then silence while the room reads it again on their own — to receive, not to analyze.

**OBSERVE (3.5 min).** *“Two layers, in order. First the data — who, what, when, where, straight off the page. Then what stands out or surprises you in it. Write three things — data first.”* Sixty seconds writing, then 3–4 shares. Receive each one; evaluate none.

**APPLY (3.5 min).** *“What is this saying to YOU, today, specifically? Not in general — in your actual life, this week.”* Ninety seconds writing, then 2–3 shares. If an Apply is vague, ask once: *“Can you make that more specific?”*

**PRAY AGAIN (90 sec).** *“Eyes closed. Tell God what you heard, and what you’re willing to do — or struggling to be willing to do.”* Hold the silence. Do not fill it.

**TELL (60 sec).** *“The Tell happens in your family cluster tonight — and again at home this week. It’s the step that makes the hearing stick.”*

### **Notes for the adult team during Block 4A**

- Do not rescue a silence. The teen was coached to hold them; let them.
- If the teen freezes, the catch runs the freeze-recovery protocol: count ten, step in at the next one-liner, warm and seamless, offer the lead back at the next step. No flag on the play.
- The handoff: if any share turns heavy, the teen catches the named adult’s eye and hands it over — and the adult takes it fully, immediately, without making the handoff feel like an alarm. The teen keeps holding the room.

## **Block 5A — Bridge to Pairs (4:42–4:43, 1 min)** *(Option 1)*

## Leading teen’s script

*“Same pair structure as the fall: in your family cluster, parent with teen from your own family, by default — one Reader, one Listener. Reader leads the six steps on tonight’s passage; Listener works alongside without interrupting. After twelve minutes, switch. Your Cluster Companion settles any odd numbers.”*

*(If the room is about ten or fewer, stay as one circle and pair up in place.)*

*“Pray with me. Father, You’ve already spoken tonight. Speak again in the pairs — something specific, something each of us can hear. Amen. Go.”*

## **Block 6A — PROAPT in Family Pairs (4:43–5:09, 26 min)** *(Option 1)*

As Weeks 7–8: parent and teen from the same family by default; two-parent-two-teen families split into two pairs; a triad works with the third person as the silent-witness reader; a teen whose parent is absent pairs with the Cluster Companion or joins another family’s pair — the teen’s choice, asked quietly before the split.

**First pass (12 min). Switch (1 min). Second pass (12 min).** Same passage both directions, or a fresh one on the second pass — Reader’s choice.

## Cluster Companion: when to intervene

- The unstick prompts, unchanged: for Observe, *“one specific word or phrase that caught you”*; for Apply, *“one situation in your life right now — be specific.”*
- The winter version of the rerun objection: *“We’ve done PROAPT all year.”* — *“Then you know the steps. Tonight isn’t about the steps; it’s about what He says through them tonight. Second hearings are where the specific word comes.”*
- Slow the rushers; hand early finishers a second passage.

## **Block 7A — Tell Step in the Cluster (5:09–5:16, 7 min)** *(Option 1)*

Re-form the family cluster out of pairs. Each participant tells the cluster one thing they heard tonight — one sentence. A parent opens, arranged beforehand; teens choose their own depth; pass-anytime holds. Receive each with *“we hear that”* or *“thank you for naming that”* — brief, no preaching.

## **Block 8A — Merge and Between-Session (5:16–5:19, 3 min)** *(Option 1)*

## Leading teen’s script

*“Going home with you tonight: the daily rhythm keeps going — the morning question, the journal note, PROAPT one short passage. And one specific ask: the Tell you gave your cluster tonight — say it once more this week at your own table. Pairs, finish anything tonight opened and didn’t close.”*

## **Block 4B — The Round Modeled: One Card, Whole Room (4:28–4:36, 8 min)** *(Option 2)*

The teen has already carried the full opening container from memory — the first half of the rep. This block is the second half’s opening move: the teen models the card round before the clusters run it, and models it by going first themselves.

## Leading teen’s script

*“Your family has had the Family Conversation Cards since the fall. Tonight we use them here — and I picked the set: the [MISSION / GOD] cards. Here’s how a round works, and I’ll go first so nobody has to wonder how honest to be.”*

*(The teen reads their chosen card aloud — question, and anchor verse. Then answers it, honestly, briefly — coached at prep to be specific and unpolished. Then:)*

*“That’s the whole practice: read the card, answer it honestly, receive each other without fixing. [Parent’s name], same card — would you go next?”*

*(The arranged parent answers. Take one or two more voices, pass anytime. Then frame the rules for the clusters:)*

*“Three rules for the clusters. The card does the work — nobody coaches, nobody steers. Receive without fixing — when someone tells you something real, the temptation is to improve it; don’t. And ‘I’d rather not take that one tonight’ is a complete answer — we pull a different card.”*

## **Block 5B — Bridge to Clusters (4:36–4:37, 1 min)** *(Option 2)*

Card sets handed to each Cluster Companion. The teen prays and sends the clusters. *(If the room is about ten or fewer, stay as one circle; the teen simply keeps facilitating.)*

## **Block 6B — Family Conversation Cards in Clusters (4:37–5:12, 35 min)** *(Option 2)*

Each cluster works two or three cards from the teen’s chosen set — about ten to twelve minutes per card, every voice offered a turn, pass anytime, a parent opening the first card’s round (arranged beforehand). The leading teen facilitates their own family’s cluster — their rep continues — and the Cluster Companions run the other clusters *on the teen’s pattern*, deliberately mirroring the frame the teen set in Block 4B, so the whole room is running the teen’s evening.

## Cluster Companion: when to intervene

- The fixer: a parent (usually) answering a teen’s answer with counsel. Redirect gently: *“Receive it. You can talk about it in the car if they bring it up.”*
- The performer: an answer polished for the room. *“That sounds like the college-essay version. What’s the smaller, truer one?”* — once, gently, then let it stand.
- The pass that looks like distress rather than preference: honor the pass, catch the adult’s eye, follow up offline. In the teen’s own cluster this is the bright line in miniature — anything heavy goes to the adult sitting right there, and the teen keeps the round moving.
- Time: better two cards done well than three done fast. Do not interrupt an honest answer to stay on schedule.

## **Block 7B — Merge and Between-Session (5:12–5:19, 7 min)** *(Option 2)*

## Leading teen’s script

*“Welcome back. One word each for what the card round was like — just the word. Pass anytime.”*

*(Around the circle. Receive each word with a nod; no commentary.)*

*“Between now and next Wednesday: your family deck comes off the shelf once. One card, any card, ten minutes — the car works, the table works. The deck only helps a family that actually pulls a card. And the daily rhythm keeps going — the morning question, the journal note, PROAPT one short passage.”*

## **Block 9 — Closing Container (5:19–5:23, 4 min)**

The teen runs the closing protocol: the standing circle, the one-word landing (*“one word for what is happening in you right now as we close”*), the one thing (*“one specific thing you are taking from tonight”*), the one practice (each person commits or modifies the between-session practice aloud). Any specific witnessed blessings offered are received; kept short. In Option 2, closing from memory completes the container half of the rep.

## **Block 10 — The Leader Feedback Round (5:23–5:27, 4 min)**

*Merged 11.7 order, exactly as Week 8. The Lead Companion runs the round; the leading teen answers first.*

## Script (Lead Companion)

*“Before the blessing — the feedback round, same as every week. [Name] led tonight, so [name] goes first. Three things: what went well tonight, what you’d do differently next time, and — if you were to teach tonight’s process to someone — what you would tell them.”*

**The teen answers first:** *“What I think went well tonight: ______. What I’d do differently next time: ______. If I were to teach tonight’s process to someone, I would tell them: ______.”*

**The Lead Companion answers the first two questions second** — for the adult side of the evening — and adds one specific affirmation of the teen’s lead (specific — *“you held the silence after Apply and didn’t fill it”* — not general) and at most one thing to try differently. One thing, not a list. If the catch stepped in at any point, this is where the recovery is named as leadership: what the teen did *well* around the freeze, said first and specifically.

**Then, by the teen’s consent** (*“[Name] — okay to open it to the room?”*): *“Now the room — same two questions about tonight. What went well? What would you do differently?”* Popcorn-style, “for you” language — the room reflects on its own experience, not grading a friend. Two or three voices on each question is plenty. The leaders receive without defending — “thank you” is the whole response. If the teen declines the room’s round, it ends after the adult’s answers — no pressure, no exposure.

## **Block 11 — Aaronic Blessing and Sending (5:27–5:30, 3 min)**

## Leading teen’s script

## **The Three Vital Signs log**

| **Sign** | **What to log tonight** |
| --- | --- |
| Costly Telling | Any share that cost something real — in the mission round, an Apply or Tell (Option 1), a card answer (Option 2). Who, roughly what register, and whether it was received well. The Leader Feedback Round itself counts as the week’s scheduled instance. |
| Response to Load | The room carried a teen-led evening: how did it bear the weight? Note the freeze-recovery moment if there was one, and how the room treated it. Note any family visibly in a hard week. |
| Sought Stray | Anyone drifting — the family whose attendance has thinned since the break, the teen who went quiet tonight. Who moves toward them this week, and how. |

## **Specifics**

- **The rotation ledger.** Tonight’s rep recorded; the remaining willing-but-not-yet teens named; the natural candidate for the next open slot penciled in. Comparison-watch: did any teen’s face say “score-keeping” tonight? A quiet word this week — *“your rep is coming; it isn’t a ranking”* — costs two minutes and saves a month of quiet resentment.
- **The teen’s debrief (separate, within 48 hours).** How did the lead feel from the inside? Find the sideways moment — there is always one — and mine the recovery. If the handoff or the freeze protocol ran, walk it again warmly. One growth edge named, and one specific honor given, from the Lead Companion personally.
- **The teen’s parent.** A two-minute word about what the team saw in their teen — especially if the family’s week was heavier than the room knew.
- **Option review.** Did the prep-meeting option call prove right? Note it for Block B week and for the next cycle’s calendar.
- **People to follow up with.** Anyone whose mission-round sentence was “it fell apart”; anyone the evening visibly landed on; any pass that looked like distress.

## **If the session worked**

- The teen led the whole arc — open to blessing — within a few minutes of the timings, leading people *through* the practice rather than talking about it.
- The room answered to the teen, not to the adults. Eyes stayed forward.
- The mission round was honest and light — real sentences, including honest misses, received without guilt.
- Option 1: Tell-step contributions were specific and varied, and at least one was costly. Option 2: at least one card answer in each cluster was the smaller, truer version — and it was received without fixing.
- The Leader Feedback Round ran in its merged order, and the teen received both halves without inflating or collapsing.
- If a freeze happened: the recovery was warm, the room barely rippled, and the round named it as leadership.

## **If the session did not work**

- The teen lectured — long explanations, short silences, the room watching instead of working.
- The evening was teen-fronted but adult-led: the room kept deferring, or an adult rescued what the protocol said to let stand.
- The mission round turned into a guilt audit, or stretched into a testimony hour that ate the marquee’s time.
- Option 2 only: the cards became an interview — adults quizzing teens — instead of a shared round with parents answering first.
- Comparison surfaced and went unnamed. If teens left the room ranking each other’s reps, the charter verse needs to be said again at the next open — by an adult, plainly.
