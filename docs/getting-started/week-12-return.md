## WEEK 12 — THE RETURN (90 minutes)

**Aim.** Full re-entry after the Christmas break. Every break is a natural test of whether the practices survived without the container holding them — tonight rebuilds the room, reads without shame what held and what slipped, welcomes back anyone who drifted, and re-anchors the cohort in the year’s map. Slips are data, not failure. The door is the whole point.

**Scripture anchor.** Lamentations 3:22–23 — “his mercies never come to an end; they are new every morning.” Supported by Psalm 126 (the restored fortunes; sowing in tears, reaping with shouts of joy).

**Connect focus.** All four, in maintenance mode. Self (the honest reading of what held), Others (the room re-formed), God (the mercies new this very morning), Mission (the year’s map, re-anchored — Week 13 turns outward).

**Mode.** Whole room for the opening, the teaching, the walk home, and everything after; SPLIT into family clusters — two or three whole families with a Cluster Companion, own family always together — for the “What held?” round only. If total attendance is about ten or fewer, skip the clustering; the whole room stays as one circle throughout. The Leader Feedback Round runs after the closing container, just before the blessing.

**Center.** Three movements. (1) “What held?” — in family clusters, parents first: which practices survived the break and which slipped, named plainly, received without commentary. (2) “The walk home” — the Path Home Card in action: the welcome spoken over everyone who drifted, in the Luke 15 register, with nobody owing an explanation. (3) Re-anchoring the year — a brief map moment: the God arc is behind us; Mission and the Rhythm are ahead.

**Who leads.** The Lead Companion, throughout. Tonight is deliberately adult-carried — the room is being rebuilt, and the container protocol itself is the teaching.

**Between-session practice.** Restart (or continue) one daily practice — just one — chosen tonight and written in the journal. Not the whole rhythm. One.

**IJH source.** Vol 2 Eighth and Tenth Explorations (the container, opened and closed on purpose); the shared Rhythm Card (“On returning”); the shared Path Home Card (the written door); Luke 15:11–32 as the ground of the welcome.

## WATCH FOR (Week 12 specific risks)

- The shame spiral over dropped practices. Someone — usually a parent — will arrive having kept nothing through the break and will be quietly rehearsing a confession. Cut it off warmly and early, from the front: slips are data, not failure. The Rhythm Card’s own rule was written for tonight: do not start over from the beginning; begin again on the next available day. The Path Home Card exists for exactly this evening.
- The family that came back reluctant. Two weeks away is long enough for a family to half-decide not to return; some in the room tonight are here on the strength of habit or a spouse’s insistence. Do not chase or spotlight them. Let the slow open, the welcome, and the blessing do the work. The Cluster Companion notes the reluctance for a warm follow-up this week — no agenda, just presence.
- The teen whose break was hard at home. Holidays are the dry season in some houses — grief anniversaries, conflict, empty chairs. A teen may arrive carrying two weeks nobody in the room knows about. Watch the one-word check-in closely tonight; it is the most information-dense thirty seconds of the evening. Anything that crosses the safety threshold runs the full Section 6 protocol.
- Competitive holding. The opposite failure: the family whose practices all held, reporting it in a register that shames the family beside them. Coach the clusters beforehand — what-held is a reading, not a scoreboard, and the parents who go first set the register for everyone.
- The re-entry that will not settle. Some nights the room takes a long time to come back — side conversations, scattered energy, teens wound up from seeing each other. Expected. The protocol is the point tonight: run it slower, not louder. Do not fight the room; contain it.
- The participant who did not come back. An empty chair tonight is a data point the team must not let slide into February. Note who is missing at the door; the Lead Companion makes warm contact this week — the Path Home Card’s promise is only true if somebody carries it to the person.

**If a practice or a conversation overwhelms someone.** Run the Settle Protocol (Handbook §6): stop with plain directive words, bring them back to the room, breathe and anchor, and nobody leaves activated — a Companion stays until their body has settled. Section 6 of the Handbook governs anything that crosses the safety threshold. The two-adult rule holds in every space, all evening, as always.

# **Block-by-Block: Scripts and Notes**

## **Block 1 — Opening Container, Run Slow (7:00–7:12, 12 min)**

Tonight the opening protocol is not the on-ramp to the session; it is the session’s first teaching. Two weeks without Wednesdays is exactly the kind of gap the container exists to close. Run every step of the Section 5 protocol, at about two-thirds the usual speed, and let the repetition do what eleven weeks of repetition has built it to do.

From the Handbook, Section 5 — the opening protocol the room has used every week:

1. **Welcome.** “Welcome back. We’re going to take five minutes to open the container. We do this every week because what happens here only happens when this is a real container, not just a meeting.”
2. **Phones silenced and put away.** A simple ask — no box, no collection. Allow a few seconds.
3. **Stand in a circle (yes, standing).** Standing brings different energy than slouching on a couch. Hold for the next four steps.
4. **One-word check-in.** “One word for how you actually are right now — not how you usually are, not how you should be. Right now.” Each person, around the circle, says one word. No commentary.
5. **Put out / bring in.** “Is there something you need to put down before we start so you can be present? Name it silently and let it go for the next ninety minutes. Now, what blessing or intention do you want to bring in?” Silent for 30 seconds.
6. **Spoken commitment.** Together: “I am here. I am paying attention. I am willing to be moved.”
7. **Opening prayer.** Brief, naming the Holy Spirit specifically. (“Holy Spirit, you are welcome here. Speak. We are listening.”)
8. **Sit.** Begin the session.

## Tonight’s adjustments — the slow open

- At step 1, name the slowness out loud (script below). The room should know the pace is on purpose.
- At step 4, the one-word check-in gets a second beat: after each word, hold one unhurried breath before the next person speaks. Do not comment, do not nod anyone along. Two weeks of life is arriving in the room one word at a time; give each word its second.
- At step 5, double the silence — a full minute instead of thirty seconds. There is more to put down tonight than most weeks.
- The Companion assigned to timing starts at 7:00 and quietly notes the minute the room becomes truly present — the fidgeting stops, the words land, the silence holds. That number goes to the debrief, and nowhere else.

## Script

*“Welcome back. It is very good to see every one of these faces.”*

*“We’re going to open the container the same way we have opened it every Wednesday since September — and tonight we are going to do it slowly, on purpose. It has been two weeks. The container we built this fall did not sit in this room over the break waiting for us; containers don’t keep like that. They are rebuilt every time, by the same small faithful steps. So tonight the steps get their full weight. Don’t rush them. There is nowhere else we need to be.”*

*(Run the eight steps. At the one-word check-in:)*

*“One word for how you actually are right now — not how you usually are, not how you should be. Right now, tonight, coming back. We’ll go slowly. Let each word have a breath.”*

*(Receive every word with eye contact. The check-in is the most information-dense thirty seconds of the evening — the Cluster Companions are listening for their own families’ words.)*

## **Block 2 — New Every Morning: Lamentations 3:22–23 and Psalm 126 (7:12–7:24, 12 min)**

Read both passages aloud, slowly, from the physical Bible. The teaching is short; the whole evening leans on it.

## Script (the read)

*“Before we read what the break did to our practices, we read what is true this morning and every morning. Listen — this is from Lamentations, chapter three. A man sitting in the wreckage of everything, saying this:”*

*“The steadfast love of the LORD never ceases; his mercies never come to an end; they are new every morning; great is your faithfulness.”*

— Lamentations 3:22–23 (ESV)

*“And Psalm 126, the song of a people coming home:”*

*“When the LORD restored the fortunes of Zion, we were like those who dream. … Those who sow in tears shall reap with shouts of joy! He who goes out weeping, bearing the seed for sowing, shall come home with shouts of joy, bringing his sheaves with him.”*

— Psalm 126:1, 5–6 (ESV)

### **Teaching points (use any format — do not read this verbatim)**

• Notice where Lamentations 3:22–23 sits. It is not in a psalm of victory. It is in the middle of the saddest book in the Bible, written in the ashes of Jerusalem. The man who wrote “great is your faithfulness” had just lost nearly everything. That is where the mercies-new-every-morning truth lives — not in the seasons when everything held, but in the mornings after the seasons when it didn’t.

• “New every morning” means exactly what it says. Not new every morning that you earned by keeping your practices. Not new every morning except the mornings after a two-week gap. His mercies do not carry over a balance from December; they are issued fresh, this morning, to everyone in this circle, whatever the break looked like.

• Tonight we are going to read our break honestly — what held and what slipped — and we can afford to read it honestly for exactly this reason. The reading costs nothing. The mercy was already new before we walked in.

• And Psalm 126 tells us what the return is like: the people coming home “were like those who dream.” Coming home is not a trudge of make-up work. In God’s economy it is the happiest walk there is. Hold that; we will come back to it after the clusters.

## **Block 3 — “What Held?” (7:24–7:44, 20 min)**

Split to family clusters — two or three whole families with their Cluster Companion, own family always together. (If the room stayed as one circle tonight, the same round runs whole-room, Companions receiving.) Each participant reads their break honestly: which practices survived the two weeks, and which slipped. A parent goes first in every cluster — arranged before the session, never a cold call on a teen — and reads an honest list that includes the slips. Teens choose their own depth after; pass-anytime holds.

## Script (bridge to the split — Lead Companion)

*“Here is the first movement of the night, and it is a reading, not a report card.”*

*“Since Week 7 you have been carrying daily practices — the morning question, the evening note, PROAPT for some of you. The Rhythm Card in your folder names them all. For eleven weeks those practices had this room behind them: Wednesday was coming, the faces were coming, the room was holding you. Then came the break — no Wednesday, no room. Every break is a natural test, and it is a test of the practices, not of you.”*

*“So in your clusters: one at a time, say plainly which practices held through the break and which slipped. That’s all. Nobody fixes anybody. Nobody compares. Slips are data — they tell us where the rhythm needs the room, and that is useful information, not a verdict. A parent will start in each cluster.”*

*“And before you split — back in Week 2, when this check-in was born, we stacked our first stone: Joshua’s twelve stones out of the Jordan, piled up on purpose so the children would one day ask, ‘What do these stones mean?’ Every check-in since September has added one. The break did not knock the pile over. Tonight’s reading is the answer to Joshua’s question — what held.”*

*“Family clusters: [name each cluster’s families and its Companion]. Twenty minutes. Go.”*

### **Inside the cluster — Companion script**

*“We have twenty minutes. One at a time, around the circle — what held through the break, and what slipped. Plain words. [Parent’s name], would you start us?”*

*(The arranged parent reads first — honest, including the slips. Receive each reading with a nod and nothing else. Teens choose their own depth; “most of it slipped” is a complete answer; pass is a complete answer.)*

*(When the round is done, if time allows:)* *“One more question, lighter: did anything about the break surprise you — a practice that held that you expected to lose, or a moment you noticed God without the room’s help?”*

### **Cluster Companion: how to hold the round**

- Guard the register. The moment a reading turns into a confession — “I’m sorry, I really meant to…” — receive it and reset gently: “No apology owed. That’s data. Thank you for reading it straight.”
- Guard against the scoreboard. If a family’s full-marks reading lands heavy on the family beside them, thank them plainly and widen the frame: “Good. And every house has different weather over a break — that’s why we read, not grade.”
- Notice, do not chase. A teen whose reading hints that the break itself was hard — not the practices, the house — gets a quiet flag to the Lead Companion, not a follow-up question in the cluster.
- Keep rough counts, quietly, for the debrief: how many in your cluster kept any daily practice through the break. Numbers only. No names travel with them.
- Watch the clock. Everyone reads. A round that lets three people talk long and four people not at all has failed its shape.

## **Block 4 — The Walk Home (7:44–7:59, 15 min)**

Merge to the single circle. This is the heart of the evening. Every participant has the Path Home Card in their folder — filed weeks ago, while the room was warm, against exactly this kind of night. Tonight the card is not taught; it is enacted. The Lead Companion speaks the welcome over the whole room at once, so that everyone who drifted walks back through the door together, and nobody has to identify themselves to be received.

## Script

*“Welcome back to the circle. Take a breath.”*

*“Now the second movement, and it is the important one. Some of you just read a break where the practices mostly held. And some of you just read a break where they mostly didn’t — where the mornings went by, and the journal stayed shut, and somewhere in the second week a quiet voice started saying, ‘Well, that’s that. You had a good run.’”*

*“I want to talk to everyone that voice talked to. You have a card in your folder — the Path Home Card. We filed it weeks ago, while everybody was here and nobody needed it, and I told you then: the person who needs this card will not be sitting in a circle like this one when they need it. They will be somewhere else, sure the door is locked.”*

*“Tonight is the small, ordinary version of that walk. And here is what I want you to know about how it ends. In the Lord’s own story of return, the father does not wait at the door with a list of questions. ‘But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him.’ Still a long way off. The father runs. Nobody in that story owes an explanation before the robe.”*

*“So hear the house rule of this fellowship, said out loud, with everyone watching me say it: nobody in this room owes anybody an explanation for the break. Not me, not your Companion, not your parents, not each other. If you drifted — for two weeks or for most of the break — you are not walking back into an accounting tonight. You already walked back. You are in the chair. That is the whole return. The door was the whole point, and you are on the right side of it.”*

*“The card says the way back is unglamorous: you just start again, small. Do not start over from the beginning; begin again on the next available day. Five dry minutes tomorrow morning is a full return. And the card says one more thing — the returning gets celebrated. So look around this circle for a second. Everybody here came back. Every chair filled tonight is a small feast. I am not going to ask who drifted and who didn’t, because it is none of my business and it changes nothing about the welcome — but I will tell you that as your Companion, seeing this room full again is the best thing I have seen since December.”*

*(Hold a beat. Then, gently:)*

*“If the break was hard in ways that have nothing to do with practices — if the house was heavy, if the season hurt — that is not a circle conversation, but it is absolutely a Companion conversation. Find one of us tonight or this week. That is what we are for.”*

### **Notes**

- Do not ask for hands, testimonies, or self-identification of any kind in this block. The welcome is spoken over everyone precisely so the drifters receive it without cost. The Companions will learn what they need to know from the clusters and the follow-ups, not from the room.
- Watch the room during the Luke 15 read. Somewhere in the circle it will land on someone — the going-still, the wet eyes. No spotlight. The nearest Companion simply notices, and the follow-up happens at the door or this week.
- If a participant spontaneously names their own drift out loud — it happens — receive it as the card receives it: bless the telling, honor the return, add nothing. “Thank you. Welcome home. That took more courage than it looked like.”

## **Block 7 — Closing Container (8:12–8:20, 8 min)**

Run the Section 5 closing protocol, unhurried. From the Handbook:

1. **Stand again.** Same circle, same posture as the open.
2. **One-word landing.** “One word for what is happening in you right now as we close.” Around the circle. Compare to the opening word — the contrast is data.
3. **The one thing.** “What is one specific thing you are taking from tonight?” Each person, brief.
4. **The one practice.** “What will you actually practice between now and next week?” The session’s between-session practice is offered, and each person commits or modifies aloud.
5. **Blessings.** Anyone who has a specific blessing to speak over another person in the room — not a general prayer, but a specific witnessed blessing of what they saw the Father doing in that person tonight — does so. These should be short. They almost always land.

### **Notes**

- The landing words against the opening words are tonight’s best single reading of the evening. The Companions listen for the contrast: “tired” at 7:00 that lands as “home” at 8:15 is the session working. Note the pattern for the debrief — the pattern, never the names.
- At the one-practice step, each person names the one daily practice they just wrote in the journal. Keep it moving; the writing already did the work.
- The blessings step may run warmer than usual tonight. Let it. The first Wednesday back is the right night for the room to bless itself.

## **The three break experiments (recorded tonight, before the team leaves)**

Every break in the year is a natural experiment: what does the formation hold when the container is not holding it? Week 12 records the Christmas-break readings. All three go in the team log the same night, per Handbook §8. No participant is named in any count.

1. **Rhythm survival count.** How many participants kept any daily practice through the break — gathered as rough counts from the Cluster Companions’ what-held rounds. A number and the room total (e.g., “9 of 21 kept at least one daily practice”). Numbers only; no names attached.
2. **Path Home Card uses.** How many walked back tonight — the team’s best count of participants whose break reading indicated a real drift and who returned to the room anyway. Assembled from cluster observation and the door, never from a hand-raise or a public question. This number is the card working; expect it to be more than zero, and log it without any register of disappointment.
3. **Time-to-settled-container.** How many minutes from 7:00 until the room was truly present — timed discreetly by the assigned Companion, who alone knew it was being timed. One number, logged with a one-line note on what the settling looked like.

These three numbers, taken again at the year’s later re-entry, are among the most honest measures the pilot has of whether the formation is becoming the participants’ own or still belongs to the room. Record them plainly; interpret them slowly; compare them only across the year’s own series.
