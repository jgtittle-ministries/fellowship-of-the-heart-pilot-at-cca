## WEEK 16 — FAMILY CONVERSATION NIGHT (90 minutes)

**Aim.** A shared meal and the Family Conversation Cards — low content, high warmth. The container the cohort has built for fifteen weeks gets to simply hold people for an evening: tables, real food, unhurried talk, and two or three card questions over dessert. Pulse 3 goes home at the close and steers the final quarter.

**Scripture anchor.** Acts 2:46 — “And day by day, attending the temple together and breaking bread in their homes, they received their food with glad and generous hearts.”

**Mode.** Tables, not the circle — the one night of the year the room is set as a dining room. Each table seats two or three whole families, own family always together, mixed loosely across the usual cluster lines. No cluster split tonight; the tables are the structure. Whole room for the opening, the Pulse framing, the Leader Feedback Round, and the close. (If total attendance is about ten or fewer, one long table. Even better.)

**Center.** The meal itself, and then the Family Conversation Cards run at the tables over dessert — two or three prompts pre-selected by the Companion team to fit the season. Parents answer first at every table. Laughter is a fully valid outcome.

**Who leads.** The Lead Companion hosts, lightly — the open, the table grace, the Pulse framing, the close. Every other Companion sits at a table tonight as a member of it, not a facilitator.

**Between-session practice.** Complete the Quarterly Pulse (H16.1) — one page, three questions, five minutes at home — and bring it back to the return bin at next Wednesday’s door. And keep the rhythm: the card you built in Week 15 is still the assignment.

**IJH source.** Vol 2 Ninth Exploration (community amplification — the ordinary meal as formation infrastructure); Acts 2:42–47 as the scriptural picture; the shared Family Conversation Cards deck.

## WATCH FOR (Week 16 specific risks)

- The team turning the meal into a program. The strongest temptation tonight is to add content — a devotional between courses, a teaching moment over dessert. Resist all of it. There is no agenda at the table beyond the cards, and the cards come out only at dessert.
- The Four Questions, quietly present at some tables. Week 15 commissioned the at-home asking for the willing. Some homes will have had that conversation by now; some will not; some will have had it and be carrying something tender tonight. NOTHING is checked publicly — invisible-by-default is one of the five guards, and nobody is ever asked, at a table or from the front, whether the asking happened. But the team watches faces. A family sitting close and quiet, a parent whose eyes are full, a teen who will not look up — any Companion who reads it follows up offline, within 48 hours, family by family.
- The rhythm-noticing round drifting into confession or coaching. One sentence each, slips as welcome as holds, no fixing. If someone starts troubleshooting a slipped practice, the leader receives it warmly and moves on — next Wednesday’s walk (if the Float runs Mode B) is the place for troubleshooting.
- A card question landing heavier than the night intends. The pre-selection guards against this — the naming-cards (14, 17, 22, 27, 37) stay in the deck tonight — but any card can find a tender place. The table’s adults receive without fixing; a Companion follows up offline if a face says so.
- The family that did not come. An empty seat at a family night reads louder than an empty seat at a lesson. Note it at the debrief — the Sought Stray question is live tonight — and someone warm reaches out this week. No accounting; just pursuit.
- The Pulse framed apologetically. “Sorry to end a fun night with paperwork” undoes the instrument. The Pulse is how this room steers its own final quarter — frame it without apology, in one unhurried minute.

## CRISIS CONTINGENCIES (Week 16)

*Week 16 is the lowest-risk evening in the series by design — no disclosure exercise, no split, no new practice. The standing protocols still stand: the two-adult rule holds all evening, and Handbook Section 6 governs anything that crosses the safety threshold.*

**If something tender surfaces at a table.** Receive it, bless it, and do not work it at the table. A Companion moves alongside quietly — the meal continues, the room barely notices — and the follow-up conversation opens before the family leaves or by phone within 48 hours.

**If a teen or parent discloses anything touching self-harm, abuse, or crisis.** Full Section 6 protocol, tonight. Warmth is not a reason to defer safety; it is the reason the disclosure came.

**Default:** Section 6 of the Handbook covers anything that crosses the safety threshold.

# **Session at a Glance**

## **Why this session, this week**

Fifteen weeks of formation work sit in this room — story, confession, hearing, gardens, doubts, mission, rhythm. Week 15 handed every participant a rhythm in their own handwriting and handed the parents the bravest homework of the year. Tonight the series does what Acts 2:46 says the first church did between the temple and the teaching: it eats together, gladly. The early church’s formation ran on three rails — the apostles’ teaching, the shared table, and the prayers (Acts 2:42) — and this series has spent almost all its weeks on the teaching and the prayers. Tonight is the table, whole.

There is also a pastoral reason for the timing. In the CCA year this is the next-to-last Wednesday of Quarter 3, with the commissionings still a quarter away. A room that only ever works eventually forgets it is a family. A meal — real food, mixed tables, parents laughing, teens going back for seconds — is how a room remembers. The cards over dessert keep just enough shape that the conversation goes somewhere; the pre-selection keeps them light enough that nobody is ambushed at dinner.

And tonight carries Pulse 3. The quarter closes next Wednesday; the final quarter — consolidation and the two commissionings — is steered by what comes back on that one page. Distributing it at the warmest evening of the year is deliberate: people tell the truth on paper more readily when the room has just fed them.

# **Companion Preparation**

## **Personal pre-work**

Light, and still real. Each Companion sits for ten minutes this week with two questions: Where has my own rhythm held since Week 15, and where has it slipped? — because the opening round asks the room the same thing, and the team answers in it too. And: Which family am I quietly carrying? — the family whose faces the team agreed to watch after Week 15. Come to the night knowing who you are watching, so the warmth has eyes.

## **Team pre-work**

The Thursday Call's look-ahead before Week 16 covers:

1. Settle the food plan (potluck or provided — see Materials) and confirm the allergy list is current from the orientation forms. Assign the food-safety lead for the night.
2. Pre-select the two or three card prompts for dessert. Fit the season: the mission arc just closed and the quarter is ending, so cards that look backward warmly and forward hopefully work best — Card 6 (One footprint), Card 32 (Where you came alive this season), and Card 20 (The ten-year question) are the recommended pilot set. The naming-cards (14, 17, 22, 27, 37) stay in the deck tonight. Print or pull the physical cards.
3. Draw the table map — two or three whole families per table, own family together, mixed loosely across the usual cluster lines. Seat each Companion at a table as a member. Check the map against the two-adult rule for every space in use, kitchen included.
4. Arrange the parents-first openings: one parent, asked ahead, opens the rhythm-noticing round; and at each table, one parent is quietly asked to answer the first card first. Never a cold call on a teen.
5. Name the cleanup crew — two or three teens plus one adult, asked ahead, named at this meeting, thanked from the front at the close.
6. Review the Week 15 watch-list: which families might be carrying a tender Four Questions week. Agree who watches whom, and rehearse the rule aloud: nothing checked publicly, faces watched, offline follow-up within 48 hours.
7. Walk the Pulse 3 framing (Block 6 script) so it lands in one unhurried minute, without apology.
8. Pray for each family by name — specifically that the meal does what meals do.

# **Materials and Setup**

### **Materials checklist**

- Tables — enough to seat two or three whole families each, set as a dining room, not a classroom. Tablecloths if available; this should look like someone meant it.
- The meal. Two workable plans; the team picked one at pre-work:
  - **Potluck:** families bring assigned categories (mains / sides / desserts / drinks) coordinated by a sign-up the week before. The team provides plates, cups, utensils, napkins, and one backup main dish — the family that forgot must never be visible.
  - **Provided:** the team (or a donor family) provides a simple hot meal — the standard is “someone’s good kitchen,” not catering polish. Families bring nothing but themselves.
- Dessert, held back until Block 5 — dessert arriving is the signal the cards are coming.
- **Food safety and allergies.** The allergy list from the orientation forms is current and posted in the serving area. Every serving dish is labeled by a card naming its major allergens (nuts, dairy, gluten, eggs, shellfish). Hot food held hot, cold food held cold; nothing sits out past the meal block. One named adult owns this for the night.

- Handout H16.1 — the Quarterly Pulse, one per participant, distributed at Block 6, not at the door.

- Name tags only if a guest family is present.
- Cleanup supplies: trash bags, wipe-down cloths, dish tubs. The cleanup crew — two or three teens plus one adult, named at team pre-work — is thanked by name at the close.
- Two adults present in every space in use, kitchen included, all evening (the two-adult rule).

### **Room setup**

Tables, not the circle — the one night of the year. Leave one open space near the door large enough for the whole room to stand in a loose circle for the opening and closing containers; the container protocols still run standing, same as every week, and then the room sits down to eat.

# **Block-by-Block: Scripts and Notes**

## **Block 1 — Welcome and Opening Container (7:00–7:07, 7 min)**

## Script

*“Welcome back. The blessing first. The Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make His face shine on you and be gracious to you; the Lord turn His face toward you and give you peace.”*

*“Look at this room. Sixteen weeks in, and tonight there are tables. Here is the whole plan for tonight, so nobody spends the evening waiting for the catch: we are going to eat together. That’s it. That’s the session. There is a short round before the food, a few good questions with dessert, and one piece of paper at the end — and in between, the point of the night is the meal and the people at your table.”*

*“Container, briefly, because it still holds even at a dinner table: Safe, Present, Clear, Intentional. Phones silenced and put away, please — the people at your table tonight are the evening.”*

*“One word for how you actually are, around the circle.”*

*(Receive the words. No commentary.)*

## Notes

Keep the open genuinely short. The room will be smelling the food. A long preamble at a family night teaches the room that the team cannot help itself; a short one teaches that the team meant what it said.

## **Block 2 — Rhythm-Noticing Round (7:07–7:14, 7 min)**

## Script

*“One round before we eat, and it is one sentence each. Two Wednesdays ago you built a Rhythm Card in your own handwriting. The assignment was to run it and notice — not to run it perfectly. So: one sentence. Where has your card held, or where has it slipped? Both answers are welcome here, and I mean that — a slip you noticed is the practice working, because noticing is the skill. No fixing, no advice, from me or anyone. [Parent’s name], would you start us?”*

*(The arranged parent opens — never a cold call on a teen. Around the circle. Pass-anytime holds. If anyone starts troubleshooting, warmly: “Good noticing. Hold the fixing — we may get to walk the cards together next week.”)*

*“Thank you. That is the whole check-in. The card is a long walk; sixteen days in, everyone in this circle is still on it.”*

## Notes

Watch the room during this round — it is tonight’s quiet diagnostic. Who reports a hold with light in their face; who reports a slip with shame in their voice (follow up warmly this week — the Returning Rule from the Rhythm Card is the medicine); who passes. Note it for the debrief, not for the circle.

## **Block 3 — Acts 2:46 and the Table Grace (7:14–7:18, 4 min)**

## Script

*“One verse over this meal. Luke is describing the very first church, the weeks right after Pentecost — the most spiritually alive room in the history of the world. Listen to what they did with their evenings.”*

*“And day by day, attending the temple together and breaking bread in their homes, they received their food with glad and generous hearts.”*

— Acts 2:46 (ESV)

*“The first church ran on three rails: the teaching, the table, and the prayers. We have given fifteen weeks to the teaching, and every one of them to the prayers. Tonight is the table. Breaking bread with glad and generous hearts is not the break between the formation — it is the formation.”*

*“Let’s pray. Father, thank You for this food and the hands that made it. Thank You for every face at these tables. Make us glad and generous tonight, like that first room was. In Jesus’ name, amen.”*

*“Families with young table-setters and servers, you know who you are — everyone else, find your table. Eat.”*

## **Block 4 — The Meal (7:18–7:55, 37 min)**

No agenda. No devotional between courses. No teaching moment. The Companions eat as members of their tables — the fastest way to kill a family night is a team that hovers instead of eats.

### **What the team is doing while doing nothing**

- Watching faces, gently — especially the families on the Week 15 watch-list. A family carrying a tender Four Questions week often shows it at a table: sitting closer than usual, or quieter than usual, or a parent watching their teen with full eyes. Nobody names it tonight. The Companion who sees it makes the offline contact within 48 hours — warm, private, no reference to what was or wasn’t asked: *“Just checking in on your crew. How is your week going?”*
- Noticing who is missing. An absent family tonight goes on the debrief’s Sought Stray line, and someone warm reaches out this week.
- Keeping the food moving and the allergen cards honest. The food-safety lead owns the serving table.
- Enjoying the evening. This is not a lesser assignment. A cohort needs to see its Companions laugh at a dinner table, sixteen weeks in, more than it needs one more well-run block.

## **Block 5 — Cards over Dessert (7:55–8:15, 20 min)**

Dessert lands at the tables; the small card stack comes out with it. The Lead Companion frames from wherever they are sitting — standing up breaks the spell.

## Script

*“Don’t stop eating — this happens over dessert. On your table there’s a small stack of cards. These are the Family Conversation Cards; some of your families already use them at home. We picked two or three for tonight. Here is how it works at your table: someone reads the first card aloud. A parent answers first — every table, every card, parents first, same as this whole year. Then anyone who wants to answers, in any order. Nobody has to. When the card feels done, read the next one.”*

*“Two things. There is no report-out afterward — what is said at your table stays at your table. And there is no wrong way for this to go. If a card takes your table somewhere real, wonderful. If a card takes your table into ten minutes of laughing at your dad’s answer — that is not a failure of the exercise. That IS the exercise.”*

*“Go ahead.”*

### **The pilot card set (selected at team pre-work; adjust by season)**

- **Card 6 — One footprint.** *Looking back at this past month — one place you can see God’s hand more clearly now than you did at the time.* (Anchor: Romans 8:28.) The quarter in one warm look backward.
- **Card 32 — Where you came alive this season.** *What activity, conversation, or work this season has made you most alive? Why?* (Anchor: 1 Peter 4:10.) The mission arc, still echoing.
- **Card 20 — The ten-year question.** *What’s one thing you hope is still true between us in ten years?* (Anchor: Psalm 78:4.) Save this one for last. It lands.

(*Alternate for a room arriving tired:* Card 21 — Where did you see Him today — the smallest, most repeatable prompt in the deck.)

### **Notes for the table parents (arranged at pre-work)**

- You answer first, and your first answer calibrates the table — honest and specific beats polished, and brief leaves room.
- Receive the teens’ answers the way the room has received answers all year: without fixing, without steering, without “that reminds me of a lesson.” A teen’s answer that worries you is a gift — unwrap it later, privately, this week, not at the table in front of another family.
- The teen who deflects every card with a joke: one joke is the exercise working — laughter is a valid outcome. Five in a row is hiding. Do not confront it; just answer the next card honestly yourself, which is the only correction that works at a dinner table.
- What is said at the table stays at the table — including the other family’s teen’s answer that their parents would love to hear. It is not yours to relay.
- If a card finds a tender place at your table, don’t work it at the table. Warmth, a hand on a shoulder, and let the Companions know afterward.
- Watch the clock loosely; two cards done well beats three done fast.

## **Block 6 — Pulse 3 Framed and Sent Home (8:15–8:20, 5 min)**

Handouts pass down the tables while the Lead Companion speaks. This framing is one unhurried minute plus questions; it is not an apology.

## Script

*“Before we close, one page — and I want to tell you plainly what it is, because it matters. This is the Quarterly Pulse, the third one this year. Same as October, same as December: one page, three questions, about five minutes, at home. It asks how present you have actually been, what has surprised you, and how you want to engage the final quarter.”*

*“Here is why I hand it to you without any apology: this room steers by these pages. The final quarter of this year — the consolidation weeks, the two commissionings — will be shaped by what you write here. When you told us things on the last Pulse, we changed things. That is the deal: you tell the truth on one page, and the team builds the last quarter out of it.”*

*“Bring it back next Wednesday and drop it in the bin at the door as you come in. Nobody watches the bin, nobody checks names off — the page can be signed or unsigned, your choice, same as always. Five minutes, sometime this week, at your own table.”*

## **Block 7 — The Leader Feedback Round (8:20–8:25, 5 min)**

*The evening’s leader closes the working part of the evening the same way every week — yes, even on a dinner night. Especially on a dinner night; the round on an easy evening is what keeps it ordinary.*

Leader: *“Before we close — three things from me, same as every week. What I think went well tonight: ______. What I’d do differently next time: ______. If I were to teach tonight’s process to someone, I would tell them: ______. Now the room — same two questions about tonight. What went well? What would you do differently?”*

Two or three voices on each question is plenty. The leader receives without defending — “thank you” is the whole response. Expect food commentary; take it seriously — next year’s family night is built from tonight’s answers.

*(Around the circle. Receive.)*

# **Between-Session Practice**

# **Team Debrief**

The Companion team debriefs within a day or two — thirty minutes is enough for tonight. Open with the Three Vital Signs, in order, same as every week:

1. **Costly Telling.** Who told something costly this week — and what did the room do with it? (On a night like tonight it may be small: a slip named without varnish in the rhythm round, an honest answer at a card table. Small counts. Log it.)
2. **Response to Load.** Where did load arrive — a card that landed hard, a family visibly carrying a tender week — and what did we see in the first ten seconds? Did the table absorb it or look for the leader?
3. **The Sought Stray.** Who was missing tonight, and who is going after them? An empty chair at a family night is the loudest empty chair of the year. Name the pursuer and the day.

Then the specifics:

- The Week 15 watch-list, family by family: what did faces say tonight? Every follow-up gets a name and a 48-hour clock — warm, private, no reference to the Four Questions unless the family opens that door.
- The rhythm-noticing round: who reported with shame rather than honesty? A warm mid-week contact with the Returning Rule in hand.
- Pulse 3: roughly how many pages went home (count the leftovers)? Spares to the door crew for next Wednesday.
- The cards: which prompts worked, which table needed rescue, anything a card surfaced that needs offline care.
- The meal logistics, honestly: food quantity, the allergy system, the table map, the cleanup. Next year’s family night is designed tonight.
- Mode call for next Wednesday: tonight’s debrief is the last scheduled team conversation before the Week 17 prep meeting — walk into it knowing whether the quarter lost an evening. See the Week 17 plan.

## **If the session worked**

- The room lingered. Families were still at tables after the blessing, and nobody hurried them out.
- Laughter and at least one real moment — a card answer that made a table go quiet in the good way.
- Parents answered first at every table without being reminded.
- The rhythm round produced honest slips, spoken without shame.
- The Pulse went home framed as steering, not homework — and nobody groaned.
- The team ate. Actually sat, actually ate, actually enjoyed it.

## **If the session did not work**

- The evening got programmed — somebody added a devotional, a teaching moment, an object lesson. Name it plainly at the debrief; the discipline of withholding content is a team skill and tonight was its test.
- The cards came out too early or ran too long — dinner became a workshop. Adjust the timing note for next year.
- A tender family was visible and nobody moved — the follow-up now happens on the debrief’s 48-hour clock, late but not never.
- A cold call happened — a teen got asked a card question pointedly, or the rhythm round landed on an unarranged opener. Repair privately this week; re-read the parents-first arrangements at the next pre-work.
- Food logistics leaked — an unlabeled dish, a missing allergy check. The food-safety lead owns the fix before any future meal night.
