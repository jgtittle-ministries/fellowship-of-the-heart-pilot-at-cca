## WEEK 6 — SAFE AND BRAVE TOGETHER (90 minutes)

**Aim.** Introduce confession-and-restoration as a community practice from James 5:16. Each participant identifies one place where they want to walk in greater honesty and receives a spoken blessing of restoration. We are not probing for sins; we are practicing the architecture of confession.

**Anchor scripture.** James 5:16 and 1 John 1:9.

**Connect focus.** Others, deepening. Week 5 named the four conditions; tonight, one specific condition (Safe) gets exercised in a way most Christians have never practiced.

**Mode.** Shared opening and teaching; SPLIT into family clusters for the practice; MERGE for blessings, the Leader Feedback Round, and closing.

**Circles.** Family clusters — two or three whole families with a Cluster Companion; your own family is always with you, never divided. If the room is about ten people or fewer, skip the split: the whole room stays as one circle and everything below still applies.

**Parents first.** In every cluster the parents confess first — the Cluster Companion models, then the arranged parent opens, then the other parents, then the teens. Every parent has been pre-briefed (Handbook Section 2): own struggles, yes; the marriage’s raw material, no; anything about the teen, never without asking the teen privately first. Teens choose their own depth; pass-anytime holds.

**Center.** Age-appropriate confession-and-restoration practice. Each participant names one place where they want to walk in greater honesty (NOT specific sins; the place, the pattern, the pull). The circle speaks a brief blessing of restoration. The Spirit does the work; we hold the space.

**Between-session practice.** The Five-Minute Examen each evening — carried through the quarter break that follows tonight. Plus: the Quarterly Pulse (H6.3) goes home tonight, the first of the year's three — one page, five minutes, back in the bin at the Week 7 door.

**IJH source.** Vol 2 Second Exploration (Emotional Knots, including the Sin Knot type) and Vol 2 Fourth Exploration (Confession and Restoration); Vol 2 Eighth Exploration on container; Vol 2 Ninth Exploration on community amplification.

## WATCH FOR (Week 6 specific risks)

- Confession misunderstood as detailed disclosure. The practice is naming where I want to walk in greater honesty, NOT confessing specific sins to the circle. Frame this three times before the split.
- Catholic-style confession associations. For some teens, this will feel foreign or theologically charged. Frame it as Protestant practice grounded in James 5:16, not as sacramental.
- The teen who has nothing to confess. Honor it. Reframe: “Where do you want to walk in greater honesty?” Almost everyone has a place.
- The over-discloser. The participant who turns the practice into specific sin disclosure. Cluster Companion gently redirects: “Thank you. The practice is the place, not the details.”
- Parents naming material their teen should not carry. The teen is in the circle this year — the parent pre-brief (Handbook Section 2) is the guard: own struggles, yes; the marriage’s raw material, no; anything about the teen, never without asking the teen privately first. Anything bigger goes to the pastoral 1:1, not the cluster.
- Teens receiving weight from a parent’s confession. The Cluster Companion watches the teens’ faces during the parents’ turns. Weight received is a Settle Protocol matter (Handbook §6).
- Performative confession — the spiritualized version designed to look humble. “I struggle with pride” as an answer to everything. Push gently for one specific concrete pattern.

## CRISIS CONTINGENCIES (Week 6)

**Week 6 can surface confession of patterns that warrant follow-up. Standard Section 6 protocols apply.**

**If a teen confesses to self-harm or suicidal ideation.** Section 6 applies in full. Two-adult rule, immediate after-session check-in, parent notification, pastoral/clinical backup.

**If a teen confesses to substance abuse, eating disorder behavior, or pornography use, that warrants intervention.** Honor in the room with brief acknowledgment and blessing. Follow up offline within 48 hours with parent and qualified care. Do NOT process in the room.

**If a parent confesses something that significantly affects family dynamics (affair, addiction, financial concealment).** Do not interrogate. Pastoral 1:1 within 48 hours. The room is not a confessional in the legal sense, but the Lead Companion may have mandatory-reporting obligations depending on what is disclosed — review in advance. The parent pre-brief exists precisely so this material never lands in front of the teen; if it lands anyway, the Cluster Companion receives it minimally, does not process it in the cluster, and moves the circle gently on. The teens who heard it are checked on before they leave.

**If a teen names abuse they have experienced.** Section 6 mandatory reporting protocol applies regardless of the framing of the disclosure.

**If a participant freezes or breaks down.** Hold the silence. Bless gently. Do not push. Some confessions are tears with no words; that is enough.

**If a practice overwhelms someone.** Run the Settle Protocol (Handbook §6): stop with plain directive words, bring them back to the room, breathe and anchor, do not resume the practice with them tonight, and nobody leaves activated — a Companion stays until their body has settled, and settling that doesn’t come is a same-evening call to the backup. A teen visibly carrying the weight of a parent’s confession counts: weight received is a Settle Protocol matter.

# Session at a Glance

## Why this session, this week

This is the architectural keystone of the Others' arc. Weeks 5 and 6 together teach the room what genuine community is for. Week 5 named the four conditions; Week 6 puts one of them (Safe) into operational load.

Confession is the most underused practice in evangelical formation. Most Christians know James 5:16 — “confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed” — and almost none of us actually do it. Tonight, we do an age-appropriate version of it in family clusters, parents first.

The frame is critical. We are not asking teens or parents to disclose specific sins. We are asking each person to name one place — one pattern, one pull, one area — where they want to walk in greater honesty before God. The circle speaks a brief, specific blessing of restoration. The Spirit does the work.

And the order inside the cluster is itself the teaching. A teen who watches their own parent name a real place, plainly and without excuses, learns more about confession in ninety seconds than in any number of lessons about it. The parents go first — pre-briefed, within rails — and the teens then choose their own depth.

## Theological framing

The session sits squarely in mainstream evangelical practice. James 5:16 is the operational verse; 1 John 1:9 is the assurance verse. We are not introducing Catholic confession, sacramental absolution, or anything contested at CCA. We are introducing the simple, scriptural practice of being honest about our patterns in front of trusted people who pray for us. And the assurance of 1 John 1:9 rests on something prior — the cross: God is “faithful and just to forgive” because “the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin” (1 John 1:7). Forgiveness is something Christ already secured, not something our confessing earns.

The IJH framework calls this addressing the Sin Knot — the type of blockage that responds specifically to confession in community (Vol 2 Exp 6). What we hold back is the deeper TPM-style processing that is not appropriate for this audience. What we lean into is the simple practice that scripture commands.

# Pre-Work for the Companion Team

## Personal pre-work

Every Companion does the practice on themselves before this week. Identify the one place where you want to walk in greater honesty before God right now. Not in general. Specifically. Write it down. Rehearse saying it in one sentence.

Two reasons. First, the cluster needs the demo. The Cluster Companion goes first in the circle; if the demo is honest, the room follows. Second, you cannot lead this practice from a place where you have not done it. You will know the difference.

## Pre-briefing the parents

This is the load-bearing pre-work of Week 6, and it happens in the week before the session, not the night of. The Lead Companion (or each Cluster Companion for their own families) has a brief, warm conversation with every parent — the parent pre-brief of Handbook Section 2:

1. **Parents go first.** In your cluster, after the Companion’s demo, the parents confess before the teens — and one parent in each cluster is asked, now, to open. Your teen will watch you do the thing before they are asked to do it. That is the gift, and it is the design.
2. **The rails.** Your own struggles: yes — a real place, named plainly, is exactly right. The marriage’s raw material: no — grievances, its private history, anything your spouse would need to consent to, stays out of the cluster. Anything about your teen: never without asking the teen privately first — and never as your confession.
3. **The escape valve.** If the truest place you carry is bigger than a family cluster can hold — an affair, an addiction, a concealment — do not bring it to the circle with your teen present. Bring it to the pastoral 1:1, before or after Wednesday. Name a smaller true place in the cluster; there is always one. The team’s job is to walk you toward freedom, not to manage your secret.

## Team pre-work

The Thursday Call's look-ahead before Week 6 covers three specific things:

1. Re-read Handbook §6 (mandatory reporting and crisis protocols). Out loud, together. Same as before Weeks 3–4. Some confession material can rise to crisis.
2. Each Cluster Companion names — to the team only — the place they will name in their demo. The team prays over each other’s confession before the room does. This is itself the practice the room will do tonight.
3. Confirm every parent has been pre-briefed and every cluster has its arranged opening parent. A cluster where the pre-brief did not happen runs the practice with the Companion carrying more of the modeling weight — and the Lead Companion knows before the doors open.
4. Confirm pastoral / clinical backup availability for the night and 48 hours after.

## Block 3 — James 5:16 and 1 John 1:9 — Confession as Architecture (7:12–7:25, 13 min)

Read both passages aloud, slowly, from the physical Bible.

*“Therefore, confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous person has great power as it is working.”*

— James 5:16 (ESV)

*“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”*

— 1 John 1:9 (ESV)

### Teaching points (use any format — do not read this verbatim)

• Two verses, two angles. 1 John 1:9 is vertical: confession to God, by ourselves, with full assurance of forgiveness. James 5:16 is horizontal: confession to one another, in community, for healing. Most Christians live in 1 John 1:9 and never enter James 5:16. Tonight we enter James 5:16 carefully and at age.

• Why both? Because patterns we hide from each other tend to keep their grip on us, even when we have confessed them to God. Sin lives in darkness; bringing it into the light, in front of trusted people, is part of what breaks its grip. The IJH framework calls these Sin Knots — patterns that respond specifically to confession in community.

• What we are not doing tonight: We are not doing detailed sin disclosure. We are not making each other priests. We are not asking anyone to expose anyone else. The circle is not a confessional in the Catholic sacramental sense; it is a Protestant small group practicing what James 5:16 actually says.

• What we are doing tonight: Each person names ONE place where they want to walk in greater honesty before God. Not the sin in detail — the place, the pattern, the area. Examples: ‘the place where I keep choosing fear over trust’; ‘the way I treat my brother’; ‘the thing I do at night that I know I shouldn’t’; ‘the part of my faith I have been performing’; ‘the resentment I keep feeding.’ The naming is the practice.

• The parents go first in every cluster. Say why, plainly: the older generation owes the younger the demonstration. A teen should never be asked to be braver than their parents were willing to be, three minutes earlier, in the same circle.

• After each person names their place, the circle speaks a specific blessing of restoration. Not a general prayer. A specific, short, witnessed blessing. We will walk through the form before we split.

• The whole thing takes about three minutes per person. There will be silences. Tears are normal and welcome. Specifics about the sin itself are not requested and not required. The naming is enough.

## Block 4 — Companion Demo (7:25–7:30, 5 min)

The Lead Companion now does the practice in front of the whole room. This sets the depth and the form for the rest of the night.

## How to do this well

- Stand. Make eye contact across the room.
- Name one place specifically: “The place where I want to walk in greater honesty is \_\_\_\_\_.” Be real but not graphic. “The way I respond to my wife when I am anxious” is real. “The full list of every time I have failed her” is too much.
- Pause. Let it land.
- A pre-arranged Co-Companion (the Parent or Teen Co-Comp) speaks a brief blessing back. “John, in the name of Jesus, we bless this place where you are walking. We pray restoration over the way you respond when anxious. May the Father’s peace meet you there. May you walk in greater honesty this week.”
- Receive it. Do not deflect, do not joke, do not minimize. Say “Thank you” and sit down.
- Close: “That is the practice. You will do it in your cluster — parents first, then teens. Yours doesn’t have to look like mine. Three minutes per person.”

## Script

*“One. Take what helps from the prompts. You don’t have to use any of them. The prompts are scaffolding for the place where you might want to walk in greater honesty.”*

## Block 6 — Confession-and-Restoration in Family Clusters (7:32–8:05, 33 min)

This is the heart of the night. Each cluster runs the same structure. The order — Companion, then parents, then teens — is not etiquette; it is the safety architecture. The parents were pre-briefed this week (Handbook Section 2) and know their rails.

## Inside the cluster

**Cluster Companion opens (1 min). “What is said in this circle stays in this circle. We have ninety seconds of silence to settle in. Then I’ll go first, then the parents, then the teens — and your depth is yours.” Silence. The silence is part of the practice.**

**Cluster Companion goes first (3 min). Names one place. Receives a blessing from one circle member (assigned in advance — talk through with the team; a parent is the natural choice).**

**The parents next (~3 min each). The arranged parent opens; the other parents follow. Within the pre-briefed rails: their own struggle, named plainly; nothing from the marriage’s raw material; nothing about their teen. After each parent names their place, a blessing — anyone in the circle who feels led, and a teen blessing their own parent is one of the best things this night can produce. While the parents confess, the Companion watches the teens’ faces, not the confessing parent — weight received is a Settle Protocol matter (Handbook §6).**

**The teens (~3 min each). Each teen names one place at whatever depth they choose. Pass is real and honored without a flicker. After each, the next person clockwise (or anyone who feels led) speaks a brief blessing of restoration. The Cluster Companion may add a sentence if helpful, but the practice is for the circle to bless.**

**Closing silence (1–2 min). The circle holds silence. Cluster Companion: “We have named real things tonight. Let’s sit with what was said for a minute before we go back to the room.”**

*Timing note: a two-family cluster (about six people) runs comfortably at three minutes per person. A three-family cluster needs the compressed pace — closer to two and a half minutes each — from the start, not discovered at 7:55.*

## Cluster Companion: when to intervene

- If a participant starts giving detailed sin disclosure — gentle, quiet: “That’s real, and we honor your trust. The practice is the place, not the details. What is the one place you want to walk in greater honesty?” Pause. Let them re-frame.
- If a parent drifts past the pre-brief rails — toward the marriage’s raw material, or toward something about their teen — redirect in the same gentle register: “Let’s keep tonight to the place that’s yours alone. The rest belongs to the 1:1.” The rails were agreed in advance; the redirect is a reminder, not a rebuke.
- If a teen is visibly carrying the weight of a parent’s confession — the frozen face, the stare at the floor, the shoulders that climbed — that is a Settle Protocol matter (Handbook §6). The Companion moves toward the teen at the first natural seam, and the check-in happens before anyone leaves the building.
- If a participant freezes — wait fifteen seconds. “Take your time. There’s no rush.” If still stuck: “Would you like to come back at the end?”
- If a participant gives a generic answer (“I struggle with pride”) — ask one specific question. “Where does that show up most this week?” Often the real answer is one layer below the spiritual cliche.
- If a participant breaks down — hold the silence. Reach out a hand if welcomed. Bless gently. Move on when they nod.
- If something rises to crisis level (suicidal ideation, abuse, self-harm, substance abuse warranting intervention) — receive in the room with brief acknowledgment and blessing. After the cluster closes, you and one other Companion step aside with the participant. Section 6 protocols apply.
- If you run out of time — do not skip anyone. Compress to two minutes per person if needed. Better to be three minutes late than to send a participant home un-named.

# Inside the Clusters: Parents First, Teens at Their Own Depth

One band of teens in the pilot, confessing in the same circle as their parents — after them. The notes below replace the old age-circle differentiation.

## The teens

The practice is fully accessible to high-school teens, and they can hold it at real depth. The challenge is that some carry specific patterns (substance use, sexual behavior, eating issues, performance anxiety, self-harm) that warrant pastoral follow-up rather than circle-level processing.

- Handout uses honest, concrete language — patterns, pulls, places where formation has stalled. Concrete beats abstract at every age: “the way I treat my sister when I’m tired” models the form better than any category.
- Watch for confessions that need follow-up: substance use, sexual behavior outside what the family’s framework supports, self-harm, eating disorder behavior. Receive in the room with brief acknowledgment and blessing. Follow up offline within 48 hours.
- If a teen names a struggle their parent is hearing for the first time — and the parent is sitting right there — the Companion holds the moment: the blessing happens in the circle; the processing does not. The parent’s job is to receive without visible reaction; that is in the parent pre-brief. The Companion checks both teen and parent at the door, and works with the Lead Companion on appropriate next steps based on the safety threshold.
- Watch for the teen who treats it as a game, or who confesses to fit in, to seem deep, or to impress a peer. Gentle redirect: “Take a breath. Pick something real. Even a small real thing.” The real thing is better than the impressive thing.
- Watch for the teen shrinking to nothing because their parent is three feet away. The pass is real — but note it for the debrief and follow up warmly this week.

## The parents

Parents have the most to lose tonight. Adults have practiced hiding for longer. And this year they go first with their own teen watching — which is exactly the point, and exactly why the pre-brief matters.

- Handout is adult: pattern, pull, area where formation has stalled.
- Frame at the start of the cluster, warmly: “Parents, your teen is about to watch you do the thing before they’re asked to do it. The most generous thing you can do for them tonight is to be honest — within the rails we talked about this week.”
- The rails, one more time: own struggles, yes; the marriage’s raw material, no; anything about the teen, never without having asked the teen privately first — and never as your confession. A parent naming their own response pattern (“the way I respond to your mother when I’m anxious”) is inside the rails; the marriage’s grievances and history are not.
- Watch for the parent who carries something significant about the family system (affair, addiction, financial concealment). The pre-brief routed this to the pastoral 1:1; if it surfaces in the cluster anyway, receive with brief acknowledgment, do not process, and follow up within 48 hours. Do not revisit it in front of the room without the parent’s consent and explicit framing — and check the teens who heard it before they leave.
- Watch for the parent who ‘confesses’ their teen’s behavior. Redirect: “Tonight is for our own places. Your teen is doing their own work — right here, in a minute.”
- If a parent breaks down — normal and welcome. Hold space. Bless gently. Do not rush. Their teen watching a parent weep over a real place, and watching the circle bless it, is formation nothing else in this series can buy.

## Categories of “place”

**Patterns I keep choosing even though I know they’re costing me.**

- A behavior I do alone that I would not do in front of anyone I respect.
- A relationship where I keep repeating something I know is wrong.
- An anxiety or fear that has become controlling.
- The performance version of my faith I have been giving.
- A doubt or anger I have been hiding rather than bringing to God.
- A way I treat someone (parent, sibling, classmate, teammate, stranger) that does not match who I want to be.
- A pull I keep giving in to — social media, comparison, food, attention, substances, sexual content.

## Specificity is the door

*“I struggle with anxiety” is general. “The way I check Instagram first thing every morning to feel okay about myself before I read scripture” is specific.*

*“I have pride issues” is general. “The way I cut down classmates in my head to feel better about my own work” is specific.*

*Specific lets the Spirit work. General keeps the pattern hidden.*

## The form

*One sentence:*

**“The place where I want to walk in greater honesty before God is \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.”**

*Pause. The circle will bless you. You do not respond, do not minimize, do not joke. Eye contact. “Thank you.” That is the practice.*

## If something heavier surfaces

**Handout H6.2 — Blessing of Restoration**

*How to bless a brother or sister after they have named their place*

*After someone in your cluster names their place, the circle (or one assigned member) speaks a brief blessing of restoration. Specific. Short. Witnessed. Not a sermon, not advice, not a prayer that becomes a teaching.*

### The form

## Three-sentence template

**Sentence 1: Address the person and name in the name of Jesus. “[Name], in the name of Jesus, we pray restoration over the place you just named.”**

**Sentence 2: Specific to what they named. “We pray over [the place — the way you respond to your son when you are tired / the pull you yielded to last week / the doubt you have been hiding].”**

**Sentence 3: A specific blessing for the week. “May the Father meet you there this week. May you walk in greater honesty by Sunday than you walked in tonight.”**

### Examples

*“Alex, in the name of Jesus, we pray restoration over the way you cut down your sister when you’re tired. We bless that specific moment when she walks into your room and you have a choice. May the Father give you the half-second of grace this week. May Wednesday’s exchange be different than last Wednesday’s.”*

*“Maria, in the name of Jesus, we pray restoration over the morning Instagram check before scripture. We pray over that ten seconds when your phone is in your hand and your Bible is on the desk. May the Holy Spirit be a steadier presence than the feed this week. May you find Him there.”*

*“Dad, in the name of Jesus, we pray restoration over the resentment you have been feeding. We pray over the conversation you have been replaying in your head for years. May the Father walk into that conversation with you this week and bring a kind of peace you have stopped expecting. We bless your willingness to name it tonight.”*

### What not to say

- Do not advise. “You should try \_\_\_\_\_” is not a blessing.
- Do not theologize. “You know, James 5:16 says...” — the practice IS James 5:16. We are doing it, not preaching it.
- Do not minimize. “Oh, that’s really not so bad” dismisses what they just trusted you with.
- Do not compare. “Me too” — not in this practice. Save it for after.
- Do not preach. The blessing is three sentences. If you are in your fourth, stop.
