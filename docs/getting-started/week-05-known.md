## WEEK 5 — KNOWING AND BEING KNOWN (90 minutes)

**Aim.** Move from Self into Others as the dominant Connect. The container we have been building is the teacher — we now examine what makes this room different from the rest of life and where each of those conditions shows up (or doesn’t) in our actual friendships.

**Anchor scripture.** Ecclesiastes 4:9–12 — “Two are better than one... a threefold cord is not quickly broken.”

**Connect focus.** Others (primary). The four container conditions — Safe, Present, Clear, Intentional — are the spine.

**Mode.** Shared teaching; SPLIT into family clusters for the friendship-mapping exercise; MERGE for closing and the Leader Feedback Round.

**Circles.** Family clusters — two or three whole families with a Cluster Companion; your own family is always with you, never divided. If the room is about ten people or fewer, skip the split: the whole room stays as one circle and everything below still applies.

**Parents first.** On every sharing round, the Cluster Companion models first and then a parent opens — arranged with that parent before the session, never a cold call on a teen. Teens choose their own depth after; pass-anytime holds.

**Center.** Each cluster works through the four conditions and asks: where does each one show up in my actual friendships, and where is it missing? The discussion itself is the formation.

**Between-session practice.** Pick one friendship in your life. This week, practice one of the four conditions in that friendship more intentionally than usual. Journal what happens.

**IJH source.** Vol 2 Eighth Exploration (the four container conditions); Vol 2 Ninth Exploration (community as amplifier); Vol 1 First Exploration (the Word→Hearing→Faith chain) as the backbone.

## WATCH FOR (Week 5 specific risks)

- Drifting into general friendship advice. The conditions are scriptural and specific. Resist. “Here are some tips for being a good friend.” Stay with Safe, Present, Clear, Intentional as Vol 2 defines them.
- Teens who quietly admit they don’t have a friendship that has any of the four conditions. This is real data, not a problem. Receive it. Do not rescue. The recognition is the first step.
- Parents who lecture about friendship choices. In a family cluster the temptation doubles — the teen is right there. Parents are mapping their own adult friendships, not coaching their kids across the circle.
- Teens who name a specific friendship as missing all four conditions and are sitting near that person in the room — in a family cluster, that person may be their own parent. Use anonymizing language: “one of my friendships,” “one adult relationship in my life,” not names. And nobody asks whether it’s them.
- Drift into nostalgia. “Kids today don’t have real friendships as we did.” Honor the observation; redirect to the conditions in front of us.
- The temptation to teach all four conditions in deep detail. Teach lightly; let the friendship-map exercise do the depth work.

## CRISIS CONTINGENCIES (Week 5)

*Week 5 is unlikely to surface an acute crisis. The risk profile is closer to Week 2 than Week 3. But “I don’t have a single friend who knows me” from a teen is its own quiet emergency — receive it, follow up offline within 48 hours, and watch them in Weeks 6–22. Default to Section 6 of the Handbook for any disclosure that crosses the safety threshold.*

# Session at a Glance

## Why this session, this week

Week 1 named the four Connects. Weeks 2 through 4 were self-work — Heart Soil and Telling Your Story across both runnings, parents first and then the teens. Week 5 begins the explicit Connect-with-Others sequence.

The room has now been in a container together for four weeks. Most of them have never been in anything like it. Tonight, we name what has been holding the room — and we name it specifically, in scriptural language, so that what they have experienced has vocabulary they can carry into other relationships.

The four conditions — Safe, Present, Clear, Intentional — are the practical implementation of the Vol 1 hearing-channel law in a group context. Each removes a specific class of interference. They are not advice; they are the conditions under which a real circle can hold real work. Tonight we make them visible.

# Pre-Work for the Companion Team (this week)

## Personal pre-work

This week, you map your own friendships. Sit with each of the four conditions and ask: in which of my current friendships is each of these condition genuinely present, and in which is it missing? Be specific. Name names privately.

Then ask the harder question: which of the four conditions am I, personally, the weakest at offering to my friends? Not which do I lack from them. Which do I withhold? That is the question your participants will be asked tonight.

## Block 3 — The Four Conditions: Teaching (7:15–7:30, 15 min)

Stand at the whiteboard. The four conditions are pre-written. Read the scripture, then walk through each condition for two to three minutes. Lightly — the cluster exercise will do the depth work.

## Script (the read)

*“Our scripture tonight is Ecclesiastes 4. Listen.”*

*“Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their toil. For if they fall, one will lift up his fellow. But woe to him who is alone when he falls and has not another to lift him up! Again, if two lie together, they keep warm, but how can one keep warm alone? And though a man might prevail against one who is alone, two will withstand him—a threefold cord is not quickly broken.”*

— Ecclesiastes 4:9–12 (ESV)

### Teaching points (use any format — do not read this verbatim)

• Solomon names something most of us already feel: there are kinds of work, kinds of warmth, and kinds of resilience that are simply not available to a person who is alone. The threefold cord is not stronger because three strands are sturdier than one; it is stronger because they hold each other in tension. The strength is in the holding.

• The threefold cord doesn’t happen automatically. Most friendships do not produce that strength. Most are still useful and good — but they are not the kind of friendship Solomon is describing. The difference is not who the friends are; it is whether certain conditions are present.

• In Volume 2 of the IJH project, we name four conditions that have to be in place for a friendship, or any small group, to actually hold the kind of work scripture describes. Each one removes a specific kind of interference. We have been quietly practicing all four for four weeks in this room. Tonight we name them.

## The four conditions — walk these on the whiteboard

**SAFE — the hearing condition.**

*“Safe doesn’t mean comfortable. It doesn’t mean nobody disagrees with you. It means: I will not be judged as a person while I am doing my work. What I reveal here will not be used against me, and it will not be published outside this room. (Romans 14:3–4.) When I am managing the risk of exposure, I cannot be honest. The two postures cancel each other out.”*

**PRESENT — the attention condition.**

*“Present means: I am actually here. Not in my head about the day I just had, not in my phone, not rehearsing what I am going to say next. Here. Aware that the Great I AM — the God who introduces Himself in the present tense — is also here. (Revelation 1:8.) Most rooms in our lives, we are physically there but not really present. The container is the practice of being actually present.”*

**CLEAR — the channel condition.**

*“Clear means: there is nothing standing between God and me right now, and nothing standing between the people and me in this circle that I have not addressed. If I am carrying an unacknowledged judgment of someone in this room, my hearing is degraded. The log in my own eye blocks my view. (Matthew 7:5.) Clearing isn’t fixing every conflict before we start; it is acknowledging what is there, naming it briefly, and setting it aside so it doesn’t occupy our attention while we work.”*

**INTENTIONAL — the obedience condition.**

*“Intentional means: I came in here on purpose, watching for what God is doing, willing to do whatever He shows me. Jesus modeled this when He said the Son does only what He sees the Father doing. (John 5:19.) The opposite is coming in with a list of things I will not consider. A friendship where one or both people have non-negotiable lines on what they will hear from each other isn’t the kind of friendship Solomon is describing.”*

## Block 5 — Friendship-Mapping in Family Clusters (7:32–8:00, 28 min)

Each cluster runs in parallel. The structure inside each cluster is identical. The Cluster Companion goes first on each condition, briefly, to model; the arranged parent opens the round after them.

### Inside the cluster — Companion script

## Opening (90 seconds)

*“We have twenty-eight minutes. Four conditions. About six minutes per condition, with a couple minutes of breathing room. I’ll go first on each one to model, and then [arranged parent’s name] will open the round. Then we go around. Pass anytime. The exercise is on your worksheet — don’t worry about answering perfectly. Notice what surfaces.”*

*“Same container rules. What’s said in this circle stays in this circle. No names of people who aren’t in this room — and for people who are, ‘one of my friendships’ or ‘one adult relationship in my life’ is the language. Nobody asks who you meant.”*

## How each condition runs (≈ 6 min each)

**Cluster Companion goes first. 30–60 seconds. Specific. Name a friendship (anonymously) where the condition is genuinely present, and one where it is missing. “One of my friendships has real Safety — I have said hard things and not been judged. Another friendship I have, I edit myself in. The Safety isn’t there.”**

**Then the arranged parent opens the round — 30–60 seconds, same shape. Then around the circle, each person 30–60 seconds, teens choosing their own depth. Pass is allowed. The Cluster Companion does not summarize or evaluate.**

**After all have gone, the Cluster Companion names one observation and moves on. “Several of you are noticing \_\_\_\_\_. Let’s move to the next condition.”**

### Cluster Companion: when to intervene

- If a participant names a person specifically by name — “my mom” or “my best friend Sarah” — gently: “We are using ‘one of my friendships’ language tonight. Try again with that frame.”
- If a teen maps a relationship that may belong to someone in the circle — their parent beside them, a sibling across it — hold the frame for everyone: the language stays anonymous, and nobody asks whether it’s them. Not tonight, and not in the car. The worksheet says the same.
- If a teen says, “I don’t have any friendships with any of these conditions,” receive it. Do not rescue. Mark it for the team debrief and follow up with the Lead Companion after the session.
- If a parent starts coaching their teen’s friendships — “this is why I keep telling you about those boys —” — redirect warmly: “Your friendships tonight, not theirs. The teens are doing their own mapping.”
- If the conversation drifts into general friendship complaints — “kids today don’t —” — redirect: “Come back to the condition. Where is it present in one specific friendship of yours?”
- Watch the time. Six minutes per condition. If you are at minute four with three people not yet spoken, gently compress: “Two minutes left on this one — quick rounds.”

### After all four conditions

If you finish before 8:00, ask one quiet final question: “Of the four conditions, which one is the one I find hardest to offer to my friends? Not lacking from them — hardest to offer.” Two minutes of silent reflection. No discussion. Then walk back to the main room together.

## Block 6 — Merge and Surface Common Patterns (8:00–8:10, 10 min)

## Script

*“Welcome back. Take a breath. Find your seat.”*

*“What happened in your cluster stays in your cluster. So I am not going to ask anyone to share what was said. I am going to ask each cluster — not naming people — what general patterns surfaced. ‘Many of us noticed Safety is the rarest condition in our friendships.’ Or, ‘A lot of us noticed we expect Presence from others but don’t offer it ourselves.’ General. Pattern-level.”*

*“Cluster Companions — each of you take ninety seconds to name one or two general patterns from your circle. Then I’ll step back into one observation, and we move on.”*

*(Each Cluster Companion names patterns. Lead Companion listens.)*

*“What I notice across the clusters is \_\_\_\_\_. (Speak truth, briefly. ‘Most of us in this room are quicker to name what is missing from our friendships than what we ourselves don’t offer.’ Or whatever the actual observation is.) That is real, and it is the seed of the practice this week.”*

## Block 7 — Between-Session Practice (8:10–8:20, 10 min)

## Script

*“Here is the practice for this week. Pick one friendship in your life. Just one. The friendship you pick is yours — you don’t have to tell anyone which one.”*

*“Pick one of the four conditions. The one you noticed is most missing in that friendship, or the one you find hardest to offer in that friendship. Doesn’t matter which.”*

*“This week, practice that one condition in that one friendship more intentionally than usual. Practically. If you picked Presence, that may mean: when I am with that friend this week, my phone is in another room. If you picked Safety, that may mean: I receive what they say without judging it, even quietly. If you picked Clear, that may mean: I name something I have been quietly carrying about that friendship and address it. If you picked Intentional, that may mean: I come into our next conversation watching for what God is doing in them, not just relating my week.”*

*“One friendship. One condition. Practice. Journal what happens.”*

*“Your Personal Heart Journal has Week 5 pages with prompts. The handout in your folder — H5.5 — has the practice in writing.”*

## Block 8 — The Leader Feedback Round (8:20–8:25, 5 min)

*The evening’s leader closes the working part of the evening the same way every week.*

## Script

Leader: *“Before we close — two things from me, same as every week. What I think went well tonight: ______. What I’d do differently next time: ______. Now the room — same two questions about tonight. What went well? What would you do differently?”*

*(The leader may drop this round if the room’s energy needs something else. Dropping it twice running goes to the team debrief.)*

# Inside the Clusters: Teens and Parents Together

One band of teens in the pilot, mapping alongside their parents. The notes below replace the old age-circle differentiation.

## The teens

- Friendship Map worksheet uses peer/relationship vocabulary (close friend, dating relationship, friendship that has changed, online friend). See Handout H5.3. “Friendship” may also honestly include a parent, sibling, grandparent, or youth leader — allow it, inside the anonymous language.
- Teens are often closer to noticing the absence of conditions than they get credit for. Honor that with specificity.
- Watch for the teen who is performing wisdom. “I think a lot of people don’t have real friendships these days.” Redirect: “You — not other people. Where is each condition in your friendships?”
- Some teens will name a friendship that ended badly. Honor it; do not press.
- The relationship a teen most needs to map may be with the parent sitting next to them. The anonymous frame — “one adult relationship in my life” — makes that possible. Hold the frame, and hold the second half of it too: nobody asks who was meant, in the circle or afterward.
- Watch for the teen with no real friendship in any condition. Receive without panic. Note for the Lead Companion.

## The parents

- Friendship Map worksheet uses adult vocabulary (close friend, marriage, work friendship, friendship across the formation arcs). See Handout H5.4.
- Parents will often name friendships that have changed shape — “the friendship I had ten years ago that we don’t have anymore.” Honor.
- The marriage counts as a friendship, and a parent may bless aloud what is present in it. What is missing in it is journal material, not cluster material — the teen is in the circle. The worksheet’s harder questions are marked private for exactly this reason.
- Watch for the parent who turns the exercise into a critique of their teen’s friendships. Redirect: “Your friendships, not theirs.”
- Parents who realize they don’t have a single adult friendship with all four conditions — this is common and not pathological. The naming is the gift. And it is its own quiet formation for a teen to watch a parent name it honestly.
- The Cluster Companion’s model turn sets the specificity for the whole circle. Abstract language is wasted; the cluster needs to hear what each condition sounds like in a real story.

# Companion Debrief Prompts

Companion team meets the day after Week 5 for a 30-minute debrief.

### Signs the session worked

- Each cluster worked through all four conditions in the time available.
- At least one participant in each cluster named the absence of a condition specifically rather than abstractly.
- The parents opened honestly after the Companion’s model turn, and the teens chose real depth rather than defaulting to the pass.
- The merge surfaced patterns that were specific and varied across clusters — not all the same.
- At least one participant left able to articulate which condition they personally find hardest to offer.

### Signs the session did not work as well as it could have

- The teaching block ran long and the cluster time ran short.
- Clusters drifted into general friendship advice rather than naming the four conditions.
- Participants used real names of people not in the room — or the anonymous frame frayed around someone who was.
- A parent coached their teen’s friendships across the circle rather than mapping their own.
- The merge felt summative and abstract — “yeah, we all have friendships,” no specificity.
- A participant left without speaking and without a check-in from a Companion.

### If the session did not work, what to adjust for Week 6

- If teaching ran long, the Lead Companion owns it. Week 6’s teaching block is even more time-sensitive.
- If clusters drifted, the Cluster Companions may need a refresher in the team pre-meeting for Week 6 on what “staying with the prompt” looks like.
- If real names surfaced, gently re-name the convention in Week 6’s opening.
- If a parent coached across the circle, fold it into the Week 6 parent pre-brief — Week 6 cannot carry that habit.

### People to follow up with this week

- Anyone who said, “I don’t have any friendships with any of these conditions.” Brief warm contact within 48 hours: “Thank you for telling us. Want to grab a coffee or a phone call this week?”
- Anyone who passed on every round in their cluster. Not because passing is bad — it isn’t — but because consistent passing is data. Soft check-in by the Cluster Companion.
- Any parent who realizes their marriage maps mostly to the absence of conditions. Pastoral 1:1 within the week if welcomed — offline, never in the cluster.

## This week’s practice — one friendship, one condition

**Pick one friendship. Just one. The friendship you pick is yours — you don’t have to tell anyone which one.**

**Pick one of the four conditions — Safe, Present, Clear, Intentional. The one most missing in that friendship, or the one you find hardest to offer in that friendship.**

**Practice that one condition in that one friendship more intentionally than usual this week.**

### What practice could look like

## If you picked SAFE

- When that friend says something hard or vulnerable this week, receive it without judging it. Even silently.
- Notice when you’re tempted to use what they’ve told you against them later — in a joke, in a comparison, in a pivot. Don’t.
- Let them hear from you that you’re glad they told you — specifically.

## If you picked PRESENT

- When you’re with that friend this week, your phone is in another room, or face-down on a table, or on do not disturb.
- Practice listening to what they’re saying instead of preparing what you will say next.
- Make eye contact. Notice when your mind drifts. Bring it back.

## If you picked CLEAR

- Name something this week that you have been quietly carrying about that friendship — a small judgment, an unspoken hurt, a misunderstanding.
- Say it briefly and gently. “Something I noticed myself carrying about us is \_\_\_\_\_. I wanted to bring it into the open.”
- Don’t demand they fix it. Acknowledged is enough.

## If you picked INTENTIONAL

- Before your next time with that friend, ask God: “What are you doing in their life right now? What can I notice and bless?”
- Come into the conversation watching, not performing.
- Ask one question this week that you would not normally ask. “What is hard for you right now that I might not know about?”
