## WEEK 18 — THE RETURN II (90 minutes)

**Aim.** Light re-entry after the spring break, and a rhythm check. Name out loud that the room knows how to do this now — the coming back, the honest reading, the beginning again — and let the cohort carry its own return. Then set the map for the final stretch: the year now begins to hand itself over.

**Scripture anchor.** Galatians 6:9 — “let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up.” Supported by Philippians 1:6 (He who began a good work will bring it to completion).

**Connect focus.** All four, in stride. The rhythm each participant built at Week 15 has now crossed its first real break; tonight reads that crossing briefly, celebrates it, and turns the room toward the sendings.

**Mode.** Whole room throughout — no clustering tonight; the one-sentence round runs the full circle. (With attendance of about ten or fewer, nothing changes; the room is already one circle.) The Leader Feedback Round runs after the closing container, just before the blessing — the teen who led answers first.

**Center.** Two movements. (1) A quick “what held” round — the whole room, one sentence each, in a celebratory register: the Rhythm Cards built at Week 15 have just been field-tested across a break, and the room reads the results together. (2) The final-stretch commissioning of the room — the map of what remains, and the frame that carries it: the year now begins to hand itself over.

**Who leads.** A Companion-in-Formation leads the opening container — by Week 18 the every-teen rotation is deep in its groove, and the open belongs to a teen tonight. The Lead Companion carries the rest of the evening.

**Between-session practice.** Continue the rhythm as built — no restart language needed this time; the returning rule covers whatever slipped. And a gentle, unaudited word to the parents: the Four Questions door (commissioned at Week 15, willing-only always) is still open for any parent who has not yet found their moment.

**IJH source.** Vol 2 (the four Connects as a sustained rhythm); the shared Rhythm Card (“On returning”); Galatians 6:9 as the long-walk verse the cohort already owns from Week 15.

## WATCH FOR (Week 18 specific risks)

- Over-programming the night. The biggest risk tonight is the team treating Return II like Return I. It is not. Week 12 rebuilt a room; Week 18 greets one that held its shape. If the Companions run tonight heavy, they teach the room that it still needs carrying — the opposite of the truth, and the opposite of the point.
- The rhythm that broke over the break. Most cards will have held in part; a few will have collapsed entirely. The one-sentence round’s celebratory register must have room for “mine mostly slipped” to be received as cheerfully as “mine held” — the returning rule, not a rescue, is the response. A Companion follows up 1:1 only if the face says more than the sentence did.
- The parent who hears the Four Questions reminder as an audit. The word is one sentence, invisible-by-default, and moves on. If any parent looks cornered by it, the Cluster Companion repeats the guards 1:1 before they leave: willing-only, not yet counts, nobody checks.
- The teen who hears the final-stretch map and feels the ending. For some teens this room has been the best hour of the week, and the map makes the ending real. Expect a quiet face or two during Block 4. That is not a problem to fix tonight — but it is a follow-up conversation worth having warmly this week, and it is exactly what the sendings are for.
- Anything heavier than the night’s weight. A light session is not a light watch. The break may have been hard in some house. The one-word check-in still gets read closely, and Section 6 of the Handbook governs anything that crosses the safety threshold, tonight as every night. The two-adult rule holds in every space.

# **Block-by-Block: Scripts and Notes**

## **Block 1 — Opening Container, Teen-Led (4:00–4:08, 8 min)**

The Companion-in-Formation runs the full Section 5 opening protocol from memory, at its normal pace — welcome; phones silenced and put away (a simple ask, no collection); stand in the circle; the one-word check-in; put out / bring in; the spoken commitment (“I am here. I am paying attention. I am willing to be moved.”); the opening prayer naming the Holy Spirit; sit. Nothing about the protocol changes because a teen holds it — that is the point, and the room knows it by now.

## Script (Companion-in-Formation — in their own words; this shape, not this script)

*“Welcome back. It’s good to see everybody. We’ll open the container the same way we always do — and notice, on your way in, that we all still know how.”*

*(Run the eight steps. Receive every check-in word. The adults read the words as closely as ever — a light night still gets a full listening.)*

### **Notes**

The Lead Companion sits where the teen can find their eyes and otherwise stays out of the way. The timing Companion starts at 4:00 and quietly notes the minute the room is truly present. Expect it to be fast tonight; that speed is the year’s work showing, and it goes in the log.

## **Block 2 — Not Growing Weary: Galatians 6:9 and Philippians 1:6 (4:08–4:17, 9 min)**

Read both aloud from the physical Bible. The teaching is brief — the room already owns the first verse from Week 15; tonight it gets its proof.

## Script (the read)

*“Two scriptures tonight, and you know the first one — it is the long-walk verse from the Rhythm week. Listen to it again, on the far side of a break:”*

*“And let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up.”*

— Galatians 6:9 (ESV)

*“And Paul again, to the Philippians:”*

*“And I am sure of this, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ.”*

— Philippians 1:6 (ESV)

### **Teaching points (use any format — do not read this verbatim)**

• When we read Galatians 6:9 at the Rhythm week, the break was still ahead of us, and the verse was a warning: the weariness is coming; build for it. Tonight the break is behind us, and the same verse is a receipt. You did not give up. You are here. Whatever your card looked like across those weeks — held, wobbled, slipped and returned — the chair you are sitting in is the “not giving up” the verse asks for.

• And Philippians 1:6 names why the not-giving-up is not white-knuckling: the work being completed in you is His. He began it; He finishes it. Our rhythm is not the engine — it is the showing-up that keeps us where the engine is running.

• One more thing, and then we read the room: this is the last re-entry of the year. Twice now this cohort has scattered and come back. The first time, in January, we rebuilt the room slowly, on purpose. Look how fast it came back tonight. That speed is not luck. That is what it looks like when the practices stop belonging to the room and start belonging to you.

## **Block 3 — “What Held” — the Whole Room (4:17–4:42, 25 min)**

No clusters tonight. The full circle, one sentence each, and the register is celebratory — this is a harvest reading, not an examination. The Rhythm Cards built by hand at Week 15 have just crossed their first real gap; the room reads the results together, out loud, quickly, with pleasure.

## Script

*“Now the reading, and this time we do it as one room, because you have earned the short version.”*

*“At the Rhythm week you each built a card in your own handwriting — the rhythm you said you would actually keep. The break just field-tested it. So: one sentence each, around the circle. What held from your card across the break — or what slipped and got begun again. One sentence. Holds and slips are both good news tonight, because both mean the card is real and you are reading it honestly. [Parent’s name], start us off.”*

*(The arranged parent gives the first sentence. Then straight around the circle — brisk, warm, no commentary between sentences beyond a nod or a smile. Pass-anytime holds, as always. Laughter is allowed and likely: the honest sentences are often funny.)*

*(When the circle completes:)* *“Listen to what this room just did. Everybody read their own rhythm out loud, in one pass, without anybody needing to be coaxed or rescued. In September that round would have taken an hour and a crowbar. The practices are yours now. That is the whole finding, and it is the best one of the year.”*

*“And Joshua’s question has sat beside every check-in this year — ‘What do these stones mean?’ You just answered it as one room, in one pass. Hold on to that, because the question is about to change hands: soon it will be yours to ask, and to answer for somebody else.”*

### **Notes**

- Keep it moving. Twenty-five minutes is generous for a one-sentence round even at full attendance; the spare minutes belong to the laughter and the closing observation, not to expansions. If a sentence starts becoming a story, receive it warmly and keep the circle turning.
- A Companion keeps the rough tally quietly: how many sentences reported any daily practice holding through the break. Numbers only; no names travel.
- If a sentence lands heavy — a slip that is clearly about a hard break, not a busy one — the nearest Companion notes it and follows up at the door. The round does not stop.

## **Block 4 — The Final Stretch: the Map (4:42–5:00, 18 min)**

The commissioning of the room. Whiteboard ready; write the five lines live, unhurried, saying each aloud. The frame sentence — the year now begins to hand itself over — is the teaching; the map is its evidence.

## Script

*“Now look ahead with me, because tonight opens the last quarter of the year, and I want you to see the whole finish from here.”*

*(Write on the board, saying each line:)*

*“Week 19 — our Companions-in-Formation teach the two mercy cards — the Signs Card and the Path Home Card — in your clusters. Not me. Them.”*

*“Week 20 — The Long Walk. The seniors teach the whole room. Your journals come with you — we walk back through the year in them — and we begin gathering the testimonies of what God has done in this room since September.”*

*“Week 21 — Sending and Blessing. Whole families, and a longer evening — two hours, 4:00 to 6:00 — because sending a family takes longer than closing a meeting.”*

*“Week 22 — the Commissioning of the Companions. The seniors this room has watched grow all year are commissioned in front of everybody who watched it happen.”*

*“Now hear the shape of that list. Week by week, the people up front stop being us and start being you. The teens teach the cards, then the teens teach the room, then the families are sent, then the new Companions are commissioned. Here is the sentence I want you to keep: the year now begins to hand itself over. That was always the plan. A club that ends with the leaders still holding everything has not finished its work — it has just stopped meeting. This one ends with the work in your hands, which is the only kind of ending worth building a year toward.”*

*“So your assignment for the final stretch is simple: come. Bring your journal. Bring your whole family to Week 21 — put the two hours on the calendar tonight. And when a teenager stands up to teach you something in the next two weeks, receive it the way you have received me — because that receiving is the last thing this room has to give them.”*

### **Notes**

- Watch the teens during the map — both kinds. The Companions-in-Formation are hearing their own commissioning named; a specific word of honor at the door tonight lands well. And the teen for whom the ending lands sad gets warmth this week, not correction tonight: the map is doing its work.
- The two-hour Week 21 notice matters practically — say it plainly, say it once, and have the date visible on the board. Families plan around what they hear twice; it will be repeated at Weeks 19 and 20.

## **Block 5 — Between-Session Practice (5:00–5:07, 7 min)**

## Script (Co-Companion, Parent)

*“The practice this week is the one already in your handwriting: run your Rhythm Card as built. Nothing new, nothing extra. If the break knocked a piece loose, you know the rule — begin again on the next available day. The evening note this week has one extra flavor worth trying: with the map on the board, note where you see God already handing pieces of this year over to you.”*

*“And one sentence for the parents, and then we are done. At the Rhythm week you were handed four questions to ask at home — willing-only, whenever the moment is right. Some of you have asked them; some of you are still waiting for your moment. Both are exactly right. Nobody will ever check, tonight or any night — I say it once a quarter so you know the door is still open, and now I have said it. The questions keep.”*

### **Notes**

The Four Questions word is one paragraph, spoken to the middle distance, moved past without a pause for reaction. It is a door left ajar, not a knock. If any parent seeks a Companion out afterward — either “I did it” or “I still can’t” — both are the word working; receive, encourage, audit nothing.

## **Block 6 — Closing Container (5:07–5:15, 8 min)**

The Companion-in-Formation runs the Section 5 closing protocol, steps 1–5, at its usual pace: stand again; the one-word landing (compare to the opening word — the contrast is data); the one thing each person is taking from tonight; the one practice each will actually keep this week (the card, as built); and the blessings — short, specific, witnessed blessings of what someone saw the Father doing in another person tonight. Expect the blessings step to lean toward the seniors tonight; let it.

## **Block 7 — The Leader Feedback Round (5:15–5:24, 9 min)**

*The evening’s leader closes the working part of the evening the same way every week.* A teen led the open and close tonight, so the senior answers first, the adult second; the room’s feedback covers them both.

1. The Companion-in-Formation: *“Before we close — three things from me, same as every week. What I think went well tonight: ______. What I’d do differently next time: ______. If I were to teach tonight’s process to someone, I would tell them: ______.”*
2. The Lead Companion answers the first two questions, briefly, for the blocks they carried.
3. Then the room — same two questions about tonight. What went well? What would you do differently? Two or three voices on each is plenty. The leaders receive without defending — “thank you” is the whole response.

*(The leader may drop this round if the room’s energy needs something else. Dropping it twice running goes to the team debrief.)*

## **The three break experiments (recorded tonight — third and final in the year’s series)**

Same three readings as the prior re-entries, same method, recorded the same night per Handbook §8. No participant is named in any count. This is the final data point in the year’s series, so tonight’s entry also gets one comparative line per measure against the earlier readings.

1. **Rhythm survival count.** How many participants reported any daily practice holding through the break — tallied quietly from the one-sentence round. Log the number and the room total, plus the comparison: is survival rising across the year’s breaks? By Week 18 it should be, and visibly — the Week 15 cards were built for exactly this.
2. **Path Home Card uses.** How many walked back tonight — the team’s best count of real drifts that returned to the room, assembled from observation, never from a hand-raise. Log it with the comparison across the series. A falling number here is not the card failing; paired with a rising survival count, it is the card succeeding.
3. **Time-to-settled-container.** Minutes from 4:00 until the room was truly present — timed discreetly, same method as before, tonight under a teen-led open. Log the number, the comparison, and one line on what the settling looked like. If the year has worked, this is the number that tells the story in one figure.

With all three points now in the log, the series goes into the Quarter 4 review as one of the pilot’s cleanest findings: what the formation holds when the container is not holding it, measured at every gap in the year.

## **If the session worked**

- The room settled fast under a teen-led open — and the timing Companion can put a number on it worth keeping.
- The one-sentence round moved briskly, laughed at least once, and received the slips as warmly as the holds. Nobody needed rescuing.
- The map landed as commissioning, not as farewell — the room left pointed forward, and the seniors left honored.
- The Four Questions word passed without a ripple in the room and produced at least one quiet doorway conversation. Either half alone is fine too.
- The evening felt lighter than Week 12 and lost nothing by it. That is the finding.

## **If the session did not work**

- The team over-carried the night — the open got shadowed, the round got managed, the room was treated like January. Name it plainly in the debrief; Weeks 19 and 20 hand the room to the teens, and the team’s hands must be off by then.
- The round’s register slipped into examination — sentences got longer, slips got explained, someone apologized. A one-line reset at Week 19’s open: “Readings, not verdicts — still true.”
- The map read as an ending and the room left heavy. The reframe belongs at Week 19’s open, briefly: the handover is the point, and the sendings are commissionings, not goodbyes.
- Any parent left looking audited by the Four Questions word. That is a 1:1 repair this week — the guards, warmly, one more time.
- Anyone whose sentence or face said the break was hard at home and who left without contact. The 24-hour warm-contact protocol per Section 6.

## **People to follow up with this week**

- Anyone who did not return tonight — warm contact within the week, the Path Home posture, no agenda.
- The teen the ending landed on. A warm word this week about what the sendings actually are.
- Any senior teaching at Weeks 19–20 — confirm their prep is on track and their nerves are honored; the next two weeks are theirs.
- Any rhythm that collapsed entirely over the break, where the face said more than the sentence — the returning rule and the Signs Card, 1:1, unhurried.
