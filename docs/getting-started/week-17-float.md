## WEEK 17 — THE FLOAT (90 minutes)

**Aim.** Close the quarter with nothing owed. Mode A (Absorb): if weather, an assembly conflict, or illness canceled an earlier evening this quarter, tonight runs that session’s plan, unchanged — the arc arrives at the break complete. Mode B (The Rhythm walk): if the quarter ran clean, tonight the whole room walks its actual Rhythm Cards together — holds celebrated, slips troubleshot without guilt, ideas borrowed shamelessly. Nothing new is taught in either mode.

**Scripture anchor.** Proverbs 16:9 — “The heart of man plans his way, but the LORD establishes his steps.” The float’s own verse: this evening exists because we planned a year knowing the LORD would establish the actual steps.

**Mode.** Dual, decided at the team prep meeting. Mode A: whatever the lifted session’s plan specifies — its clusters, its circles, its pairs. Mode B: whole room, one circle, all evening. Either mode: Pulse 3 returns collected in the bin at the door as families arrive; the Leader Feedback Round and the Aaronic blessing close the night; the break practice is chosen before anyone leaves.

**Center.** Mode A: the missed session’s own center, run from its own plan. Mode B: the live walk — each member of the room, parents too, leaders too (leaders first), briefly walks their actual card aloud: daily, weekly, monthly, as lived.

**Who leads.** Mode A: whoever the lifted plan names, with the standard student-leadership gate applying as always. Mode B: the Lead Companion hosts the walk and — this is the design — goes first with their own genuinely slipped card.

**Between-session practice.** The quarter break follows tonight. Everyone carries ONE practice through the break deliberately — chosen tonight, spoken in the close, written in the journal. The same move that carried the cohort through Christmas. The Return II (Week 18) will ask what held.

**IJH source.** Proverbs 16:9 and the held-plans posture; Vol 2 (the four Connects as sustained rhythm); the shared Rhythm Card, including its Returning Rule — tonight’s Mode B is that card walked in community.

## WATCH FOR (Week 17 specific risks)

- **Mode B drifting into performance.** The room’s oldest temptation with anything shareable: whose card is most impressive. The antidote is structural and it is non-negotiable — the leaders walk first, and the leaders walk slipped cards honestly. A Lead Companion who opens with “my morning question has held every day” has ended the evening before it starts. Open with the slip.
- **Troubleshooting sliding into guilt.** The walk celebrates holds and troubleshoots slips — but troubleshooting is engineering, not repentance. The frame to keep saying: a slip you noticed is the practice working. The Returning Rule (begin again on the next available day; streaks are not the goal, returning is) is quoted from the front at least once.
- **The family absent all quarter, reappearing tonight.** Welcome, no accounting. Nobody asks where they have been — not from the front, not at the circle, not in the parking lot. The seat was theirs the whole time; the welcome says so. A Companion makes warm contact this week so the break does not swallow them again.
- **Mode A run half-heartedly.** If tonight is a lifted session, it is that session — not a summary of it, not “the highlights.” Families were told which night this is; the plan runs whole, with its own prep honored (see the lift checklist). The one legitimate trim: the lifted plan’s closing yields to tonight’s quarter-close constants.
- **The Pulse bin treated as a checkpoint.** The bin sits by the door; nobody watches it, nobody is asked whether they dropped a page. Spares sit beside it for those who forgot, and a page next week is as welcome as a page tonight.
- **The break minimized from the front.** “See you in a couple weeks” is how practices die. The break is named plainly as the year’s next gap, and the one-practice choice is made with the same seriousness as any assignment of the year.

## CRISIS CONTINGENCIES (Week 17)

# **Session at a Glance**

## **Why this session, this week**

Every calendar meets the real year eventually. Snow falls on a Wednesday; the school claims the gym; a stomach bug empties half the room. A fifteen-session plan with no slack answers those surprises by amputating content or sprinting to catch up — and formation work sprints badly. So the CCA year builds the surprise into the schedule: one evening at the end of Quarter 3 whose whole job is to absorb whatever the year did. That is not a scheduling trick; it is Proverbs 16:9 practiced as calendar-making. We planned the way. The LORD established the steps. The float is where the plan bows to the steps without losing the arc.

And if the quarter ran clean — if the float arrives with nothing to absorb — the evening has a second job that is arguably better than its first. Week 15 put a rhythm in every participant’s handwriting; Week 16 asked for one sentence about it. Tonight, in Mode B, the room opens the cards all the way: every member walks their actual rhythm aloud, the leaders first and slipped-first, and the room does together what the Rhythm Card can only describe — celebrates holds, troubleshoots slips without guilt, and borrows each other’s ideas shamelessly. A teen hears how a father actually keeps a morning question alive during tax season. A parent steals a teen’s trick of taping the card inside a locker door. That trading floor is community amplification doing exactly what Vol 2 says it does.

## **The mode decision (the team’s call, made early)**

**The decision rule.** If one evening this quarter was lost — snow day, assembly conflict, illness — tonight runs that session’s plan, unchanged. If two were lost, the team chooses the one the arc needs most for the final quarter (as a rule: the session whose fruit Weeks 18–22 depend on), and the other’s essential content folds into Week 18’s opening block — named there explicitly, not smuggled. If none were lost, tonight is Mode B.

**When.** The call is made no later than the team prep meeting, 48 hours out. No game-day decisions — the modes prepare differently, and families deserve to know which evening they are walking into.

**The communication plan.** Families are told by the weekend before, through the normal channel: which mode, and — if Mode A — which session is running and anything that plan asks them to bring. One plain sentence of why: *“We lost [date] to [the snow/the assembly/the bug], so this Wednesday runs that evening whole.”*

**The lift checklist (Mode A).** Lifting a plan into the float slot is more than reprinting it:

1. Re-read the lifted plan’s Companion pre-work section — and actually do it, compressed if needed. If it requires personal pre-work (a practice run on yourself), the compressed week does not excuse it.
2. Re-read its materials list; print its handouts; stage its room setup.
3. Re-read its watch-fors and crisis contingencies aloud at the prep meeting — they travel with the plan.
4. Re-arrange its parents-first openings with the specific parents, fresh — the old arrangement was for a different night.
5. Confirm its leadership: if the lifted session is teen-led, the Companion-in-Formation re-rehearses per that plan’s spec.
6. Plan the seams: tonight’s constants (Pulse bin at the door; the Leader Feedback Round; the one-practice break commissioning; the Aaronic close) replace the lifted plan’s final blocks. Trim the lifted plan’s closing, never its center.
7. At the open, announce why tonight is that night — one sentence, unapologetic (script in Block A1).

## **Mode B — Block B2: Proverbs 16:9 — The Float’s Verse (7:08–7:16, 8 min)**

Read from the physical Bible. The teaching is short; tonight’s center is the walking, not the talking.

*“The heart of man plans his way, but the LORD establishes his steps.”*

— Proverbs 16:9 (ESV)

### **Teaching points (brief — use any format)**

• This is the verse this evening is named for. The team planned a year — twenty-two Wednesdays, on paper, last August. And the plan itself assumed the LORD would establish different steps than we drew: this very night sits in the calendar as a shock absorber, an evening whose job was to catch whatever the year threw. The year threw nothing this quarter. So tonight the shock absorber becomes a gift — a free evening with the family, on the house.

• The verse is also the truest thing anyone will say tonight about a Rhythm Card. Three weeks ago every person here planned a way — daily, weekly, monthly, in your own handwriting. Since then, the LORD established your actual steps, and for most of us the steps and the plan do not perfectly match. That is not the card failing. That is Proverbs 16:9 happening to your card. The question tonight is never “did you keep the plan?” It is “what did the steps actually look like — and what is worth adjusting, and what is worth celebrating, and what is worth stealing from the person next to you?”

## **Mode B — Block B3: The Leaders Walk First (7:16–7:30, 14 min)**

The whole evening stands on this block. The Lead Companion walks first, and walks a genuinely slipped card — chosen at personal pre-work — honestly, briefly, without self-flagellation and without rescue. Then two or three more Companions walk theirs. The room learns in these fourteen minutes whether tonight is an honesty exercise or a performance review, and it learns it from the leaders’ tone about their own slips.

## Script (Lead Companion — shape, not verbatim; the specifics must be yours)

*“I go first, and here is my actual card. Daily: the morning question, 6:40, kitchen table — that one has held, maybe five days in seven, and the two misses are the mornings I pick up the phone first. The evening journal note — I want to tell you it held. It slipped ten days ago and I noticed on day three. I ran the card’s own rule: don’t start over, just begin again on the next available day. So it is four days old again, and that is the honest state of it. Weekly: my hour with [name] has held — it moved to Thursdays, which turned out to matter. Monthly: my clearing time is on the calendar for the break and has not happened yet. That is my card. Not the card I would design; the card I am actually walking.”*

*(Then, to the team:)* *“[Co-Companion’s name], walk us yours.”*

## Notes

- The model is: specific, brief, slips named in the same tone as holds, the Returning Rule shown in action rather than preached.
- Watch the teens during the leaders’ walks. The moment a teen realizes the adults’ cards have slips in them is the moment their own walk becomes possible.
- Two to three minutes per walker. The Lead Companion times the team as strictly as the room — long leader walks teach the room to perform.

## **Mode B — Block B4: The Room Walks (7:30–8:05, 35 min)**

## Script

*“Now the room. Same shape you just watched: two or three minutes, your actual card — daily, weekly, monthly, as it really went. Holds and slips in the same voice. When someone names a hold, we get to be glad out loud. When someone names a slip, there is exactly one thing we never do with it, and that is make them feel bad — a slip you noticed is the practice working. If you want help with a slip, ask for it, and we will spend a minute on it together. If you do not, name it and keep walking. And everyone: listen like a thief. If you hear an idea you want — a time of day, a trick, a place to keep the card — take it. That is not cheating. That is what a fellowship is for.”*

*“Parents and teens both, everyone walks their own card — and pass-anytime holds tonight like every night. [Parent’s name], start us off?”*

*(The arranged parent opens — never a cold call on a teen. Around the circle. The Lead Companion hosts: glad about holds, matter-of-fact about slips, one-minute troubleshoots only when invited, and a steady hand on the clock.)*

### **Hosting the troubleshoots (when invited)**

- Keep them to a minute and keep them practical: the time of day, the trigger, the phone, the partner. Most slips are engineering problems wearing guilt costumes.
- Recruit the room: *“Anyone else solved this one?”* A teen’s fix offered to a parent is worth ten minutes of Companion advice.
- If a slip is not an engineering problem — if the card collapsed because the house is carrying something heavy — receive it as costly telling: bless it, do not troubleshoot it, follow up offline within 48 hours.
- Watch the quiet ones. A participant who walks a suspiciously perfect card, or passes with their eyes down, gets a warm word after, not a spotlight during.
- The teen whose card has been blank since Week 15: a spare build sheet, no shame, and the honest frame — *“then tonight is a fine night to start at zero; walk us the card you’d actually keep.”* A card rebuilt mid-walk, with the room’s borrowed ideas fresh in it, is often better than the original.
- Parents walk their own card, never their teen’s. *“And Emma has been doing so well with her evening note”* is love, but it is also surveillance — the teen walks their own card at their own depth, or passes. A parent’s honestly slipped card is the best teaching a teen gets tonight; that is why parents walk early in the round.

# **Between-Session Practice**

## Across the break

- ONE practice, chosen tonight and written in the journal — the practice, the time, the place. Carried deliberately, every day or every week of the break, whichever it is. This is the same move that carried the cohort across Christmas, and The Return II will open by asking what held.
- Everything else on the Rhythm Card is allowed to rest. That is sabbath, not failure — a card fully suspended on purpose beats a card half-kept in guilt.
- The Returning Rule travels: if the break knocks even the one practice down, begin again on the next available day. No starting over; no streak accounting.
- The break channel: the named Companion is reachable for anything the break surfaces, and every family leaves knowing who and how.

# **Team Debrief**

The Companion team debriefs within a day or two — thirty minutes, before the break scatters everyone. Open with the Three Vital Signs, in order, same as every week:

1. **Costly Telling.** Who told something costly tonight — a genuinely slipped card walked without varnish, a hard thing surfaced in a Mode A exercise — and what did the room do with it?
2. **Response to Load.** This quarter’s load WAS the calendar (or wasn’t): how did the room handle tonight being the float — the reframe, the lifted session, the walk? And where did load arrive in the room tonight, and what did we see in the first ten seconds?
3. **The Sought Stray.** Who was missing tonight — and, tonight’s special case, who reappeared after a long absence? The reappearing family gets warmth logged and a during-the-break contact named. The still-missing family gets a pursuer and a day, before the break, not after.

Then the specifics:

- Pulse 3: collect the bin, count the pages, read them this week. The Quarter 4 planning conversation starts from what these pages say — that was the promise made at Week 16, and the team keeps it visibly.
- The break-practice round: note (privately, per the Measurement Covenant — watch, never score) roughly what the room chose. The Return II’s opening block is built from this.
- Mode A only: how did the lifted session actually run — its own debrief prompts apply and are walked now, plus: what folds into Week 18’s opening (if a second session was missed), said in one named block, not smuggled?
- Mode B only: who walked a suspiciously perfect card; who passed with their eyes down; whose slip was a household in trouble wearing a scheduling costume. Names and 48-hour clocks.
- Break coverage: which Companion is reachable over the break, and does every family know it? The break channel is confirmed before this meeting ends.

## **If the session worked**

- **Either mode:** the quarter closed with nothing owed — no unfinished arc content, no unframed break. Every participant left with one named practice in their journal. The bin came back respectably full, unwatched.
- **Mode A:** the lifted session got its full evening — its center uncompressed, its watch-fors honored — and the room received it as the real thing, not a make-up.
- **Mode B:** the leaders’ slipped cards set the tone and the room followed — slips spoken in the same voice as holds; at least one teen’s idea stolen by an adult; at least one troubleshoot that actually solved something; laughter somewhere in the walk.
- The reappearing family, if there was one, left having been simply welcomed.

## **If the session did not work**

- **Mode B drifted into performance** — polished cards, rounded-up numbers, slips confessed with a wince. The team looks first at Block B3: almost always the leaders’ walks were too clean. Name it honestly in this debrief; The Return II gets the corrected model.
- **Troubleshooting turned into guilt** — someone left feeling like their card was a report card. Warm repair contact this week, with the Returning Rule in hand.
- **Mode A felt like a consolation prize** — rushed blocks, a summarized center. The lift checklist gets a hard review before this plan is ever lifted again.
- **The break practice was chosen limply** — vague answers, no journals opened. The Return II’s opening asks anyway; the team lowers no bar, but notes that commissioning-under-time-pressure needs a bigger block next year.
- A page-drop was watched, or a returned family was asked for an accounting. Quiet repair, and the rule re-said at the next team meeting.
