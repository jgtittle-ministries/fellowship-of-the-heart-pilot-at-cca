## WEEK 19 — SECOND RUNNING: BLOCK B (75 minutes)

**Aim.** Give every Companion-in-Formation two live small-room reps teaching the Signs Card and the Path Home Card — coached from inside the cluster, with one specific “keep” and one specific “adjust” after each rep — so that next Wednesday at The Long Walk they carry the same teaching to the whole room, steady, as their final rep before commissioning.

**Scripture anchor.** 2 Timothy 2:2 — *“and what you have heard from me in the presence of many witnesses entrust to faithful men, who will be able to teach others also.”* Four generations in one verse: Paul, Timothy, the faithful, and the others also. Tonight is the third handoff happening in real time.

**Mode.** Brief whole-room frame; then family clusters — two or three whole families with a Cluster Companion, own family always together — each cluster led by a Companion-in-Formation walking it through both cards. A mid-evening rotation moves each CiF one cluster over, so each teaches twice. Adults coach from inside the cluster with a light touch. Whole room as one circle when about ten or fewer (see the small-room variation). The Leader Feedback Round runs at the close with the CiFs first, together.

**Between-session practice.** Both cards go home tonight, one set per participant folder. Each family finds them this week and puts them somewhere findable — a drawer everyone knows, the inside of a cabinet door, the family Bible. The point of these two cards is that they gather dust in a known location, because the person who eventually needs them will not be in a room like this when they do.

**Watch for (Week 19 specific risks).**

- Probing. The teaching is scripted and low-disclosure by design: the CiFs teach the cards; they do not probe anyone’s current dryness. *“Take out the card”* — never *“which column are you in?”* Coach this hard at prep; it is the night’s one real hazard.
- Diagnosing. The Signs Card’s own rule: it is for your own heart, and for one trusted person to help you read it — never a tool for filing somebody else under a column. If a cluster starts sorting an absent friend, the adult redirects.
- The live dry season. If a cluster member says *“this is me right now,”* the adult receives it — immediately, warmly, fully. That is the bright line working in front of everyone, and the CiF’s clean handoff is itself the rep succeeding.
- Case 4 romanticized. A first-time teacher can make God’s own stripping sound like the elite tier of dryness. Coach the correction: the card sorts; it does not rank. Most dryness is the tide, and neglect and weariness are named without shame.
- Comparison among the CiFs. Two reps each, side by side, one week before the big room — the ranking temptation is real. Name it at prep and again in the feedback framing: keeps and adjusts are per-teacher coaching, not a leaderboard.
- Performance panic. One or more CiFs will feel next week’s room tonight. The frame that settles it: tonight is *practice*, the adjust is *expected*, and the second rep exists precisely so something can be tried while it is warm.

**Crisis contingencies (Week 19).** Low-to-moderate risk. The material is about dryness and coming home, and someone in the room is statistically in one or the other right now. Standard Section 6 protocols apply. If a disclosure opens mid-teaching, the CiF hands it to the adult in the cluster and keeps teaching — the handoff honored publicly as leadership, not failure. If the gray someone names has swallowed everything — sleep, appetite, humor — the card’s own fourth rule applies: that is a treatable illness, not a spiritual verdict; the Lead Companion connects the family with the backup this week. And if a returner’s story surfaces (someone who has been away and is quietly back), the Path Home Card’s celebration rule holds: the returner chooses how public. Nothing is announced for them.

# **Companion Preparation**

## **Why this evening exists**

Two reasons, one pastoral and one formational.

**The pastoral reason:** the Signs Card and the Path Home Card are the most pastoral equipment this year hands out. Every participant in this room will someday hit a season where the practices go gray — and the most dangerous thing in that season is not the dryness but the wrong diagnosis. And some, someday, will leave and need a written way back. The Signs Card must be learned before the dry season, not improvised inside it, because two of its four cases command opposite motions. The Path Home Card must be placed in every home while everyone is present to hear what it is for. Tonight both things happen.

**The formational reason:** next Wednesday at The Long Walk, the Companions-in-Formation teach this material to the whole room — their final rep before commissioning at Week 22. Nobody’s final rep should also be their first. So tonight is the rehearsal: the same teaching, in the small room, twice, with coaching between. See one (the prep meeting), do one small (tonight, twice), do one large (next week). Misreading a dry season is the first hazard of peer leadership — which is exactly why this teaching, of everything in the year, is the one the FC1s must own cold.

The bright line (Handbook 11.2) governs the night unchanged: the CiFs lead a scripted practice with adults present; they never carry another person’s disclosure or care. Anything heavy passes immediately to the adult in the cluster, and that handoff is honored publicly as leadership, not failure.

## **The prep meeting — where the see-one happens**

The received-first rule (Handbook 11.6) is satisfied deliberately this week: at the prep meeting, the Lead Companion teaches the full Dry-Season block to the CiFs *as participants first* — the whole thing, unhurried, exactly as it will be given to the room. The room has already met both cards in working form — the Path Home Card put to use at the winter Return, the Signs Card’s sorting question walked at both re-entry rhythm checks — but the CiFs receive the complete teaching here, before they carry it. The scripts live in the Week 15 appendix (the Dry-Season block, preserved intact in [week-15-rhythm.md](week-15-rhythm.md)); each CiF builds their facilitation card from it at this meeting.

Then, still at prep:

1. **Each CiF rehearses their cluster arc aloud, timed** — Signs Card first, Path Home second — with the team playing the cluster. Not a conversation about the arc; the arc itself.
2. **Assign clusters and draw the rotation map.** Each CiF gets a first cluster and a second (one over, clockwise). A CiF does not teach their own family’s cluster first — the second rep may land there; the first rep goes to a cluster with no home audience.
3. **Name the adult in each cluster** — the coach and the catch, one job with two faces: light-touch coaching during the teaching, instant receiving if anything live surfaces.
4. **Drill the two disciplines** until the answers are boring. The probing question: *“Someone looks tearful during the four-kinds table — what do you do?”* Keep teaching, catch the adult’s eye. The diagnosing question: *“A parent says ‘I think my brother is a case 1’ — what do you say?”* The card’s own rule: it is for your own heart, and for one trusted person helping you read it — nobody gets filed under a column.
5. **Frame the feedback economy.** Two reps, two rounds of one keep and one adjust each. Tell the CiFs plainly: the adjust is the point of the rehearsal. A rehearsal that produces no adjusts was wasted.

## **Coaching the Companions-in-Formation**

- **Teach the card, not the person.** The whole lesson is on the two cards the cluster is holding. The CiF’s job is to make the cards legible and loved — not to demonstrate insight into anyone present.
- **The sorting question is the spine.** *Which way does the desire point?* If a CiF gets lost mid-table, coming back to that one question recovers the whole teaching.
- **Cases 3 and 4 are the load-bearing minutes.** One says *resist*, the other says *receive* — opposite motions, and the costliest mistake in the dry country is getting them backwards. Every CiF must be able to say the pair-difference in their own words, plus the card’s own safety rail: when you cannot tell which one you are in, do not decide alone.
- **The Path Home Card is warmth, not procedure.** The five steps matter less than the reception: the father runs. A CiF who teaches nothing else well but lands *“it ends with a feast, not a hearing”* has taught the card.
- **Low-disclosure discipline.** The CiF asks questions of information (*“does the difference between conviction and shame make sense?”*), never questions of condition (*“has anyone felt this?”*). The adults hold every door that opens.

## **Team pre-work**

1. Confirm the cluster assignments, the rotation map, and the adult coach-and-catch in each cluster. Confirm which CiF teaches where, both rounds.
2. Agree the coaching touch: from inside the cluster, light — at most one whispered cue per rep, notes saved for the feedback round. The rehearsal is the CiF’s room, not the adult’s.
3. Walk the feedback protocol: after each rep, the adult models first — one specific keep, one specific adjust — then invites the cluster to add one or two of the same, teens and parents both. Specific means quotable: *“keep the way you paused after the desire question”* — not *“you did great.”*
4. Walk the merged Leader Feedback Round for the close (Handbook 11.7 folded into the weekly round): tonight the CiFs answer first, together — each briefly, both questions — then the Lead Companion, then the room by the CiFs’ consent.
5. Arrange the parents-first voices: a parent in each cluster primed to offer the first cluster keep-and-adjust after the adult models, so no teen has to go first in feedback either.
6. Schedule The Long Walk prep meeting now, and name the note-carrier: tonight’s coaching notes, per CiF, travel to that meeting in writing.

# **Block-by-Block: Scripts and Notes**

## **Block 1 — Welcome and Opening Container (4:15–4:22, 7 min)**

A Companion-in-Formation opens with the full Section 5 protocol from memory — the standing circle, phones silenced and away, the one-word check-in, put out / bring in, the spoken commitment, the opening prayer naming the Holy Spirit. By this point in the year the container rep is routine for the CiFs; give tonight’s opening to one who is *not* teaching first-round in their strongest cluster, so the evening’s reps spread rather than stack.

## **Block 2 — The Frame: Why These Two Cards (4:22–4:28, 6 min)**

## Script (Lead Companion)

*“Take out the two cards in your folder — the Signs Card and the Path Home Card. Of everything this year has handed you, these two are the most pastoral equipment you will take home. Here is why.”*

*“Sooner or later, everyone who walks with God hits a dry season. Prayer feels like talking to the ceiling; scripture reads like cardboard. When that season comes, the dangerous thing is not the dryness — it is the wrong diagnosis. Whole journeys have ended over a misread dry season. The Signs Card exists so that never happens to anyone in this room. And some people, someday, leave — a month, a year, a decade — and conclude the room wouldn’t want them back. Most churches leave the way back unwritten. Not here. The Path Home Card is the written door, and it ends the way the father in Luke 15 ends it: he runs.”*

*“We met both of these cards in the winter — the Returns put the Path Home Card to work, and the rhythm checks asked the Signs Card’s one question. Tonight they get taught, properly — and not by us. Listen to this, from Paul near the end of his life: ‘and what you have heard from me in the presence of many witnesses entrust to faithful men, who will be able to teach others also.’ Second Timothy 2:2. Four generations in one sentence — Paul, Timothy, the faithful, and the others also. Tonight you are watching the third handoff happen. Our Companions-in-Formation teach these two cards in your clusters tonight — and next Wednesday, at The Long Walk, they teach them to this whole room. So let’s say plainly what tonight is: a rehearsal. They know it, we know it, and that is the design — the small room before the large one. Your cluster is their first congregation. Receive them, work the cards with them, and at the end your cluster will give its teacher two gifts: one thing to keep, and one thing to adjust.”*

*“One more thing, gently. Tonight is about the cards, not about anyone’s current season. Nobody will be asked where they are. But if the card finds you tonight — if one of those columns has your name on it — the adult in your cluster is there for exactly that, tonight or this week. That is what the room is for.”*

## **Block 3 — Bridge and Split (4:28–4:29, 1 min)**

The cluster map — each cluster, its families, its adult, its first-round CiF — is posted before the evening; read it out rather than building it here. Pray one sentence — for the teachers by name and for any dry ground in the room, unnamed. Split. *(If the room is about ten or fewer, see the small-room variation after Block 7.)*

## **Block 4 — First Teaching Rep: Both Cards (4:29–4:51, 22 min)**

The Companion-in-Formation leads their cluster through both cards from their facilitation card. The adult coaches from inside — seated in the circle, not behind it — with a light touch: at most one whispered cue, everything else saved for the feedback. The arc:

## The CiF’s arc (from the facilitation card)

**The Signs Card (~12 min).** Why the card exists — the wrong diagnosis, not the dryness, is the danger. The one question written up or said twice: *which way does the desire point?* Then the four kinds of dry, walked from the card’s own table — neglect, worn out, the enemy’s dry wind, God’s own stripping — with the load-bearing minute on cases 3 and 4: *one says resist, the other says receive; opposite motions; and when you cannot tell which you are in, do not decide alone — that is what Companions are for.* Then the rules of the card, all four, fast — your own heart only; tell someone in every case; no big decisions in the dark; know when to call the doctor. Close with the card’s word to the dry: *the fact that His absence hurts you is the evidence that He has not gone. Drifting hearts do not ache.*

**The Path Home Card (~9 min).** Why it exists — the way back is written here, and it ends with a feast, not a hearing: *“while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran”* (Luke 15:20). The five steps, plainly: come as you are and check which voice brought you (conviction draws toward home; shame drives away); tell it straight, to God and to one person; grieve what needs grieving; return to the table — small, next available day; let it be celebrated, at the returner’s own choosing. Close on the last line of the card: *the door is not locked. The light is on.*

**Close (~1 min).** *“These two cards go home with you tonight. This week your family gives them a spot — somewhere findable. Their whole job is to be findable on the day somebody needs them.”*

## Model script — the Signs Card taught in the cluster

*Written as a model for the prep-meeting rehearsal, not a cage. Each CiF says it in their own words; what is fixed is the spine — the one question, the four kinds, the opposite motions, the rules, the word to the dry.*

*“Take out the Signs Card. Here’s why this card exists. Sooner or later, everyone who walks with God hits a season where the practices go gray — prayer feels like talking to the ceiling, scripture reads like cardboard. And when that season comes, the dangerous thing is not the dryness. It’s the wrong diagnosis — the voice that says dryness means failure, or fake faith, or a God who looked at you and left. Whole journeys have ended over a misread dry season. This card exists so that never happens to anyone in this room.”*

*“The card says dryness is usually the tide, and only sometimes the thorn. The tide goes out and comes back — that’s how seas work, and how souls work. And one question sorts everything.”* (Write it up, or say it twice:) *“Which way does the desire point? Not ‘how do you feel’ — feelings are exactly what have gone quiet. Underneath the feelings: when you’re honest, do you still want God? Do you miss Him — does the distance ache? Or has the wanting itself faded, and the world tastes fine without Him?”*

*“That question splits four different kinds of dry — they’re the four columns on your card. Neglect: the wanting faded, the world still tastes fine, the practices quietly lapsed and you don’t grieve them. First step: wake and turn — rebuild the rhythm. Worn out: the wanting is buried under duty; you want escape, not God; everything is joyless and you keep thinking you’d be better off somewhere else. First step: rest — a lighter yoke, smaller faithful steps, and stay put.”*

*“Now the two that matter most, because they command opposite motions. The enemy’s dry wind: you feel accused and pressured, and there’s a voice counseling change — quit, undo, leave, walk away from things you decided in the light. The card says resist: change nothing, pray a little more, tell someone. And God’s own stripping: the desire is intact and starving — the ache is the sign — the world has gone gray too, you keep showing up and it feels like nothing, and you’re painfully worried about failing God. Lukewarm hearts never worry about that. The card says receive: wait, stay faithful, stay accompanied.”*

*“Look at those two again. One says fight; the other says yield. Getting them backwards — fighting God’s stripping, or yielding to the enemy’s wind — is the costliest mistake in the dry country. So when you cannot tell which one you’re in, the card’s own answer is: don’t decide alone. That is what Companions are for.”*

*“Four rules, fast, and they’re on the card. One: this card is for your own heart, and for one trusted person helping you read it — never for diagnosing somebody else at a distance. Nobody gets filed under a column. Two: in every case, tell someone.”* (A beat of silence.) *“Dryness that stays hidden is the kind that goes bad; dryness that’s spoken loses half its weight. Three: no big decisions in the dark — nothing decided in the light gets undone until the light returns. Four: know when to call the doctor. If the gray has swallowed everything — sleep, appetite, humor — that’s a treatable illness, not a spiritual verdict, and getting help is obedience.”*

*“Last thing, and it’s the card’s best sentence. If the dry season ever has you: the fact that His absence hurts you is the evidence that He has not gone. Drifting hearts don’t ache. Abandoned fields don’t thirst.”*

## Model script — the Path Home Card taught in the cluster

*“Now the second card. The Signs Card is for the dry season; this one is for the person who actually left — a month, a year, a decade, quietly or spectacularly — and thinks the room wouldn’t want them back. Most churches have shelves of material for starting the journey and not one page for returning to it. So the person coming back has to improvise the hardest walk of their life, alone, guessing at the reception. Not here. This card is the written door — and you should know how it ends before you read a single step. ‘But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him.’ The father runs. It ends with a feast, not a hearing.”*

*“Five steps, and they’re all on the card. Come as you are — and check which voice brought you: conviction is specific and hopeful and gets lighter the closer you get to home; shame is vague and hopeless and gets louder. Follow the one that leads somewhere. Tell it straight — to God in plain words, and to one trusted person; one is enough, and nobody here will ever require a public confession. Grieve what needs grieving — a third of God’s own songbook is griefs sung out loud, so He can bear to hear yours. Return to the table — small: don’t start over from the beginning; begin again on the next available day. Five dry minutes tomorrow morning is a full return. And let it be celebrated — the returner chooses how public, but heaven throws this party, and so do we.”*

*“The last line of the card is the whole card: the door is not locked. The light is on.”*

### **Receiving the teachers — a word the cluster adults carry in**

The cluster’s posture matters as much as the teaching. Parents especially set it: lean in, work the cards, answer information-questions when the CiF asks them, and resist every impulse to co-teach, correct from the floor, or beam commentary at your own teen at the front. A cluster that receives its teacher warmly tonight is rehearsing too — next week the whole room does this, and the week after next, some living room or dorm floor does. The feedback block is where help goes; the teaching block is where trust goes.

### **Notes for the cluster adult during Block 4**

- Coach light. One whispered cue at most (*“desire question”* is usually the only one needed). Everything else goes on the note sheet for feedback.
- Hold every door. If a cluster member says *“this is me right now”* — or says it with their face — the adult receives it, warmly and at once, while the CiF keeps teaching. The handoff, if the CiF initiated it, gets named in the feedback as the rep *working*.
- Guard the two disciplines. Probing (*“which one are you?”*) and diagnosing (an absent friend filed under a column) both get a gentle immediate redirect — the card’s own rules, quoted, so the correction lands on the card and not the person.
- Questions of information are welcome and are the CiF’s to answer; let them. Only step in on questions of condition.

## **Block 5 — Cluster Feedback: One Keep, One Adjust (4:51–4:56, 5 min)**

The adult models the shape first, specifically:

*“[Name], before you move — two gifts from this cluster. Mine first. Keep: the way you slowed down at the resist-or-receive difference and said it twice — that is the minute this teaching lives or dies on, and you gave it its weight. Adjust: the four rules went by fast; next room, land rule two — tell someone, in every case — with a beat of silence after it.”*

Then the arranged parent adds one keep or one adjust; one or two more voices if brief — teens welcome. The CiF receives without defending; *“thank you”* is the whole response. The adult writes both gifts on the note sheet. Total: five minutes, protected — this block is the reason the evening exists.

## **Rotation (4:56–4:58, 2 min)**

Each CiF moves one cluster clockwise, adjust in hand. The Lead Companion calls it from the center: *“Teachers, travel.”* The new cluster does not hear the previous cluster’s feedback — the second rep gets a clean room.

## **Block 6 — Second Teaching Rep: Both Cards, Tightened (4:58–5:15, 17 min)**

The same arc, compressed — Signs ~9, Path Home ~7, close ~1. The compression is not a downgrade; it is the second rep’s own skill, and it is next week’s reality (The Long Walk holds more than the teaching). Coach it at prep as *the tightened telling*: same spine, fewer words, the load-bearing minutes untouched — the desire question, resist-versus-receive, the father runs. The adjust from Round 1 gets tried here, while it is warm; the adult watches for it specifically.

*If a cluster’s first round ran long and the room is against the clock, the CiF’s fallback (agreed at prep): teach the Signs Card in full and give the Path Home Card its two sentences — the father runs; the door is not locked — with the card in every hand. Next week restores the whole.*

## **Block 7 — Second Cluster Feedback (5:15–5:19, 4 min)**

Same shape, briefer: the adult’s keep and adjust — the keep, wherever honest, naming the adjust that got tried — then one cluster voice. Both recorded per CiF. These notes are next week’s prep meeting.

### **Small-room variation (about ten or fewer, or fewer CiFs than clusters)**

- **One circle:** skip the clustering. Two CiFs split the material — one teaches the Signs Card, one the Path Home Card — feedback after each; then a compressed second pass with the cards swapped, so each CiF still touches both cards and still teaches twice.
- **One CiF only:** the single CiF teaches Round 1 in one cluster and Round 2 in another; adult Companions teach the remaining clusters from the same arc — the room still receives the cards everywhere, and the rehearsal still happens.
- **More CiFs than clusters:** pair them — one teaches Signs, the other Path Home, swap at the rotation. Every CiF still teaches twice.

## **Block 8 — Merge and Between-Session (5:19–5:22, 3 min)**

## **If the session worked**

- Every CiF taught twice, both cards, within a few minutes of the timings — and Round 2 was visibly tighter than Round 1.
- At least one adjust from Round 1 was demonstrably tried in Round 2. The rehearsal produced adjusts; that was the point.
- The clusters gave real feedback — specific, quotable keeps and adjusts, from parents and teens, not just the adults — and the CiFs received all of it without defending.
- The teaching stayed on the cards: no probing, no diagnosing, and any live disclosure passed cleanly to the adult and was received well.

## **If the session did not work**

- A rep became a lecture *about* the cards rather than a walk *through* them — or the teaching drifted into probing the cluster’s current seasons.

- The rotation ate the clock and Round 2 got squeezed into a summary; note the logistics fix for next cycle.
- A CiF visibly carried a critique badly — inflated or collapsed — and next week’s readiness call needs the honest conversation this week, not Wednesday at 4:05.
- The evening treated the material as an exercise about teaching rather than a teaching about mercy — if the room forgot that the dry season is real and the door is really unlocked, the frame block gets its weight back next week from the Lead Companion personally.
