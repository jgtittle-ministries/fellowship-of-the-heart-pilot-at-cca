# The Four Connects: the starting place

*Six modules that ground heart work in the two greatest commandments. The
recommended prequel to every Fellowship of the Heart curriculum.*

---

## Why start here

A lawyer asked Jesus which commandment in the Law is the greatest. Jesus
gave him two (Matthew 22:35–40):

> "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your
> soul and with all your mind. This is the great and first commandment. And
> a second is like it: You shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two
> commandments depend all the Law and the Prophets."

The Four Connects walk those two commandments as four connections:

1. **Connect with yourself.** The second commandment assumes a self that can
   be known and loved honestly.
2. **Connect with others.** Love for the neighbor you can see.
3. **Connect with God.** Love for God beyond performance, and learning to
   hear him.
4. **Connect with mission.** Where that love goes outward, into the good works
   God prepared beforehand (Ephesians 2:10).

The commandments name God first. The Four Connects walk from the near end,
self and others first, for a reason John gives: "he who does not love his
brother whom he has seen cannot love God whom he has not seen" (1 John 4:20).

This is **heart work**: practices that open the heart for the Spirit to do
his work. It is the starting place, and it stays the spine. Every choice in
the three curricula that follow traces back to one of these four
connections.

## The six modules

| | Module | What you do | Scripture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Spiritual formation, and why the Four Connects** | Talk through what spiritual formation is, for each person and for a group, and why the two greatest commandments come first | Matt 22:35–40; Rom 12:2; Heb 5:14; Eph 4:15–16 |
| 2 | **Connect with yourself** | *Are you ever surprised by what comes out of your mouth?* The Garden of Your Heart: picture your heart as a garden, then invite Jesus into it | Luke 6:43–45; Matt 12:35 |
| 3 | **Connect with others** | The Samaritans in your life: the people who irritate you, and what God may be doing through them. Four questions to ask someone close | 1 John 4:20; Matt 5:23–24; Luke 10:25–37 |
| 4 | **Connect with God** | Remember a time you felt connected to God, and a time you did not. Then hearing God: obedience to what you have heard opens you to hear more | John 14:8–14; 1 Cor 6:17; Matt 13:3–23 |
| 5 | **Connect with mission** | Name your *shadow mission*, the way your gifts get used to build yourself up. Then reverse it into your *gold mission*, the same gifts given to the Kingdom | Matt 25:14–30 |
| 6 | **Ecology check** | Count the cost. Did something new show up, and do you want to live it? Set goals and ask for help | Luke 14:28–30; Luke 12:35–48; Mark 4:1–20; 1 Cor 3:10–15 |

Each module starts with Scripture, uses imagery and parable for the
language of the heart, and moves from a personal process to a group
discussion. Every invitation is optional.

## How it is walked

The modules can be walked in **one full day** or spread across **six
sessions**. Fellowship of the Heart itself began with one full day: all of
the adults walked the Four Connects end to end in a single sitting, before
anything else was built.

*Fellowship of the Heart* itself began that way; [How we got here](how-we-got-here.md) tells the rest.

It is a prequel, not a prerequisite. A group can start a curriculum
without it. But it is a good on-ramp, and we recommend it highly: it
exposes people to heart work most of them have never thought of, and
what they taste here carries them into what follows.

**Adults first.** In our own practice the parents walked the Four
Connects before the teens. A leadership team should walk it for themselves
before leading anyone else through it.

**Therapeutic, not therapy.** Some of this work touches deep places. The
leader keeps the room on the right side of that line, and every invitation
to share carries a pass.
