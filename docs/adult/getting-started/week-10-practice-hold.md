# Practice Hold II — Two Weeks, No Meetings

*After Week 10 · the second seam of the 5/5/5 rhythm · re-entry at Week 11*

Again the structure lets go, and this time the stakes are higher, because what you are carrying now is the center of the whole series.

## Why the hold is here

Weeks 6 through 10 taught the hearing of God: bravery, the Scripture-hearing practice, and the garden of your heart. Week 10 deliberately dropped the garden rhythm from three visits to two, and said why: *the goal is a rhythm that survives, not a sprint that impresses — two visits, journaled, is the shape of a garden habit that could still be alive in a year.*

A claim like that deserves its test, and this is it. Two weeks with no room, no leader, no reminder. If the garden habit is going to be alive in a year, it will survive a fortnight.

## What to carry through the hold

- **The garden rhythm: two visits a week, journaled.** Same shape as Week 10 commissioned, nothing added.
- **The Scripture-hearing practice as your visits lead you** — and remember that a quiet visit is still a visit. The garden has quiet weeks. Showing up is the practice.
- The Week 5 thread, if it is still alive, needs no assignment anymore. Notice whether it continued on its own. That noticing is data too.
- **And carry the room’s question** — keep, change, tell — sent home on no paper at the last session. We answer it together when we return.

## What happens when we return

Week 11 opens with the what-held round: visits kept, visits missed, what the quiet weeks were like, and anything God said that you have not yet told anyone. Every report is received as it comes. *"I stopped after the first week"* told honestly is worth more to this room than a polished streak — and a quiet garden faithfully visited is not a failed practice; it is a real one.

---

*For the leader: same discipline as the first hold — the what-held round opens the session, every report received without correction. This hold reads the series' central practice, so listen especially for the difference between a practice that lapsed and a practice that went quiet while continuing; they sound similar in the first sentence and are opposites underneath. Time-to-settled on re-entry is again worth a quiet note.*
