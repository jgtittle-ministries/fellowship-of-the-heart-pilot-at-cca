# Evening 16 — Carrying the Word

*FotH Evening · Scripture by heart, as families · Anchor: Deuteronomy 6:6–9; Psalm 119:11; Colossians 3:16*

**This evening.** Tonight each family chooses a short passage, two to four
verses, and begins to learn it by heart together. The teens teach the room
how to memorize. Families practice at their own table, and before the
night ends each family says its passage to the room, haltingly is fine.
Then it goes home to be said every day.

**Where we are on the map.** The end of the third movement, *Mission and
Rhythm*. At *The Rhythm and the Four Questions* each person built the
rhythm they will carry. Next time, at *The Float*, everyone chooses one
practice to carry on purpose through the break. A passage learned tonight
is the most portable thing there is to carry.

**Why this evening.** Moses told Israel's parents: "These words that I
command you today shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently
to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and
when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise"
(Deuteronomy 6:6–7). That is this whole design in one sentence: the Word
passed from parent to child at home, in ordinary moments. "I have stored
up your word in my heart, that I might not sin against you" (Psalm
119:11). A verse carried by heart is there when no Bible is open, long
after the evenings stop.

**Bring.** Your journal, and your Bible. Before tonight, each family has
talked about which passage it might choose.

---

## Choosing the passage

- **From the family's own journey,** if it can be: the passage that spoke
  most in its PROAPT weeks, at Heart Bible Study, or in Doing the Word.
- **Short:** two to four verses for the family together.
- **Every age carries what it can.** A young child may carry one verse,
  or one line of it, and that counts as fully as a parent's four. A
  teen may choose to carry more.
- **One translation per family,** so everyone says the same words.

## For the two leads

- **No contests, no grading, no shame.** Nobody is scored, and nobody is
  compared. Forgetting is part of learning; a family that gets one line is
  doing the practice.
- **Every age gets a length it can carry.** Watch the youngest and the
  ones who find memorizing hard. Shorten their portion quietly and without
  fuss.
- **Parents go first.** In each family, a parent says the passage first,
  stumbles included. Teens learn to carry the Word by watching their
  parents carry it.
- **It is the Word, not a performance.** Say it slowly, meaning it. The
  goal is a passage on the heart, not a passage recited fast.

## The take-home (two weeks)

- **Every day:** say your passage together as a family, at the moment you
  named tonight. Once a day is enough; more is a gift.
- **End of week one:** the check-in with your partner, who asks you to say
  it.
- **Week two:** in your journal, one line: *where this passage met me this
  week.*
- **Bring it back:** at *The Float*, each family says its passage again,
  and it is the first thing offered as the one practice to carry through
  the break. At *The Return II*, after the break, we will say it once more,
  and see what carried.
