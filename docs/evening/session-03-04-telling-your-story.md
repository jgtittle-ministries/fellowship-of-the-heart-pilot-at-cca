# Sessions 3–4 — Telling Your Story

*FotH Evening · I & II in one evening · Anchor: Psalm 139:23–24*

**This evening.** The practice of being known: seen and safe at the same
time. One question, one circle, and everyone tells, parents first and then
the teens at their own depth. After each telling, a few seconds of silence
and a short blessing. Nothing leaves the circle.

**Where we are on the map.** The evening opens on the Four Connects, and
tonight crosses from the first box toward the second: *Self* opening toward
*Others*.

**Why combined.** The school form gives this two sessions, the parents'
night and then the teens' night. This room already knows each other, so
everyone tells in a single evening. That is how the founding group walked
it in August 2026. The two-session form, with room-splits for a larger
group of newer people, stands in reserve.

**Bring.** Your notebook or journal, and your take-home from Sessions 1–2.

## The take-home (two weeks)

*Printed sheets are handed out in the room; this is the same content.*

**The standing practice — every day, both weeks.** Morning, five minutes:
"Lord, what are you up to today, and what do you want me to notice?"
Evening, one journal line: "What did I notice today?"

**1. The homework practice — week one.** Joint Footprints. Each parent and
teen sit together once this week, for twenty minutes: not in the car, not
over a screen, phones away. Five minutes of footprints each way, a
follow-up question or two, then one sentence each: "What I love about your
footprints is…"

**2. The end-of-week-one check-in.** At the end of week one, connect with
the partner you named at commissioning and report how it's going against
the practice. You are not grading each other; you are expecting each other.

**3. The homework practice — week two.** Continue or deepen the practice;
journal what happens.

**4. One sentence of preparation.** Set at commissioning.

## For the leads

The full scripts, watch-fors, and crisis contingencies are in the school
form's Companion lesson plans, [Week 3](../getting-started/week-03-story.md)
and [Week 4](../getting-started/week-04-story-2.md). What follows is what
the combined evening form changes.

- **Everyone tells, one night.** Nothing new is taught between the two
  school sessions; the completion is the point, so it happens in one
  evening.
- **The demonstration shrinks to a reminder.** This room has seen the form.
  Three minutes at most; a leader models it briefly only if someone new is
  present.
- **One circle, no split.** Parents and teens tell to the same circle.
- **Safety never condenses.** This is the series' highest-risk material.
  Re-read Handbook Section 6 within 48 hours before, and have pastoral
  and clinical backup on call. Twice the tellings in one night means twice the
  vigilance, not half.
- **Arrange the order beforehand.** The arranged parent tells after the
  leader; the order is set at the planning lab, and it holds.
- **A parent's story may carry news a teen has not been told.** Talk it
  through with the Lead Companion beforehand and hold that plan. It matters
  more in the combined form, where every teen hears every parent's story.
- **Watch faces during the tellings,** each teen during their parent's
  story and each parent during their teen's. Weight received is a matter
  for the Settle Protocol; a quiet check-in at the door.
- **Never send anyone home untold.** If the clock runs short, shorten the
  blessings to a sentence; never drop a telling.
- **The container holder is the busier seat:** the clock and the room
  across fifty minutes of telling. Anything of weight goes to the backstop
  adult.
