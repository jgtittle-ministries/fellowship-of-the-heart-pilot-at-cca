# Evening 13 — Serving Together

*FotH Evening · The room hears, plans and agrees together · Anchor: John 13:12–17; Acts 13:1–3; Acts 15:28*

**This evening.** Tonight the room decides, together, how it will serve
someone in the next two weeks. At *What Was Prepared for You* each of us
looked for the works God prepared for us to do (Ephesians 2:10). Tonight
the whole room chooses one to do side by side. We practice three things
any group that obeys together has to learn: **hearing** God together,
**planning** together, and **agreeing** together. The serving itself
happens in the two weeks before the next evening, on a date the room sets
tonight. At Evening 14 the room reports back.

**Where we are on the map.** The third movement, *Mission and Rhythm*.
At Evening 11, *Doing the Word*, each person took one small step of
obedience. Tonight the room plans one together, and carries it out before
we meet again. At Evening 14, *The Rhythm and the Four Questions*, the
room tells what happened, and each person builds the rhythm they will
carry. Serving can be part of it.

**Why this evening.** On His last night, Jesus washed His disciples' feet
and said, "If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them"
(John 13:17). The early church learned to do it together. At Antioch,
while they were worshiping and fasting, the Holy Spirit spoke to the
whole group, and the group acted on it together (Acts 13:1–3). At
Jerusalem, the church heard each other out, weighed it against
Scripture, and wrote, "it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us"
(Acts 15:28). The series has evenings for hearing God together and for
each person's obedience. Tonight is the room's obedience: hearing, then
planning, then agreeing, before anyone picks up a tool.

**Bring.** Your journal, and the idea the last take-home asked for:
someone near us the room could serve together.

---

## Hearing, planning, agreeing

**Hearing together.** Before anyone argues for an idea, the room asks
the Lord: *whom would you have us serve together, and how?* Three minutes
of quiet. Then each person says what came: a name, a need, a picture, a
verse, or nothing. It goes on the chart beside the ideas people brought.
The rule is simple: say what you heard, not why it is right.

**Planning together.** The teens lead the planning at the chart, one
holding the marker and one keeping the Serving Plan. The room narrows
the list to a first choice and a second, using four checks:

- **Local and concrete:** someone the room can actually reach in the next
  two weeks. Meals for a family who is sick or grieving; yard work or a
  repair for a widow or an elderly neighbor; a shift at a church pantry
  or a ministry the host church already serves.
- **Something families can do together,** with a job for every age.
- **About an hour or two of real work,** usually on a Saturday morning,
  close enough to drive to and back.
- **Arranged with the people being served:** what they want, not what we
  think they need.

Then the plan: the jobs and who does them, what each family brings, the
date, the drivers, and who calls the people we hope to serve, by when.

**Agreeing together.** Not a vote. Each person in turn says one of three
things: *I'm in*; *I'm in, with a concern*, and names it; or *Not yet*,
and says what would make it a yes. Concerns are heard, and the plan
changes until every voice is in. The room also agrees how the plan may
change later: if the people we call ask for something different, the
teens and a parent may change the plan to fit, within the four checks;
if they say no thank you, we go to the second choice. Then the room
prays over the plan and the people, by name (Matthew 18:19).

**The room may pass.** A person may pass in any circle, and so may the
room. If every voice is not in by the end of the agreement round, or if
the room says together that none of these is ours to do now, the room
passes for tonight. That is a full answer, not a failure. It is what
happens then that matters:

- **We say it plainly.** The container holder names it: "We have not come
  to one mind tonight." Nobody is blamed, and nobody is named as the one
  who held it up.
- **Nobody decides for the room.** The plan is not finished afterwards by
  the leaders, by John or by text. If the room wants to try again, it
  does so together at a later evening.
- **We write down what held us apart,** on the Serving Plan: the concerns
  as they were said, without names.
- **We pray for one mind,** not for a plan: "that they may all be one"
  (John 17:21).
- **The two weeks still have a practice.** Each person keeps eyes open
  for one chance to serve someone, unplanned, and asks: *what kept us from
  one mind, and what would have made it a yes?*

Whether the room agreed or passed, John records it at the debrief. It is
one of the clearest signs the series has of whether the group is becoming
one, alongside which skill the room found hardest.

## The serving day

On the date the room set, usually a Saturday morning in the two weeks:

- **Gather and go.** Meet, pray briefly for the people we serve, by name,
  and drive over together.
- **The work.** About an hour or two, the teens leading on site with an
  adult alongside each job.
- **Before we leave.** Two minutes standing together: one word each, and
  a blessing prayed for the people we served, out of their hearing if
  they would rather.

## The report at Evening 14

At Evening 14's check-in, the teens who planned it give the room's
report, about five minutes: **what we planned, what happened, what
changed from the plan and why, and where we saw God.** Then each person
adds one sentence. The report is the end of the work: hearing, planning,
agreeing, doing, and telling what God did.

If the room passed, the report is shorter and just as honest: each
person says, in a sentence, the chance to serve they took or let pass,
and what they think kept us from one mind.

## For the two leads

- **Listen before you advocate.** In the hearing block, nobody argues for
  an idea. What someone heard is received with "We hear that," and goes on
  the chart.
- **Every voice, not the loudest.** In the agreement round, the container
  holder goes around the whole circle, parents and teens alike. A quiet
  "not yet" is worth more than a fast yes; ask what would make it a yes.
- **Do not push the room past its pass.** At 8:06 the container holder
  says, "Two minutes. If we are not one by then, we pass, and that is a
  full answer." Then keep to it. No leader saves the plan by talking
  longer.
- **The teens hold the marker.** Parents give their ideas and concerns
  like everyone else, but the teens run the chart and the plan. That is
  their leadership rep tonight.
- **Serve with people, not at them.** The people we serve keep their
  dignity. We do what they asked for, the way they want it done. The
  call asks what they would like; it does not announce what we decided.
- **No photos for show.** "Beware of practicing your righteousness before
  other people in order to be seen by them" (Matthew 6:1). A photo for the
  family who asked for one is fine; a photo to post about ourselves is not.
- **Safe and covered.** On the serving day, parents are present; no one
  works alone or out of sight; no ladders, roofs or power tools for teens.
  The day walks under the same pastoral covering as every evening, named
  aloud before we go.

## The take-home (two weeks)

- **At the top:** our plan, written in at the commissioning: whom we
  serve, what they asked for, our date, and my part.
- **Week one:** daily PROAPT, as always, with the morning question
  "Lord, how should we serve them?" The call is made by the day agreed,
  and the teens tell the room what the people asked for. Get your part
  ready.
- **End of week one:** the check-in with your partner: *Is your part
  ready? What do you need?*
- **On our date:** we serve together. Afterwards, two lines in your
  journal: *what I did*, and *where I saw God in it*.
- **Bring it back:** the report at Evening 14's check-in, and your one
  sentence.
- **If the room passed:** no project this time. Each day, eyes open for
  one chance to serve someone, unplanned; take it if you can. The
  check-in asks *Did a chance come? Did you take it?* In week two, two
  journal lines: *the chance I took or let pass*, and *what I think kept
  us from one mind*.
