# Session 6 — Safe and Brave Together

*FotH Evening · Anchor: James 5:16 and 1 John 1:9*

**This evening.** Confession and restoration as a community practice.
Each of us names one place where we want to walk in greater honesty — *the
place, not the details* — and the circle speaks a blessing of restoration
over it. We are not probing for sins; we are practicing the architecture of
confession, so that the room learns it is safe to be honest in.

**Where we are on the map.** The evening opens on the Four Connects —
Self, Others, God, Mission — and tonight closes the first quarter, *Connect
with Self and Others*. Three questions about the group go home tonight and
come back to be answered together when we next gather.

**Bring.** Your notebook or journal, and your take-home from Session 5.

## The take-home (two weeks)

*Printed sheets are handed out in the room; this is the same content.*

**The standing practice — every day, both weeks.** Morning, five minutes:
"Lord, what are you up to today, and what do you want me to notice?"
Evening, one journal line: "What did I notice today?"

**1. The homework practice — week one.** The Five-Minute Examen, each
evening before bed, through the two weeks:

1. Thank Him for one specific thing from the day.
2. Notice one place where you saw God moving.
3. Ask Him about one place where you walked in less honesty than you
   wanted to today. Just notice. No fixing; the Spirit does the work.

**And three questions go home, printed on The Road So Far**, to be answered together, out
loud, when we next gather: what is working well in this group that we
ought to keep doing; what could we do differently to make it even better;
and what would you tell somebody else about what this group is like?
About the group, never about any one person.

**2. The end-of-week-one check-in.** At the end of week one, connect with
the partner you named at commissioning and report how it's going against
the practice. A text, a call, or a hallway conversation all count. You are
not grading each other; you are expecting each other.

**3. The homework practice — week two.** Continue the Examen, or deepen it;
journal what happens.

**4. One sentence of preparation.** Set at commissioning.

**A second page goes home tonight: The Road So Far.** One page recalling
the quarter, evening by evening: the heart of each session and the
practice that went home, and the three questions themselves. It is there
so the road is fresh while you think on them.

## For the leads

This session's full scripts, watch-fors, and crisis contingencies are in
the school form's [Week 6 Companion lesson plan](../getting-started/week-06-brave.md);
read them before the planning lab. What follows is what the evening form
changes.

- **The place, not the details.** Say it before the groups, and say it
  again if anyone starts into a story. The practice is the naming and the
  blessing, not the account.
- **The place, the pattern, the pull** get a slide of their own, because
  the whole practice rests on them: the place is required; the pattern
  and the pull are each person's to add or keep. Ask for questions and
  wait — get it clear in the room, not in the groups. Each group takes a printed
  Breakout Card with the same three words, the form, the order and a
  sample blessing, for when it is away from the screen.
- **Parents go first.** The older generation owes the younger the
  demonstration: a teen should never be asked to be braver than their
  parent was willing to be, minutes earlier, in the same circle.
- **The pass holds, here most of all.** Anyone may pass, with no reason
  asked for. A pass is a full participation.
- **Small groups by gender, by default.** The teen leads' call on
  7 October, and it worked better than pairs: men and boys together,
  women and girls together, so each person has more than one witness and
  the parents-first order holds inside each group. Pairs, or one circle,
  are the leaders' judgement for the room that night — its size, its
  makeup, how new people are, who is a guest. Every group has an adult
  and a named holder who keeps the time and the order.
- **The merge belongs to the container holder.** The content presenter
  carries the groups; the container holder leads the merged blessing and its
  debrief, because anything important for reading the room is the
  container holder's charge, and the moment after confession is exactly
  that.
- **The bright line.** Anything of weight goes to the backstop adult. That
  is the design working, not the container failing.
- **The three questions go home on The Road So Far.** Read them aloud at
  commissioning; they are answered aloud next time, by the group, about the
  group.
- **A pair leads, visibly,** and practices the handoffs together
  beforehand, at the planning lab or before dinner.
