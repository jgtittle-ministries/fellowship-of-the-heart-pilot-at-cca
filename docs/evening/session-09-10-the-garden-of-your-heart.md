# Evening 7 — The Garden of Your Heart

*FotH Evening · Garden I & II in one evening · Tuesday, November 10 · Anchor: John 15:1–11; Psalm 23:1–3; John 10:27; 1 Thessalonians 5:20–21*

**This evening.** Last time we practiced hearing God in scripture. Tonight
we begin by remembering a real time we heard God, and asking how we knew.
Then we meet him in the inner room: each of us pictures our heart as a
garden, and then invites Jesus in. A senior teen reads, slowly. Whatever
we notice tonight we hold loosely and test on the returns: notice, don't
conclude.

**Where we are on the map.** The evening opens on the Four Connects. We are
in the second movement, *Connect with God*: hearing him in scripture
(Evening 6), meeting him in the garden (tonight), and bringing him our
honest doubts (Evening 8).

**Why one run.** The school form gives the garden two sessions. Here a
senior reads from the start, and the returns to the garden at home are the
second visit. Most of us only get all the way in on a return.

**The long gap ahead.** There is no evening over Thanksgiving week, so the
next evening is four weeks away, on Tuesday, December 8. The first two
weeks run as always: the garden, with a check-in after week one. Weeks
three and four are yours to choose: each week, pick one of the practices
we have walked so far, whichever you would really like to do. On December
8 we open by asking which ones you chose, and how it went.

**Bring.** Your notebook or journal, and your one sentence from the
Evening 6 take-home.

## The practices so far — choose from these

Every practice we have walked since August. In weeks three and four, choose
one for each week: a different one each week. Choose the one you would
really like to do, not the one you think you should.

| Practice | From | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **The abundance question** | Evening 1 | Read John 10:10b once, then ask: where is abundance in my life right now, and where isn't it? Watch for one specific moment. |
| **Soil-noticing** | Evening 2 | Three times in the week, journal: where did each kind of soil show up today? No fixing; just noticing. |
| **Joint Footprints** | Evening 3 | A parent and teen sit together once, for twenty minutes, phones away: five minutes of footprints each way, a follow-up question or two, then "What I love about your footprints is…" |
| **Offering a condition** | Evening 4 | In one relationship, offer one of the four conditions (safe, present, clear, intentional) a little more than usual. Journal what you notice. |
| **The Five-Minute Examen** | Evening 5 | Each evening: thank him for one specific thing; notice one place you saw God moving; ask him about one place you walked in less honesty than you wanted to. |
| **PROAPT** | Evening 6 | One paragraph of scripture a day, the six steps, ending with the Tell: one sentence of what you heard, to someone. |
| **The garden** | Evening 7 | Return to the garden, five to ten minutes. Journal what you notice, then: does it fit Scripture? who could I tell? |

The morning question and the evening line keep running underneath all of
these, as they have since Evening 1.

## The take-home (four weeks)

*Printed sheets are handed out in the room, with the Practices So Far card
and the How Did You Know? card; this is the same content.*

**The standing practice — every day, as you can.** Morning, five minutes:
"Lord, what are you up to today, and what do you want me to notice?"
Evening, one journal line: "What did I notice today?"

**1. Week one — three returns to the garden.** Five to ten minutes each; no
script needed. Journal what you notice, then two lines: does it fit
Scripture? who could I tell? Notice, don't conclude. If something heavy
surfaces, stop, come back another day, and tell a trusted adult.

**2. The end-of-week-one check-in.** Tell your named partner how it's going.
You are not grading each other; you are expecting each other.

**3. Week two — keep returning.** Two or three times, with the same two
lines. If something keeps coming back, test it with someone you trust.

**4. Weeks three and four — your choice.** Each week, choose one practice
from the list above, the one you would really like to do; a different one
in week four. Write down which, and why. If it slips, begin again the next
day. No starting over, and no counting streaks.

**5. Two sentences of preparation.** Before December 8, in your journal:
"One thing I'm not sure was God, or a question I'd bring to *Any Doubts?*,
is ____." And: "The practice I most wanted to keep was ____, because ____."

*A missed day is never a failure — it is an invitation to return.*

## For the leads

The full script is in the school form's Companion lesson plan,
[Week 9](../getting-started/week-09-garden.md), with the senior's pacing
in [Week 10](../getting-started/week-10-garden-2.md). The evening's run
sheet, deck and leader notes carry the two rounds and the testing that
comes before them. What follows is what the evening form changes.

- **Testing comes before the garden.** The room remembers a real time of
  hearing God and names how it knew. No one decides aloud whether another
  person's hearing was God. "I can't think of a time" is an honest answer.
- **The senior reads from the start.** Two words a second; the pauses are
  the work. Rehearse the reading at the planning lab, aloud, with a clock.
- **Receive without amplifying.** Simple, undramatic encounters are usually
  the most formative. Thank each share plainly and move on.
- **Name the gap plainly.** "See you in a couple of weeks" is how
  practices die. It is four weeks, with Thanksgiving in the middle.
- **Walk the list, don't pick for them.** Read the practices once, aloud,
  so everyone knows the menu. The choices for weeks three and four are made
  at home, freely. The point is to learn which practices people really
  want to do, so nobody is steered toward one.
- **Holidays are hard in some houses.** Watch the one-word landings
  closely tonight. Over the gap, the named adult makes one warm, light
  contact with each family, with no accounting asked.
- **The Settle Protocol stands.** Anything heavy in the garden: stop for
  the person, not the circle. The senior never takes a disclosure; adults
  stay accessible fifteen minutes after the close.
