# Sessions 7–8 — Hearing God in Scripture

*FotH Evening · PROAPT, I & II in one evening · Anchor: Romans 10:17; Mark 1:14–20; Mark 2:1–12*

**This evening.** The journey turns from Self and Others toward God. Faith
comes by hearing (Romans 10:17), and hearing is a skill that grows with
practice. Tonight we learn the practice of hearing God in scripture,
PROAPT, and walk it twice: once together on Mark 1:14–20, then in family
pairs on a second passage, Mark 2:1–12. Hearing is a skill, and skills take
reps.

**Where we are on the map.** The evening opens on the Four Connects, and
tonight opens the second quarter, *Connect with God*. It begins with the
room reading itself: the three questions that went home from Session 6,
answered together.

**Why combined.** The school form gives PROAPT two sessions, one received
and one led. This room already knows each other, and its teens lead, so
both reps fit inside one evening. The separate second session stands in
reserve for a larger room of newer people.

**Bring.** Your notebook or journal, your Personal Heart Journal if you
have one, and your take-home from Session 6.

## PROAPT — what each step is for

*The order protects the hearing.* Ground yourself in what the passage
actually **says** before you work out what it **means**: first what it
meant in the world where it was written, then what it means for you, in
yours. Skip ahead and you will mostly hear yourself.

| Step | What you do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Pray** | Ask the Spirit to speak, briefly. | You come expecting to *hear*, not to extract information. |
| **Read** | Read it through slowly, twice if short, aloud if you can. | Let it land whole before you take it apart. |
| **Observe: the data** | Who, what, when, where, straight off the page. | The ground. Skip it and you import what you already believe; most misreadings start here. |
| **Observe: the interpretation** | What stands out, surprises, or seems strange, and why it would have mattered to those who first heard it. | The passage meant something in its own world before it means something in yours. |
| **Apply** | What is this saying to *me*, today, in my actual situation? One specific thing. | Where reading becomes hearing: the general word becomes a specific word. |
| **Pray again** | Tell God what you heard, what you are willing to do, and what you are struggling to be willing to do. | Hearing becomes a response; obedience begins here. |
| **Tell** | Say one sentence of what you heard to someone. | Spoken hearing stays. The most often skipped step. |

## The take-home (two weeks)

*Printed sheets are handed out in the room; this is the same content. A
second page goes home with it: "PROAPT, What Each Step Is For," the fuller
form of the table above.*

**The standing practice — every day, both weeks.** Morning, five minutes:
"Lord, what are you up to today, and what do you want me to notice?"
Evening, one journal line: "What did I notice today?"

**1. The homework practice — week one.** PROAPT one short passage a day,
five to ten minutes, at the time of day you named aloud. The Personal
Heart Journal's Week 7–8 pages carry a daily template. Pick a passage path:
Mark 1–2, a story a day; the Sermon on the Mount or Romans 8; or a
Psalm-and-Gospel pairing.

**2. The end-of-week-one check-in.** At the end of week one, connect with
the partner you named at commissioning and report how it's going against
the practice. You are not grading each other; you are expecting each other.

**3. The homework practice — week two.** Continue daily PROAPT, or deepen
it; journal what happens.

**4. One sentence of preparation.** Set at commissioning.

*The chain only works if we work it. A missed day is never a failure — it
is an invitation to return.*

## For the leads

The full scripts are in the school form's Companion lesson plans,
[Week 7](../getting-started/week-07-proapt.md) and
[Week 8](../getting-started/week-08-proapt-2.md). What follows is what the
combined evening form changes.

- **Both reps in one evening.** The room's teens lead the walk-through
  together; the second running becomes pass two in the pairs, on
  Session 8's passage. Say the reps aloud: pass one is rep one, pass two is
  rep two. A passage speaks differently on a second hearing.
- **Teach the order before the pens move,** and slow down at the line
  between the data and the interpretation. That line is the lesson.
- **The room reads itself first.** The leader answers the three questions
  first, with a moment attached to each, then the room. If an answer drifts
  toward a person, bring it back gently: the group, never any one person.
- **Pairing with your own parent or teen is the design working,** not the
  consolation prize. Say it before the split.
- **Stuck on Observe?** "What's one word or phrase that caught your
  attention?" **Stuck on Apply?** "Where in your life right now might this
  passage be talking?" **Rushing?** "The Spirit isn't in a hurry."
- **Pass-two fatigue.** If a pair flags in the second pass, shorten it
  rather than skip it. The second hearing is the point of the combined
  form.
- **The container holder is the busier seat:** the clock and the room both,
  through two passes. Anything of weight goes to the backstop adult.
