# The Evening Handbook

*FotH Evening — Two-Week Rhythm · Teen-Led. The one-sitting guide to how
the evenings work. The deep protocols — the full scripts, the safety and
crisis procedures, the theology of the container — live in the
[Companion Handbook](../getting-started/handbook.md); this handbook
carries what the evening form asks of the people in the room.*

---

## What this is

FotH Evening is the Getting Started journey walked in a home: every two
weeks, around a family table, with teens leading and adults participating.
The curriculum is the same one the CCA pilot walks on Wednesday
afternoons; the evening changes the clock, the room, and — deliberately —
who leads.

The test here is the curriculum and the leaders, not the participants. If
an evening wobbles, that is a finding about the materials, and the
materials change. Nobody in the circle is being graded.

## The shape of an evening

| Time | What | Who leads |
|---|---|---|
| 6:00 | Dinner together. Arrival, names, hospitality — the table is part of the container. Over the meal, John asks about the homework: what's working, and what's not. | The household; John asks |
| 7:00 | The session begins (blocks below). | The teen leads |
| 8:30 | Curriculum session complete. Fellowship, leadership feedback, planning for next meeting. | Everyone |
| 9:00 | Done. | |

The 90-minute session keeps a steady shape:

| Time | Block | Lead |
|---|---|---|
| 7:00–7:05 | Welcome and centering: blessing, the Four Connects map (where tonight sits), container reminder, frame the evening. | Container holder |
| 7:05–7:09 | Check-in: one word around the room for what the homework did in you. The homework itself was talked over at dinner. | Container holder |
| 7:09–7:25 | Teaching block: the session's content, one block (Scripture, teaching, framing and bridge together). | Content presenter |
| 7:25–7:50 | Practice block: the session's exercise, in circle or pairs. Its time is protected. | Content presenter, container holder watching the room |
| 7:50–7:57 | Shared circle: what surfaced, two or three voices or one word each. Parents first. | Container holder |
| 7:57–8:02 | Homework commissioning: the take-home handed out on paper and walked, check-in partners named aloud. | Content presenter |
| 8:02–8:12 | Leader Feedback Round: the leaders' own answers, then the room's, every parent answering, each adding only what hasn't been said. | Both leaders |
| 8:12–8:16 | Closing blessing. | Container holder |
| 8:16–8:30 | Buffer: for a practice that runs deep or a room that needs it. | |

Each session's page carries its own run sheet where the evening bends this
shape; the shape itself is the constant.

## Keeping the evening to time

*Added 11 November 2026, after Evening 7 (John's word).* The evenings were
running long. Pair work takes the same time however many are in the room;
whole-room rounds grow with every person added, and with guests and a
growing group they are what overran. So the fix keeps the practice and
shortens the rest.

- **Protect the practice.** The practice blocks keep their full time. So do
  a short container open and close, the return question after a break, the
  take-home, and the Leader Feedback Round.
- **The homework is talked over at dinner.** John asks around the table
  what's working and what's not. In the circle, the check-in is one word
  each: what the homework did in you.
- **Whole-room rounds scale.** A round that everyone answers becomes one
  word each, or two or three voices, or pairs. One-word merges stay.
- **One teaching block.** Scripture, teaching, framing and bridge run as one
  block. Anything more goes on the take-home sheet.
- **The take-home goes out on paper**, walked in a few minutes, not read.
- **The Leader Feedback Round stays whole.** The leaders give their own
  answers, then the room answers the same two questions about them, and
  every parent answers. It is feedback and affirmation at once, for leaders
  who are still finding their way, and the parents' word is a big deal to
  the teens. When a teen leads, the room's answers are kind and brief, but
  every voice is welcome. It is never the block that gets squeezed. **Each
  person adds only what hasn't already been said**; agreeing with what
  someone said is a nod, or "same." At Evening 7 the round ran about
  fifteen minutes, much of it repeating what others had said.
- **An adult keeps a visible clock**, with each block's end time, and gives
  the container holder a two-minute signal.
- **The buffer is for depth, not for more material.** A messy evening
  whose practice lands is a good evening.

Two standing flexibilities inside the shape. **Every evening opens on the
map**: the Four Connects — Self, Others, God, Mission — with tonight's
session located on it, so the work is always grounded in the journey it
serves. And **splits fit the room**: where a session's practice block
splits the group, the split assumes numbers — a small room stays one
circle, or drops into quick pair work in place, which does the same work
faster and doubles as the time-recovery lever (a full split-and-merge
costs about five minutes of the evening).

## The current-condition walk

The series is walked intentionally at the scale of the room it has —
right now, the four prototype teens, their two parents, and John: a room
that already knows each other. Three things follow. **One circle by
default** — no room-splits, no parent/teen separation. **Condensations
where completion doesn't need two evenings** — the second runnings fold
into their firsts, each in the way its material asks:

- **Evening 3**: everyone tells, one round, one evening
  (proven in the prototype group's own running). The size of the room
  decides it: a room large enough, with parents and teens, takes the two
  sessions.
- **Evening 6**: both PROAPT reps inside one evening — rep
  one walked together, rep two in the pairs on the Session 8 plan's passage
  (Mark 2:1–12).
- **Evening 7**: one guided garden run, a senior reading
  from the start; the week's three returns *are* the second visit,
  received at the next check-in.
- **Session 14 absorbed**: its purpose — every willing teen a full
  leading rep — is the standing pattern of every current evening; any
  re-run middle can be called at a planning lab.
- **Session 19 moved to the lab**: the mercy-card rehearsal happens at
  the off-week planning lab with John; Evening 19 teaches the cards to
  the room as designed.

And **the full form held in reserve** — the two-session runnings, the
room-splits, and the parent/teen separations all stand ready in the v1
materials for a larger room of newer people; the walk backs up to them
whenever the room grows. Safety never condenses: Section 6 preparation
and the Settle Protocol apply to the combined forms in full.

## The two roles and the rotation

- **The container holder** opens and closes the evening, holds the
  check-in and shared circles, and watches the room during practice.
  Safety and warmth, presence, clear and intentional — the container
  holder's charge is the room itself. The criterion is simple: anything
  that is important for reading the room belongs to the container holder —
  which is why, when a split practice merges back, the container holder
  leads the merge and its debrief.
- **The content presenter** carries the teaching and practice blocks,
  hands the room to the container holder at the merge, and takes it back
  to commission the take-home.

With enough teens, the roles can be held by pairs or by individuals as the
rotation allows. Teens step up as ready and willing — and they have been.
The rotation is kept on the sign-up sheet, settled at the planning labs.

In practice the container holder is often the *busier* seat: keeping the
clock **and** continuously reading the room against the four conditions —
safe, present, clear, intentional — including noticing anyone triggered or
checked out. Anything of weight goes to the backstop adult; that is the
bright line working, not the container failing.

When two leaders hold an evening, two habits keep it smooth: the pair
**practices the session together beforehand** — at the planning lab, or
before dinner — so each knows who is where; and the welcome **names the
pair out loud**, so the room expects to see the evening handed back and
forth between them. The handoffs are the design, not a wobble.

## The adult backstop and the bright line

An adult — John or a parent — is always in the circle as a participant,
and is never far from anything personal. Teen leads hand off to an adult
whenever something surfaces that is heavier than a teenager should carry;
that bright line holds always, and handing off is *leading well*, not
failing. A door out of any conversation is named aloud in the welcome,
every evening, along with the pastoral covering the group walks under.

The full safety, confidentiality, and crisis protocols are in the
[Companion Handbook](../getting-started/handbook.md) and they govern here
unchanged. They cover guests too. If anyone, teen, parent or guest, says
they have been troubled at night or by a sense of evil, the backstop adult
follows [If someone reports oppression from outside](../getting-started/handbook.md#if-someone-reports-oppression-from-outside):
pray with them in Jesus' name afterward, bring the parent in, and never
make it a moment in the circle.

## When something said isn't right

The circle receives what is told, with *"We hear that,"* and no fixing,
advice or comment. Receiving is not agreeing. Sooner or later someone, a
teen or a guest, will say in the circle something a parent knows is not
right. The rule moves correction; it does not remove it.

- **Not in the circle.** The circle never debates or corrects what a person
  says they heard. Correction in front of the room teaches everyone that
  honesty is unsafe.
- **Testing is the practice.** Anything heard that is to be acted on is
  first tested in company: Scripture first, then others, then time (the
  *How Did You Know?* checks, and the whole *Any Doubts?* evening). A
  leader may point the room to the checks in general, never at one
  person's sentence.
- **Afterward, privately, gently.** Anything said that conflicts with
  Scripture, or that could do harm, is taken up after the evening, in
  private and gently (Matt 18:15; Gal 6:1): by the teen's parent, or by the
  backstop adult when no parent is there, as with a guest. Anything that
  could do harm, or a step that involves another person, goes to the
  backstop adult that same evening.
- **Parents keep their authority.** What a family teaches at home is the
  family's. The group never overrides a parent, and no one in the group
  asks a teen to keep anything from a parent.
- **Weighing together comes later.** Later in the series the room learns to
  weigh what it hears together (Evening 13: hearing, planning, agreeing).
  Receiving first is the rule for this stage of the road, not the whole of
  it.

## The planning lab

In the off week, the leading team walks the coming session with John —
about thirty minutes, by phone or at the dinner table when the schedule
lets the lab happen face to face. **Every lab opens with one question:
have you reviewed the slides?** Then John walks the slides with the
leading pair, from a printed copy he brings, and the content presenter
says each teaching transition aloud once: the container is learned by
now, and the content is the challenge each time. The lab then walks the session's issues,
fragilities, and passes; names the watch-fors; and settles anything the
run sheet leaves open. No teen leads a session cold. The content presenter
also practices delivering the teaching in the lab, to John if no one else,
to get its rhythm before the evening; and a presenter who has not yet
walked the session's practice as a participant walks it first, with a
parent or a lead, before the evening.

**Every lab also rehearses the cold start.** Each leading pair practices
a 60–90 second *where we are* for someone walking in for the first time,
from the middle of the series: what this group is, where tonight sits on
the map, and what the guest will be asked to do (and not asked to do). A
leader who can explain the evening from the middle can carry it anywhere.
That is what keeps the work from depending on the people who started it.

## The two-week take-home

Each session ends with a one-page take-home in four parts:

1. **The homework practice, week one** — the session's practice for the
   first week, with a box to check for each day.
2. **The end-of-week-one check-in** — at the end of week one, connect with
   the partner you named aloud at commissioning and report how it's going.
   A text, a call, or a hallway conversation all count. You are not
   grading each other; you are *expecting* each other. If your family
   wants to do the week-one check-in together at the table as well, that
   is welcome: one family found the parents hearing each check-in was a
   gift. Keep your named partner too; the partners cross families on
   purpose, and listening is not checking.
3. **The homework practice, week two** — the practice continued, deepened
   or repeated as the session specifies.
4. **One sentence of preparation** — read, notice, or bring something for
   the next session.

Check-in partners are chosen fresh at each commissioning, so the reporting
lines move around the group. Printed sheets are handed out in the room;
each session's page here carries the same content.

And the standing closer, every cycle: *a missed box is never a failure —
it is an invitation to return.*

## The leadership window (8:30–9:00)

After the closing blessing, the evening turns to the leaders:

- **After the Leader Feedback Round.** The round itself happens in the
  room, before the closing: the leaders' own three answers (what went
  well, what I would do differently, and the teach-back), then the room's,
  every parent answering. Anything harder than the room's kind, brief
  answers is taken up here, not in front of the room.
- **The five-question materials debrief**, held by John: Were the
  instructions clear? Could the team use them as written? What changed on
  the fly? Where did the room wobble? What should the materials say
  differently?
- **Planning**: the next session's leads confirmed, the calendar checked,
  the planning lab scheduled.

This half hour is where the curriculum gets tested and the leaders get
built. It is not an afterthought; it is half the point.

## Newcomers, drop-ins, and guests

Every session stands alone by design. The welcome block always includes
two minutes of *where we are on the map*, so a first-time guest can
usefully take part in any single evening. Bring a notebook or journal —
there is reflective writing in every session — and clipboards with paper
are on hand for anyone who needs them.

**Guests are welcome, and invited by the group.** The teens are encouraged
to invite their friends, and families to invite others. A guest is a
guest: not a participant in the pilot in the way the founding families
are, so no consent form and no special announcements. The adults in the
room care for guests as they would in any Bible study.

**Guests test the evening.** A guest arriving in the middle is the plainest
test of the design's claim that every evening is whole on its own. The
measure is light and belongs to the friendship: some days later, the
person who invited the guest asks two questions.

- *How did it land?*
- *Are you doing anything with it?*

Whatever the answers are, they come back to the leadership window as the
inviter tells them, without names in any record, and they feed the
five-question materials debrief: did that evening carry a newcomer?

**A guest who comes back is one sign.** Even one evening gives outside eyes
on whether the work holds anything of value, and a return says more. It is
one sign among others, since friendship and a good table bring people back
too, and a guest who doesn't return is never held against an evening. So
at each leadership window John notes, with no names, how many guests came,
what the inviters heard, and how many guests have come back.

**Guests train the leaders.** Starting someone in the middle, and
explaining enough of what is going on for them to take part, is part of
what the teen leads are learning. It is rehearsed at every planning lab
(the cold start, above).

**A guest's partner is a bridge.** At commissioning, invite a guest to
choose a check-in partner who knows them and has walked the practices. That
partner explains any practice the guest missed and expects to hear from
them in the week, which is also the likeliest reason a guest comes back.
It is teaching others in its smallest form. Anything of weight still goes
to an adult.

## How the group is watched over

The [three vital signs](../shared/three-vital-signs.md) are watched as
always. At each long break, the return round reads what held and which
practices people chose, and the leads count the choices quietly, with no
names, per the [measurement covenant](../shared/measurement-covenant.md)
and the family's consent. What is measured is the health of the work —
never the worthiness of the people.
