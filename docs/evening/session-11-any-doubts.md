# Evening 8 — Any Doubts?

*FotH Evening · Tuesday, December 8 (expected) · Anchor: Mark 9:14–29; "I believe; help my unbelief!" (Mark 9:24)*

**This evening.** We practice naming the doubts we usually do not say out
loud and bringing them honestly to God. The model is the father in Mark 9:
"I believe; help my unbelief!" The naming is our part; the answer is the
Spirit's work. The practice runs in pairs, a parent with their own teen,
and the parent always goes first. Then we go to the silent place: a time
when God seemed silent, with the time we did hear him brought in.

**First, the return.** This is our first evening after four weeks apart.
Before anything new, we come back together and read the gap honestly:
which practices you chose for weeks three and four, what held, and, when
it slipped, whether you came back to it on your own, because you wanted
to.

**Where we are on the map.** The evening opens on the Four Connects.
Tonight closes the second movement, *Connect with God*: hearing him in
scripture, meeting him in the garden, and now bringing him our honest
doubts.

**The Christmas break ahead.** Tonight is the one evening in December. The
Christmas break follows, and Evening 9, *The Return*, opens January. So
tonight closes with a send-off, as Evening 7 did: two weeks as always,
then, each week of the break, one practice of your own choosing.

**Bring.** Your notebook or journal, and your two sentences from the
Evening 7 take-home.

## The return round — how it runs

Around the circle, one person at a time, the leader first, then a parent,
with the two sentences from the Evening 7 take-home in hand.

1. **Which did you choose?** The practice you chose for week three, and the
   one for week four, and in a few words, why those.
2. **What held?** Which made it through the week, and which slipped. Plain
   words.
3. **Did you come back?** When it slipped, did you return to it on your
   own — not because an evening was coming, but because you wanted to?
4. **Which one would you keep?** The one you most wanted to keep doing.
5. **What you're not sure was God.** Say it aloud, or keep it for your
   pair tonight.

Nobody fixes anybody, and nobody compares. Slips are data, not a verdict.
Nobody owes anyone an explanation for the gap. If the gap was hard in ways
that have nothing to do with practices, that is not a circle conversation,
but it is a conversation with an adult, tonight or this week.

## Any Doubts? — the four steps

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Your partner reads a scripture you chose, one you say you believe. Notice the flicker: "Yes, but…" Name that doubt specifically. "I doubt he hears my prayers about my brother," not "I doubt God." |
| **Exaggerate** | Make the doubt bigger; say it stronger. You are not endorsing it; you are bringing it out where the Spirit can meet it. |
| **Sit** | What does it feel like? Sad, tired, resigned? Sit with it for ten or fifteen seconds. Don't analyze, fix or spiritualize it. |
| **Re-read** | Your partner reads the scripture again, slowly. Notice what is different. The doubt may not resolve tonight; that is fine. |

## The take-home (across Christmas)

*Printed sheets are handed out in the room, with the Practices So Far card
(now with Any Doubts? on it); this is the same content.*

**The standing practice — every day, as you can.** Morning, five minutes:
"Lord, what are you up to today, and what do you want me to notice?"
Evening, one journal line: "What did I notice today?"

**1. Week one — the Personal Doubts Inventory.** Twenty minutes, once: list
five to ten scriptures you say you believe; rate your trust in each, one to
five; for the lowest two or three, write the specific doubt and sit with
it. No shame; just data. The list stays with you.

**2. The end-of-week-one check-in.** Tell your named partner how it's
going. You are not grading each other; you are expecting each other.

**3. Week two — two journal questions.** Is there something I heard and did
not obey? What is at risk for me to hear? Not sure something was God? Test
it with the How Did You Know? card, in company.

**4. The rest of the break — your choice.** Each week, choose one practice
from [the practices so far](session-09-10-the-garden-of-your-heart.md#the-practices-so-far-choose-from-these),
whichever you would really like to do. *Any Doubts?* is on the list now:
with a partner, Name, Exaggerate, Sit, Re-read. Write down which, and why.
If it slips, begin again the next day. No starting over, and no counting
streaks.

**5. Two sentences of preparation.** Before *The Return*, in your journal:
"One thing I heard that I will act on is ____." And: "The practice I most
wanted to keep was ____, because ____."

*A missed day is never a failure — it is an invitation to return.*

## For the leads

The full script is in the school form's Companion lesson plan,
[Week 11](../getting-started/week-11-doubts.md). The evening's run sheet,
deck and leader notes carry the silent place and the return round. The
school form closes a quarter tonight; the evening form has no quarters, and
its breaks are the holidays. Whether a Pulse goes home tonight is still to
be decided. What follows is what the evening form changes.

- **Open with the return, not the content.** Rebuild the container slowly.
  The return round replaces the usual check-in and takes four more minutes,
  given back by the pairs.
- **Cut off the shame spiral early, from the front.** Someone, usually a
  parent, will arrive rehearsing a confession for a dropped practice. Say
  it before anyone starts: slips are data, and nobody owes an explanation.
- **Ask "did you come back?" plainly.** It is the one we most want to
  hear. Receive "No, not yet" as warmly as "Yes."
- **The choices are the read.** Which practices people chose for weeks
  three and four, and which they most wanted to keep, tell us which
  practices carry the "I really want to do this" pull. At the debrief,
  tally them quietly: a count for each practice, chosen and kept, with no
  names. The same tally runs again at *The Return* for the Christmas
  choices.
- **Frame doubt three times before the pairs.** It is the gap between what
  we say and what we trust, not a faith crisis. Push gently for the small,
  personal doubt.
- **Parents go first in every pair,** arranged with each parent
  beforehand. The teen chooses their own depth, and pass-anytime holds.
  "I don't notice any right now" is a complete answer.
- **The silent place is not a verdict.** If nothing comes, that is honest.
  Sharing is optional; say so twice.
- **Name the Christmas break plainly** in the send-off: how long it is, and
  that *The Return* in January opens by asking which practices were
  chosen and how it went. Walk the list once, aloud; the choices are made
  at home, freely.
- **Holidays are hard in some houses.** Watch the return round and the
  one-word landings closely. Over the break, the named adult makes one
  warm, light contact with each family, with no accounting asked.
