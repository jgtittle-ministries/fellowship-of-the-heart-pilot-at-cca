# Evening 8 — Any Doubts?

*FotH Evening · Tuesday, December 1 · Anchor: Mark 9:14–29; "I believe; help my unbelief!" (Mark 9:24)*

**This evening.** We practice naming the doubts we usually do not say out
loud and bringing them honestly to God. The model is the father in Mark 9:
"I believe; help my unbelief!" The naming is our part; the answer is the
Spirit's work. Tonight it is done slowly, parents first: John walks each
parent through it, one at a time, in front of the room, and the room talks
over the process after each. Then the parents lead John through it. The
teens watch, help talk it over, and lead it themselves in the new year, at
Evening 12. If there is time, we go to the silent place: a time when God seemed
silent, with the time we did hear him brought in.

**First, the return.** This is our first evening after three weeks apart, across Thanksgiving.
Before anything new, we come back together and read the gap honestly:
which practice you chose after the first two weeks, what held, and, when
it slipped, whether you came back to it on your own, because you wanted
to. We talk it over at dinner; in the circle, one word each.

**Where we are on the map.** The evening opens on the Four Connects.
Tonight is the third evening of the second movement, *Connect with God*:
hearing him in scripture, meeting him in the garden, and now bringing him
our honest doubts. Heart Bible Study, in January, closes the movement.

**The Christmas break ahead.** Tonight is the one evening in December. The
Christmas break follows; in January, Evening 9, *Heart Bible Study*, welcomes everyone home, and Evening 10, *Spiritual Styles*, follows it. So
tonight closes with a send-off, as Evening 7 did: two weeks as always,
then, each week of the break, one practice of your own choosing.

**Bring.** Your notebook or journal, and your two sentences from the
Evening 7 take-home.

## The return — how it runs

**At dinner.** Over the meal, John asks the return questions around the
table, the way he asks about the homework at every dinner. Bring the two
sentences from the Evening 7 take-home.

1. **Which did you choose?** The practice you chose after the first two
   weeks, and in a few words, why.
2. **What held?** Which made it through the week, and which slipped. Plain
   words.
3. **Did you come back?** When it slipped, did you return to it on your
   own — not because an evening was coming, but because you wanted to? And
   did anyone remind you?
4. **Which one would you keep?** The one you most wanted to keep doing.

**In the circle,** after the welcome: one word around the room for what
your chosen practice did in you, received with a nod. What you're not
sure was God stays in your journal tonight, to be tested later with someone you trust.

Nobody fixes anybody, and nobody compares. Slips are data, not a verdict.
Nobody owes anyone an explanation for the gap. If the gap was hard in ways
that have nothing to do with practices, that is not a circle conversation,
but it is a conversation with an adult, tonight or this week.

## Any Doubts? — the four steps

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Your partner reads a scripture you chose, one you say you believe. Notice the flicker: "Yes, but…" Name that doubt specifically. "I doubt he hears my prayers about my brother," not "I doubt God." |
| **Exaggerate** | Make the doubt bigger; say it stronger. You are not endorsing it; you are bringing it out where the Spirit can meet it. |
| **Sit** | What does it feel like? Sad, tired, resigned? Sit with it for ten or fifteen seconds. Don't analyze, fix or spiritualize it. |
| **Re-read** | Your partner reads the scripture again, slowly. Notice what is different. The doubt may not resolve tonight; that is fine. |

## The take-home (across Christmas)

*Printed sheets are handed out in the room, with the Practices So Far card
(now with Any Doubts? on it); this is the same content.*

**The standing practice — every day, as you can.** Morning, five minutes:
"Lord, what are you up to today, and what do you want me to notice?"
Evening, one journal line: "What did I notice today?"

**1. Week one — the Personal Doubts Inventory.** Twenty minutes, once: list
five to ten scriptures you say you believe; rate your trust in each, one to
five; for the lowest two or three, write the specific doubt and sit with
it. No shame; just data. The list stays with you.

**2. The end-of-week-one check-in.** Tell your named partner how it's
going. You are not grading each other; you are expecting each other.

**3. Week two — two journal questions.** Is there something I heard and did
not obey? What is at risk for me to hear? Not sure something was God? Test
it with the How Did You Know? card, in company.

**4. The rest of the break — your choice.** Each week, choose one practice
from [the practices so far](session-09-10-the-garden-of-your-heart.md#the-practices-so-far-choose-from-these),
whichever you would really like to do. *Any Doubts?* is on the list now:
with a partner, Name, Exaggerate, Sit, Re-read. Write down which, and why.
If it slips, begin again the next day. No starting over, and no counting
streaks.

**5. Two sentences of preparation.** Before *Spiritual Styles* (Evening 10), in your journal:
"One thing I heard that I will act on is ____." And: "The practice I most
wanted to keep was ____, because ____."

*A missed day is never a failure — it is an invitation to return.*

## The stillness before the break

*Added 17 November 2026 (John's word).* Before the blessings, the
container holder slows the room for about a minute. The room is about to
scatter for Christmas, and minds are already turning toward home; for
these few minutes there is nowhere else we need to be.

1. **Two slow breaths,** together.
2. **Philippians 1:6, read aloud:** "And I am sure of this, that he who
   began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of
   Jesus Christ."
3. **One question, asked once, slowly, and not answered aloud:** *What has God begun in me that I can trust him to continue?*
   Carry it into the break.

Then the blessings and the Aaronic blessing, as always. In January,
Evening 9's thanks round gives the question its answer: one thing you
thank God for from the break, perhaps what he continued while we were
apart.

*Doubt is held here the way tonight teaches it: honestly, with
curiosity, and without hurrying to resolve it. The work God has begun
is not finished, and he will finish it.*

## For the leads

The full script is in the school form's Companion lesson plan,
[Week 11](../getting-started/week-11-doubts.md). The evening's run sheet,
deck and leader notes carry the silent place and the return round. The
school form closes a quarter tonight; the evening form has no quarters, and
its breaks are the holidays. No Pulse goes home: the return rounds and the
quiet tally of chosen practices are the evening form's read. What follows
is what the evening form changes.

- **Open with the return, not the content.** Rebuild the container slowly.
  The return round replaces the usual check-in and takes four more minutes,
  given back by the pairs.
- **Cut off the shame spiral early, from the front.** Someone, usually a
  parent, will arrive rehearsing a confession for a dropped practice. Say
  it before anyone starts: slips are data, and nobody owes an explanation.
- **Ask "did you come back?" plainly.** It is the one we most want to
  hear. Receive "No, not yet" as warmly as "Yes."
- **The choices are the read.** Which practices people chose for weeks
  three and four, and which they most wanted to keep, tell us which
  practices carry the "I really want to do this" pull. At the debrief,
  tally them quietly: a count for each practice, chosen and kept, with no
  names. The same tally runs again at *Spiritual Styles* (Evening 10)
  for the Christmas choices.
- **Frame doubt three times before the walk-throughs.** It is the gap between what
  we say and what we trust, not a faith crisis. Push gently for the small,
  personal doubt.
- **What scripture means by doubt.** Mostly a divided heart, not a
  question. The one who doubts is "a double-minded man," literally
  two-souled (James 1:6–8); Peter on the water stood two ways (Matt.
  14:31); Elijah's people limped between two opinions (1 Kings 18:21).
  Honest questions are welcome; the practice brings the two halves of the
  heart together, as the father did, both halves said aloud to Jesus.
  Scripture is gentle with the one caught there: "have mercy on those who
  doubt" (Jude 22); on the mountain "some doubted," and were sent out with
  the rest (Matt. 28:17). A sentence or two in the room, not a word study.
- **Tonight the parents go first, all the way** (the parents' meeting, 22
  November). John walks each parent through it, one at a time, arranged
  with each beforehand. Each brings a real doubt, not a raw one: honest,
  and fine for the teens to hear, with adult matters kept for adult
  conversation. Then the parents lead John through it, with his own real,
  small doubt. Pass-anytime holds, and "I don't notice any right now" is a
  complete answer.
- **One teen holds the container; John carries the content.** The teen
  leads the welcome, the return round, the merge and the close, and keeps
  the room. The teens watch the walk-throughs, help talk over the process,
  and lead the practice themselves in the new year, at Evening 12.
- **Guests, and their parents.** The family is in touch with the two guest
  teens from Evening 7. If they come, their parents are welcome too: this is
  family formation, not only teen formation.
- **The silent place is not a verdict.** If nothing comes, that is honest.
  Sharing is optional; say so twice. If the parents' pass takes its time,
  do not squeeze it into the buffer; it moves to another evening.
- **Name the Christmas break plainly** in the send-off: how long it is, and
  that *Spiritual Styles* (Evening 10) in January asks which practices were
  chosen and how it went. Walk the list once, aloud; the choices are made
  at home, freely.
- **Holidays are hard in some houses.** Watch the return round and the
  one-word landings closely. Over the break, the named adult makes one
  warm, light contact with each family, with no accounting asked.
